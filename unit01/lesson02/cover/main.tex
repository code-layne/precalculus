\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can describe how two quantities vary in tandem --- as the input
        increases, what the output does --- from a table, a graph, or a
        sentence.
  \item I can name the intervals where a function is increasing or decreasing,
        using inequalities on the \textit{inputs}.
  \item I can decide whether a function is concave up or concave down by
        checking whether its rate of change is growing or shrinking.
  \item I can tell the ``which way'' question from the ``concavity'' question
        and know that all four combinations of answers exist.
  \item I can identify a zero of a function and say what it means in context.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
Last lesson you learned to ask a function for one output at a time. Today you
watch the whole story: as the input climbs, does the output rise or fall ---
and is it rising \textit{faster and faster} or \textit{slower and slower}?
Those are two separate questions, and you need both words to describe a curve.
\end{remindbox}
