\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Describe how two quantities change \ans{in tandem}, and read that
        story off a graph.
  \item Name the intervals where a function is \ans{increasing} or
        \ans{decreasing}.
  \item Decide whether a function is concave \ans{up} or concave down by
        watching what its \ans{rate of change} does.
  \item Identify a \ans{zero} of a function and say what it means in context.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Vary in tandem:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Two quantities change together --- the input's change drives the output's change.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Increasing:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{On an interval, larger inputs give larger outputs: if $a < b$, then $f(a) < f(b)$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Decreasing:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{On an interval, larger inputs give smaller outputs: if $a < b$, then $f(a) > f(b)$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Concave up:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The rate of change is increasing --- each step gains more (or loses less).}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Concave down:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The rate of change is decreasing --- each step gains less (or loses more).}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Zero:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{An input whose output is 0; the graph meets the horizontal axis there.}
\end{vocabbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{1. Two Quantities in Tandem}
Every function ties two quantities together. Change the input and the output
answers --- the two \textbf{vary in tandem}.

\medskip
\textbf{A tank fills.} A hose runs into an empty tank. As the time $t$
(minutes) increases, the volume $V(t)$ (gallons) responds.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$V(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 8 & 16 & 24 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item The input quantity is \ans{time, in minutes}; the output quantity is
        \ans{volume, in gallons}.
  \item Read the table in tandem: every 2 minutes that pass, the tank gains
        \ans{8} gallons --- the same amount each time.
  \item Sentence frame: as $t$ increases, $V(t)$ \ans{increases, and it does
        so at a steady pace}.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
A table shows a handful of input--output pairs. A \textbf{graph} shows
\ans{all of them at once} --- which is why the behaviors in this lesson
(rising, falling, bending) are things you can \textit{see}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[27]
\begin{notesbox}{2. Question 1 --- Which Way Are the Outputs Going?}
This is the first of the two questions we ask about every stretch of a
function.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $f$ is \textbf{increasing} on an interval when larger inputs give
        larger outputs: whenever $a < b$, then $f(a)$ \ans{$<$} $f(b)$.
        The graph rises left to right.
  \item $f$ is \textbf{decreasing} on an interval when larger inputs give
        smaller outputs: whenever $a < b$, then $f(a)$ \ans{$>$} $f(b)$.
        The graph falls left to right.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.62cm, y=0.42cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,-3) grid (8,3);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-3.5) -- (0,3.7) node[above] {\small $h(x)$};
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.5,0) -- (8.6,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8} \node[below right, font=\tiny, fill=white, inner sep=0.6pt] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-3,-1,1,3} \node[left, font=\small] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,-2) -- (2,2);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (2,2) -- (3,3);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (3,3) -- (5,0);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (5,0) -- (6,-3);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (6,-3) -- (8,-1);
  \fill[plum] (0,-2) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (8,-1) circle (2.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $h$ is increasing on \ans{$0 < x < 3$} and on \ans{$6 < x < 8$}.
  \item $h$ is decreasing on \ans{$3 < x < 6$}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Two traps to avoid.}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Intervals are named with \ans{inputs} --- $x$-values, never outputs.
  \item Decreasing does \textbf{not} mean negative. Cocoa cooling from
        $90^\circ$ to $40^\circ$ is decreasing the whole way, and every
        output stays \ans{positive}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[18]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Question 2 --- How Is the Change Changing?}
``Increasing'' does not finish the story. Both tanks below fill for 6 minutes
and both end at 24 gallons --- but they do not fill the same way. Fill in the
\textbf{gains} and compare.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\multicolumn{5}{|c|}{\textbf{Tank A}} \\
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$A(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 14 & 20 & 24 \\
\hline
gain over the last 2 min & --- & \ans{14} & \ans{6} & \ans{4} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\hspace{0.15cm}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\multicolumn{5}{|c|}{\textbf{Tank B}} \\
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$B(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 4 & 10 & 24 \\
\hline
gain over the last 2 min & --- & \ans{4} & \ans{6} & \ans{14} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Tank A's gains are \ans{shrinking}, so its rate of change is
        \ans{decreasing}. We say $A$ is \textbf{concave down}.
  \item Tank B's gains are \ans{growing}, so its rate of change is
        \ans{increasing}. We say $B$ is \textbf{concave up}.
\end{itemize}

Both tanks are increasing the entire six minutes. Concavity is a
\textit{second} question, and it is about the \ans{gains}, not the outputs.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[19]
\begin{notesbox}{4. The Four Corners}
Two questions, two answers apiece --- so there are \ans{four} combinations,
and every one of them happens in real life.

\smallskip
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.20\linewidth}|p{0.15\linewidth}|c|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.26\linewidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{Shape} & \textbf{The situation} & \textbf{Which way?} &
\textbf{Concave} & \textbf{The rate of change is\ldots} \\
\hline
\minicurve{(0,0.2) (1,0.4) (2,0.9) (3,1.7) (4,2.9)} &
A rumor spreads through a school &
\ans{Increasing} & \ans{up} & \ans{increasing --- more people each hour} \\
\hline
\minicurve{(0,0.2) (1,1.4) (2,2.1) (3,2.6) (4,2.8)} &
A cold soda warms toward room temperature &
\ans{Increasing} & \ans{down} & \ans{decreasing --- fewer degrees each minute} \\
\hline
\minicurve{(0,2.8) (1,1.6) (2,0.9) (3,0.5) (4,0.3)} &
Hot cocoa cools toward room temperature &
\ans{Decreasing} & \ans{up} & \ans{increasing --- fewer degrees lost each minute} \\
\hline
\minicurve{(0,2.9) (1,2.7) (2,2.2) (3,1.4) (4,0.2)} &
A battery drains as more apps are opened &
\ans{Decreasing} & \ans{down} & \ans{decreasing --- more percent lost each minute} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\normalsize
\smallskip

The two middle columns are \ans{independent}: knowing a function is increasing
tells you nothing about its concavity, and ``concave down'' does \textbf{not}
mean the function is falling.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[13]
\begin{notesbox}{5. Zeros --- Where the Output Is 0}
A \textbf{zero} of a function is an input whose output is \ans{0}. On a graph
the curve meets the \ans{horizontal} axis there. In Lesson 1.1's language, a
zero is an answer to ``solve $h(x) = 0$'' --- so it is a \ans{solve} question,
not an evaluate question.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item From the graph in Part 2, the zeros of $h$ are $x = $ \ans{1} and
        $x = $ \ans{5}.
  \item Spiral from 1.1 --- that same graph has domain \ans{0} $\le x \le$
        \ans{8} and range \ans{$-3$} $\le y \le$ \ans{3}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A zero is an event.} Let $D(t)$ be a drone's height in meters $t$
seconds after takeoff. Given $D(48) = 0$, write what happened in a sentence.

\vspace{0.05in}
\ansline{Forty-eight seconds after takeoff the drone is back on the ground --- it has landed.}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item A snowbank melts. The table gives its depth $S$ in inches after $d$
        days.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        $d$ (days) & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
        \hline
        $S(d)$ (in.) & 24 & 18 & 14 & 12 \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \smallskip

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The daily changes are \ans{$-6$}, \ans{$-4$}, and
                \ans{$-2$}.
          \item $S$ is \ans{decreasing} and concave \ans{up}.
          \item Justify the concavity using the changes, not the shape.

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{Each day the snowbank loses less than the day before, so the rate of change is rising: $-6$, then $-4$, then $-2$.}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item Look back at the graph of $h$ in Part 2.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $h$ is decreasing on \ans{$3 < x < 6$}.
          \item The zeros of $h$ are $x = $ \ans{1} and $x = $ \ans{5}.
          \item $h(2) = $ \ans{2} \quad (an evaluate question --- from 1.1)
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
