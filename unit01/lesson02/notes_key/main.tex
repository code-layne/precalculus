\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Describe how the input and output values of a function vary together, and identify
        intervals where the function is \ans{increasing} or \ans{decreasing}. \hfill\textit{(LO 1.1.A)}
  \item Construct a graph representing two quantities varying together in context, and identify
        where the graph is concave \ans{up} or concave \ans{down}. \hfill\textit{(LO 1.1.B)}
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\begin{vocabbox}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Function:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A rule that assigns each input exactly one output.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Domain (independent variable):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The set of all allowable inputs; the independent variable; plotted on the horizontal axis.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Range (dependent variable):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The set of all possible outputs; the dependent variable; plotted on the vertical axis.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Increasing:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{$f$ is increasing on an interval if $a < b$ implies $f(a) < f(b)$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Decreasing:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{$f$ is decreasing on an interval if $a < b$ implies $f(a) > f(b)$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Concave up:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The rate of change is increasing on that interval; the graph curves upward like a bowl.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{Concave down:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The rate of change is decreasing on that interval; the graph curves downward like a hill.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{hookbox}
A car pulls away from a stop sign. Consider two quantities: the car's \textit{speed} and
the car's \textit{distance from the sign}, both as functions of time.

Without drawing, describe in words what each relationship looks like:
\ansline{Speed starts at 0 and increases (concave up at first as the car accelerates). Distance also starts at 0 and increases; the graph is concave up because the car covers more ground each second.}
\end{hookbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Functions \& Covariation}
A \textbf{function} is a rule that assigns each \ans{input} exactly \ans{one} output.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 1 (Numerical).} A tank is being filled with water.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
Time $t$ (min)   & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
Volume $V$ (gal) & 0 & 8 & 16 & 24 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{independent variable} (input) is \ans{time $t$}.
  \item The \textbf{dependent variable} (output) is \ans{volume $V$}.
  \item Domain: \ans{$[0, 6]$} \quad Range: \ans{$[0, 24]$}
  \item As $t$ increases, $V$ \ans{increases}. The function is \ans{increasing} on $[0, 6]$.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Example 2 (Verbal).} A hiker's elevation above sea level starts at 500 ft, rises steeply to
2{,}400 ft over the first 2 hours, continues rising more gradually to 2{,}800 ft by hour 4, then
descends back to 500 ft by hour 7.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Elevation is \textbf{increasing} on the interval \ans{$[0, 4]$}.
  \item Elevation is \textbf{decreasing} on the interval \ans{$[4, 7]$}.
  \item The domain of the hiker's elevation function (based on this description) is \ans{$[0, 7]$}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Increasing, Decreasing, \& Concavity}
\textbf{Formal Definitions:}

A function $f$ is \textbf{increasing} on an interval if, for any $a < b$ in that interval,
$f(a)$ \ans{$<$} $f(b)$.

A function $f$ is \textbf{decreasing} on an interval if, for any $a < b$ in that interval,
$f(a)$ \ans{$>$} $f(b)$.

\medskip
\textbf{Concavity} describes whether the rate of change is itself increasing or decreasing:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Concave up:} the rate of change is \ans{increasing}
        (graph curves like a bowl that opens \ans{upward}).
  \item \textbf{Concave down:} the rate of change is \ans{decreasing}
        (graph curves like a hill that opens \ans{downward}).
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Hiker example continued.} The table below shows elevation at each hour.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
Hour $t$          & 0   & 1     & 2     & 3     & 4     & 5     & 6     & 7 \\
\hline
Elevation $E$ (ft) & 500 & 1{,}700 & 2{,}400 & 2{,}700 & 2{,}800 & 2{,}400 & 1{,}400 & 500 \\
\hline
Change per hour   & --- & \ans{+1200} & \ans{+700} & \ans{+300} & \ans{+100} & \ans{$-$400} & \ans{$-$1000} & \ans{$-$900} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

On $[0, 4]$, the changes per hour are \ans{decreasing (1200, 700, 300, 100)}, so the graph is concave \ans{down}.\\
On $[4, 7]$, the changes per hour are \ans{more negative each hour}, so the graph is concave \ans{down} on $[4,6]$.

\begin{teachernote}
  On $[4, 7]$: changes are $-400, -1000, -900$. The magnitude increases then slightly decreases at the end ---
  accept concave down on $[4, 6]$ (becoming steeper) and note the irregularity from $6$ to $7$.
  The main takeaway: the descent accelerates, which is concave down.
\end{teachernote}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item For the hiker's elevation function above, state all intervals where $E$ is increasing,
        all intervals where $E$ is decreasing, and the domain and range.

        Increasing: \ans{$[0, 4]$} \quad Decreasing: \ans{$[4, 7]$}

        Domain: \ans{$[0, 7]$} \quad Range: \ans{$[500, 2800]$}

  \item The graph of a function $g$ is concave up on $[1, 5]$. A classmate says "that means $g$ is
        increasing on $[1, 5]$." Is the classmate correct? Explain.

        \vspace{0.1in}
        \ansline{No. Concave up means the rate of change is increasing, not that the function itself is increasing. For example, a function could be decreasing but slowing down (becoming less negative) --- that would be concave up but still decreasing.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
