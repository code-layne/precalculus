\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{1. Two Quantities in Tandem}
Every function ties two quantities together. Change the input and the output
answers --- the two \textbf{vary in tandem}.

\medskip
\textbf{A tank fills.} A hose runs into an empty tank. As the time $t$
(minutes) increases, the volume $V(t)$ (gallons) responds.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$V(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 8 & 16 & 24 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item The input quantity is \blank{4.2cm}; the output quantity is
        \blank{4.2cm}.
  \item Read the table in tandem: every 2 minutes that pass, the tank gains
        \blank{1.4cm} gallons --- the same amount each time.
  \item Sentence frame: as $t$ increases, $V(t)$ \blank{7.4cm}.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
A table shows a handful of input--output pairs. A \textbf{graph} shows
\blank{4.6cm} --- which is why the behaviors in this lesson
(rising, falling, bending) are things you can \textit{see}.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[27]
\begin{notesbox}{2. Question 1 --- Which Way Are the Outputs Going?}
This is the first of the two questions we ask about every stretch of a
function.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $f$ is \textbf{increasing} on an interval when larger inputs give
        larger outputs: whenever $a < b$, then $f(a)$ \blank{1cm} $f(b)$.
        The graph rises left to right.
  \item $f$ is \textbf{decreasing} on an interval when larger inputs give
        smaller outputs: whenever $a < b$, then $f(a)$ \blank{1cm} $f(b)$.
        The graph falls left to right.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.62cm, y=0.42cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,-3) grid (8,3);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-3.5) -- (0,3.7) node[above] {\small $h(x)$};
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.5,0) -- (8.6,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8} \node[below right, font=\tiny, fill=white, inner sep=0.6pt] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-3,-1,1,3} \node[left, font=\small] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,-2) -- (2,2);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (2,2) -- (3,3);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (3,3) -- (5,0);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (5,0) -- (6,-3);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (6,-3) -- (8,-1);
  \fill[plum] (0,-2) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (8,-1) circle (2.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $h$ is increasing on \blank{2.6cm} and on \blank{2.6cm}.
  \item $h$ is decreasing on \blank{2.6cm}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Two traps to avoid.}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Intervals are named with \blank{2.2cm} --- $x$-values, never outputs.
  \item Decreasing does \textbf{not} mean negative. Cocoa cooling from
        $90^\circ$ to $40^\circ$ is decreasing the whole way, and every
        output stays \blank{2.2cm}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[18]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Question 2 --- How Is the Change Changing?}
``Increasing'' does not finish the story. Both tanks below fill for 6 minutes
and both end at 24 gallons --- but they do not fill the same way. Fill in the
\textbf{gains} and compare.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\multicolumn{5}{|c|}{\textbf{Tank A}} \\
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$A(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 14 & 20 & 24 \\
\hline
gain over the last 2 min & --- & \blank{0.65cm} & \blank{0.65cm} & \blank{0.65cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\hspace{0.15cm}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\multicolumn{5}{|c|}{\textbf{Tank B}} \\
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$B(t)$ (gal) & 0 & 4 & 10 & 24 \\
\hline
gain over the last 2 min & --- & \blank{0.65cm} & \blank{0.65cm} & \blank{0.65cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Tank A's gains are \blank{2.2cm}, so its rate of change is
        \blank{2.6cm}. We say $A$ is \textbf{concave down}.
  \item Tank B's gains are \blank{2.2cm}, so its rate of change is
        \blank{2.6cm}. We say $B$ is \textbf{concave up}.
\end{itemize}

Both tanks are increasing the entire six minutes. Concavity is a
\textit{second} question, and it is about the \blank{1.8cm}, not the outputs.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{5. Zeros --- Where the Output Is 0}
A \textbf{zero} of a function is an input whose output is \blank{1cm}. On a graph
the curve meets the \blank{2.6cm} axis there. In Lesson 1.1's language, a
zero is an answer to ``solve $h(x) = 0$'' --- so it is a \blank{1.8cm} question,
not an evaluate question.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item From the graph in Part 2, the zeros of $h$ are $x = $ \blank{1.2cm} and
        $x = $ \blank{1.2cm}.
  \item Spiral from 1.1 --- that same graph has domain \blank{1.2cm} $\le x \le$
        \blank{1.2cm} and range \blank{1.2cm} $\le y \le$ \blank{1.2cm}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A zero is an event.} Let $D(t)$ be a drone's height in meters $t$
seconds after takeoff. Given $D(48) = 0$, write what happened in a sentence.

\end{notesbox}
