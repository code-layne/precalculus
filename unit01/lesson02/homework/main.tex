\item \textbf{AP-Style Multiple Choice.} A function $g$ is defined so that as $x$ increases on
$[2, 6]$, the output $g(x)$ increases, but the amount of increase gets smaller with each unit.
Which of the following best describes the graph of $g$ on $[2, 6]$?

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*), itemsep=3pt]
  \item Increasing and concave up
  \item Increasing and concave down
  \item Decreasing and concave up
  \item Decreasing and concave down
\end{enumerate}

\begin{extensionbox}
\textbf{Extension (optional).} A hiker takes a break partway through a trail. Her elevation
$E$ (in feet) behaves as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item From $t = 0$ to $t = 2$: elevation increases from 300 ft to 1{,}200 ft.
        The gain per hour is getting smaller (starts fast, slows down).
  \item From $t = 2$ to $t = 3$: elevation stays constant at 1{,}200 ft (she rests).
  \item From $t = 3$ to $t = 5$: elevation increases from 1{,}200 ft to 2{,}000 ft.
        The gain per hour is getting larger (she's energized again).
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=5pt]
  \item Identify all intervals where $E$ is increasing, decreasing, or constant.
  \item On which piece(s) of the domain is the graph of $E$ concave up? Concave down? Explain.
  \item At $t = 2$ and $t = 3$, what happens to the rate of change? What does that mean for concavity?
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}
