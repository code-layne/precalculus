\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=10pt]

\item Each description tells you both things at once. Name which way the
      function goes and its concavity.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=6pt]
  \item A pumpkin gains weight through August, and it gains more each week
        than it did the week before.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ans{Increasing} and concave \ans{up}
  \item After a parachute opens, the skydiver's speed drops sharply at first
        and then levels off toward a steady fall rate.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ans{Decreasing} and concave \ans{up}
\end{enumerate}

\item The graph of a function $g$ is shown.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.62cm, y=0.42cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,-2) grid (8,4);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-2.5) -- (0,4.7) node[above] {\small $g(x)$};
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.5,0) -- (8.6,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8} \node[below right, font=\tiny, fill=white, inner sep=0.6pt] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-2,2,4} \node[left, font=\small] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,4) -- (3,-2);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (3,-2) -- (6,4);
  \draw[very thick, plum] (6,4) -- (8,2);
  \fill[plum] (0,4) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (8,2) circle (2.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
  \item $g$ is decreasing on \ans{$0 < x < 3$} and on \ans{$6 < x < 8$}.
  \item $g$ is increasing on \ans{$3 < x < 6$}.
  \item The zeros of $g$ are $x = $ \ans{2} and $x = $ \ans{4}.
  \item Domain: \ans{0} $\le x \le$ \ans{8} \qquad
        Range: \ans{$-2$} $\le y \le$ \ans{4}
\end{enumerate}

\item A car pulls away from a stop sign. The table gives the distance $D$, in
      feet, it has travelled after $t$ seconds.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ (s) & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
\hline
$D(t)$ (ft) & 0 & 3 & 12 & 27 & 48 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
  \item The second-by-second changes are \ans{3}, \ans{9}, \ans{15},
        and \ans{21}.
  \item $D$ is \ans{increasing} and concave \ans{up}.
  \item Explain the concavity using the changes.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{Each second the car covers more ground than the second before, so its rate of change is increasing.}
\end{enumerate}

\item Can a function be \textbf{decreasing} and \textbf{concave up} at the same
      time? Answer yes or no, give an example, and explain with rate-of-change
      language.

\vspace{0.05in}
\ansline{Yes. Hot cocoa cooling toward room temperature keeps falling, but each minute it loses fewer degrees than the last.}
\ansline{The rate of change is negative and rising toward 0 --- rising is what makes it concave up.}

\item A function's outputs change by the amounts listed below, one amount per
      step. Which list belongs to a function that is \textbf{increasing and
      concave down}?

\begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., itemsep=3pt]
  \item $+2,\ +4,\ +6,\ +8$
  \item $+8,\ +6,\ +4,\ +2$ \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
  \item $-2,\ -4,\ -6,\ -8$
  \item $+5,\ +5,\ +5,\ +5$
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\boxguard
\begin{extensionbox}
A hiker climbs for two hours, sits down and rests for one hour, then climbs
again. During the rest hour, is the elevation function increasing, decreasing,
or neither? What is its rate of change during that hour?

\vspace{0.05in}
\ansline{Neither. The elevation holds steady, so larger inputs give neither larger nor smaller outputs --- the graph is flat.}
\ansline{Its rate of change during that hour is 0.}
\end{extensionbox}
