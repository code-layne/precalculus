\item The table below shows values of a function $h$.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$    & 1 & 2  & 3  & 4  & 5  \\
\hline
$h(x)$ & 3 & 7  & 11 & 15 & 19 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
  \item Find $h(3)$. \quad \ans{11}
  \item Is $h$ increasing or decreasing? Circle one: \quad \ans{Increasing} \quad / \quad Decreasing
\end{enumerate}

\item A ball is thrown upward. Its height above the ground increases from $t = 0$ to $t = 2$ seconds,
then decreases from $t = 2$ to $t = 4$ seconds.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
  \item On what interval is the height function \textit{increasing}? \quad \ans{$[0, 2]$}
  \item On what interval is it \textit{decreasing}? \quad \ans{$[2, 4]$}
\end{enumerate}

\item The input values of a function are all real numbers from $-3$ to $5$, including both endpoints.
Write the domain in interval notation.

\vspace{0.12in}
Domain: \ans{$[-3, 5]$}

\item A function $f$ has $f(0) = 4$, $f(2) = 4$, and $f(4) = 4$.
Could the graph of $f$ cross the $x$-axis between $x = 0$ and $x = 4$?
Explain briefly.

\vspace{0.1in}
\ansline{Not necessarily. All given outputs are 4 (positive), but the function could dip below zero between these inputs. Without additional information, we cannot rule it out.}

\end{enumerate}
