    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will read and write function notation
    --- $f(x)$ --- evaluate a function given by a formula, a table, or a graph,

    state the domain and range of a function presented as a list of points or a
    graph. \hfill\textit{\small (Foundations ramp --- no CED topic)}

\textbf{This is the tightest period in the unit.} Four ideas --- notation,
evaluating, the two directions, and domain/range --- share one block, so the guided
phase runs 22 minutes. The whole release lives in the guided notes; there is no
activity sheet to raid when it overruns. If the clock slips, cut \textbf{problem 18}
(the formula domain) first, then problem 17, and protect the \textbf{We Do}
direction table in the TWO DIRECTIONS row: it is the one thing the exit ticket cannot
be passed without.

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
Read and write function notation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $f(x)$ is read ``$f$ of $x$'' --- never ``$f$ times $x$.''
  \item One statement $f(3) = 7$ carries three facts: input 3, output 7,
        and the point $(3, 7)$ on the graph.
  \item An input need not be a number --- only something the rule turns into
        exactly one output.
\end{itemize} \\
Evaluate a function from a formula, a table, or a graph. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item The slot takes anything: $f(\square) = 5(\square) - 8$. Write the
        parentheses first, then fill them.
  \item Negative inputs are the error magnet: $(-4)^2 \ne -4^2$.
\end{itemize} \\
Tell an \textit{evaluate} question from a \textit{solve} question. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item ``Find $f(2)$'' gives an input, asks for the output --- exactly one
        answer.
  \item ``Solve $f(x) = 2$'' gives an output, asks for the input(s) --- zero,
        one, or many answers, and many is still a function.
\end{itemize} \\
State the domain and range of a function. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Domain = every input the function uses; range = every output it
        produces (a repeat listed once).
  \item From a graph, both are read as inequalities --- $0 \le x \le 8$ --- or
        with brackets, $[\,0, 8\,]$.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.24\linewidth} X @{}}
Function notation & The symbol $f(x)$: $f$ names the rule, $x$ is the input, $f(x)$ is the output at $x$. Read ``$f$ of $x$.'' \\
Evaluate          & Find the output a function produces for a given input. \\
Solve             & Find the input(s) that produce a given output --- the opposite direction. \\
Domain            & The set of all inputs a function uses. \\
Range             & The set of all outputs a function produces; a repeated output is listed once. \\
Undefined         & What we call an expression with no value, such as a division by zero --- inputs causing it are excluded from the domain. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review (5 min)]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: evaluating a linear expression at a negative value,
    order of operations with a squared negative,
    solving a two-step linear equation,
    and the function decision from Lesson 1.0 (many-to-one vs.\ one-to-many).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook (2 min)]{lilac}
    \textbf{The machine gets a name.} Yesterday's trustworthy vending machine
    comes back, this time labeled. Let $P(b)$ be the price in dollars of the
    item at button $b$.
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item On the board, write only: $P(\text{B4}) = 1.25$. Ask what the
              equation is \textit{saying}. Push for a full sentence --- ``the
              item at button B4 costs \$1.25.''
        \item Follow up: what is the input here? It is \textbf{B4} --- a button,
              not a number. Inputs do not have to be numbers; they only have to
              produce exactly one output.
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} one short symbol just packed in the machine, the
    input, and the output. That efficiency is the whole point of function
    notation --- and it is why every lesson from here on is written in it.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- I Do / We Do / You Do]{lilac}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{I DO --- Part 1: Notation and the Empty Slot} (10 min)

\textit{Notes rows NOTATION and EVALUATING: read each definition, mark up the
display, work problems 1 and 3. Teacher works; students watch and annotate.
Nothing is cold-called in this phase.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the NOTATION sentences aloud: $f$ is the machine's name, $x$ is the
        input slot, $f(x)$ is the output. Insist on ``$f$ of $x$,'' and confront
        the misreading head-on --- those parentheses hold the input; they do not
        multiply.
  \item Mark up the machine display: $F(m) = 2.50 + 1.75m$ for a rideshare fare.
        Fill the two label boxes yourself --- $m$ is miles driven, $F(m)$ the fare
        in dollars.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 1} aloud, the three-facts reading: $f(3) = 7$ says
        input 3, output 7, point $(3,7)$.
  \item Read the EVALUATING sentences and the slot display, then work
        \textbf{problem 3} by doing the habit, not asking for it:
        $f(x) = 5x - 8$ becomes $f(\square) = 5(\square) - 8$. Write the
        parentheses first, \textit{then} fill them, narrating both moves.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 2: Evaluating, Negatives Included} (6 min)

\textit{Problems 2 and 4--6. Cold-call every one.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 2} first --- back to the NOTATION grid: say what $F(4)$
        is asking for before anyone computes 9.50.
  \item \textbf{Problems 4 and 5}: $f(-2)$, then $g(x) = x^2 + 3x$ at $g(-4)$ ---
        two negatives to track in one substitution.
  \item \textbf{Problem 6} is the classic trap $-4^2$ vs.\ $(-4)^2$: make the
        error visible on the board once, on purpose, and let the class catch it.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 3: Domain and Range} (7 min)

\textit{Notes row DOMAIN \& RANGE. Teacher reads the definition, marks up the
graph, works problem 7; the class works 8--10, every one cold-called. This is new
vocabulary, not practice.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Definitions first: domain = all inputs, range = all outputs. Then the
        display: label the gold bracket \textit{domain} and the red bracket
        \textit{range} on the board.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 7} yourself --- from a list of points both sets are
        just lists, with the repeated 6 written once.
  \item \textbf{Problem 8} reads the graph: $0 \le x \le 8$ and $1 \le y \le 7$,
        then $[\,0, 8\,]$ --- say plainly that both forms are acceptable all year.
  \item \textbf{Problem 9} is the formula, first look: $k(x) = 8/(x+3)$ has no
        output at $x = -3$. One example only; the general treatment belongs to
        Unit 3. \textbf{Problem 10} lands ``a repeat is listed once'' and keeps
        many-to-one alive.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 4: Two Directions} (9 min)

\textit{Notes row TWO DIRECTIONS --- the spine of the lesson. Teacher fills the
direction table with the class, marks up the two paths on the graph, works
problem 11; the class works 12--14, every one cold-called.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fill the direction table \textit{before} any reading: given the input
        vs.\ given the output; asked for the output vs.\ asked for the input(s);
        one answer vs.\ possibly many.
  \item Mark up the graph: the gold path is $f(2) = 1$, \textit{evaluating}; the
        red path is $f(x) = 3$, $x = 1$ and $3$, \textit{solving}. Read the boxed
        headline aloud, then give one minute for the ``in your own words'' line.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 11}, $f(6)$, by walking \textit{up}. Then
        \textbf{12 and 13 are the crux}: $f(3) = 3$, but solving $f(x) = 3$ gives
        $x = 1$ and $3$ --- same graph, same number 3, different question,
        different answer. \textbf{Problem 14}, $f(x) = 5$, has \textbf{three}
        answers --- many-to-one from 1.0, alive again.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{YOU DO --- Part 5: The You Do Row} (11 min)

\textit{Notes row TABLE \& FORMULA, problems 15--18 --- the whole independent
phase. Students work alone; circulate and say nothing a neighbor could say
instead.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 15} evaluates $f(x) = 3x - 7$ at a positive and a negative
        input; \textbf{16} reads the $s$ table in \textit{both} directions and
        names which was the evaluate question --- the crux, transferred.
  \item \textbf{Problem 17} states $p$'s domain and range, then flips the machine:
        two inputs share the output 6, so running $p$ backward is not a function.
        Many-to-one from 1.0, and the first quiet hint of 1.6.
  \item \textbf{Problem 18} is the finisher's question --- a formula domain,
        $m(x) = 20/(x - 6)$, where the excluded input has to be found rather
        than read off.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the \textbf{You Do} phase --- the eleven minutes where students
are working problems 15--18 without you --- and check. The crux is
\textbf{problem 13} (solve $f(x) = 3$ against problem 12's $f(3)$); its You Do
transfer is \textbf{problem 16}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} reading $f(x)$ as multiplication --- watch for
        students ``solving for $f$'' or dividing both sides by $f$. Cold-call:
        ``In $F(m)$, what is the machine's name? What goes in the slot?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} dropping parentheses on negative inputs, so
        $g(-4) = -4^2 + 3(-4)$. Ask the student to say the substitution aloud
        using the word ``quantity.''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} answering ``solve $s(x) = 9$'' in problem 16
        with an output, or with only one of the two inputs. Push: ``Is the 9
        going \textit{into} the machine or coming \textit{out}?'' Point at the
        direction table they filled in.
  \item \textbf{Domain/range:} students listing a repeated output twice, or
        swapping the two sets. Cold-call: ``Domain --- inputs or outputs?''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Differentiation --- During You Do (11 min)]{redbox}
There is no separate activity sheet: students work the notes' You Do row
(problems 15--18) alone while you circulate. Differentiate by \textit{where you
stand}, not by handing out different paper.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Support.} Sit with a struggling student on \textbf{problem 15}
        and make them write $f(\square) = 3(\square) - 7$ before substituting.
        The empty-slot habit from Part 1 is the whole fix for $f(-1)$; if they
        write the substitution straight down with no parentheses, the sign error
        is already in.
  \item \textbf{On level.} \textbf{Problem 16} is the direction check ---
        $s(4)$ is a lookup, ``solve $s(x) = 9$'' is the reverse, with two
        answers. Do not answer it; point at the direction table they filled in
        during Part 4 and walk away.
  \item \textbf{Extend.} \textbf{Problems 17 and 18} are the finisher's
        questions. 17 is many-to-one from 1.0 seen backward --- a student who
        says ``two inputs share 6, so running it backward gives one input two
        outputs'' has previewed 1.6 without being told. 18 asks for an excluded
        input that must be \textit{found}; a student who solves $x - 6 = 0$
        rather than pattern-matching has the idea. Either answer presents in the
        Debrief.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (5 min)]{redbox}
Whole class, teacher-led, packets still open. Three moves, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Share out the You Do} (3 min) --- one answer per problem,
        not a full review. Problem 15: $f(-1)$, said aloud with the parentheses.
        Problem 16: both directions, the lookup and the solve. Problem 17:
        whether running $p$ backward is a function. Problem 18: the excluded
        input and why. If a student settled 17 cleanly, they present it --- it
        previews inverse functions in 1.6, and it is the only exposure the rest
        of the class gets to it.
  \item \textbf{Name the headline} (1 min) --- say it, then make the class say it
        back: \textit{evaluate hands you the input and asks for the output ---
        exactly one answer. Solve hands you the output and asks for the
        input(s) --- possibly several, and several is still a function.} Point at
        the direction table while you say it.
  \item \textbf{Point forward} (1 min) --- 1.2 stops asking what the output
        \textit{is} at a given input and starts asking what it \textit{does} as
        the input climbs.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Do not} re-teach domain and range here. If the exit tickets say it did
not land, it is the 1.2 warm-up's job, not this minute's.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment (5 min)]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (final 5 minutes, individual, silent, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Evaluate $f(x) = 6x - 5$ at $x = 3$ (worked space provided) and at
        $x = -1$.
  \item From a small table, read $g(2)$ and then solve $g(x) = 8$.
  \item State the domain and range of a function given as four ordered pairs,
        with one repeated output.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} Item 2(b) is the direction check --- a student who
answers it with an output has missed the lesson's central distinction. Sort
tickets by 2(b) and reteach ``into the machine vs.\ out of the machine'' in the
1.2 warm-up window if more than a handful miss it. On item 3, listing the
repeated 8 twice is a minor slip; swapping domain and range is not.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework --- DeltaMath.} This lesson ships no printed homework; assign the
DeltaMath set and have students record its name, due date, and score in the
homework row of the packet cover. Target coverage: evaluating a function at
positive \textit{and} negative inputs, reading a table or a graph in both
directions (evaluate and solve), and stating domain and range from a list of
points and from a graph. If the DeltaMath set offers bracket notation as its own
item type, include it --- it gets only problem 8 of in-class practice.

\textbf{Extension (optional):} Why does $y = 6/(2x - 8)$ forbid an input?
Students name the excluded value and write the domain in words. This is practice
problem 18 with a coefficient on $x$ --- assign it to anyone who cleared 18
early, and to anyone who did not reach it at all.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson (1.2, Change in Tandem) asks what the output
\textit{does} as the input climbs --- increasing, decreasing, or holding
steady --- and how a graph shows it.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Items 1--3 are the exact algebra
today's evaluating runs on --- a student who misses item 1 or 2 will make the
same sign error inside $f(-2)$ within the hour, so note names. Item 4 is the
promised revisit of the 1.0 function decision and should be quick; the ZIP-code
rule is the one-to-many case.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The notes are one Main Ideas / Notes table, four instruction rows and a You Do
row; in each instruction row you read the printed definition, mark up the
display, and work the first problem of the grid (1, 3, 7, 11), and the class
works the rest with the pen in their hand, every problem cold-called. The
NOTATION row lives or dies on the language: make students \textit{say} ``$f$ of
$x$'' aloud several times, and translate $f(3) = 7$ (problem 1) into a sentence
before anyone computes. In the EVALUATING row the $\square$ display is the whole
point --- write the empty parentheses first, every time, and let problem 6 catch
the $-4^2$ trap in the open. The DOMAIN \& RANGE row comes \textit{before} the
directions on purpose, so that the graph is familiar by the time it is read both
ways; label its two brackets yourself. TWO DIRECTIONS is the spine: fill the
direction table \textit{before} touching the graph, and treat problems 12 and 13
--- $f(3) = 3$ but $f(x) = 3$ at $x = 1$ and $3$ --- as the crux, with the
three-answer problem 14 as the payoff, not a curiosity. If time runs short, read
the bracket sentence aloud without stopping for questions and flag it for the
DeltaMath set; do not cut the direction table.

The You Do row (15--18) is the entire independent phase and carries eleven
minutes, so treat it as the lesson's third act. Problem 16 is the crux
transferred to a table; problem 17 is the hinge: two inputs share the output 6,
so running $p$ backward is not a function --- many-to-one from 1.0, seen from
the other side, and the first quiet hint of 1.6. If the clock slips, cut
problem 18 (the formula domain) and let the Debrief carry it; it returns as the
DeltaMath extension either way.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Debrief]
Five minutes, and on this lesson they are genuinely at risk --- the guided phase
runs 22 minutes and will try to borrow them. Borrow from problem 18 instead;
it is the one piece of the period that returns as homework. The share-out is one
answer per You Do problem so that a student who stalled on 15 still hears the
two-direction result in 16, which is the exact shape of exit-ticket item 2(b)
and the only version of it they will have seen otherwise.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Collected, completion credit only. Scan item 2(b) first --- an answer of 11 (an
output) instead of 1 (the input) is the diagnostic miss, and it is the one worth
reteaching. Item 3: accept the range with or without braces, but a doubled 8
earns a margin note, not a deduction.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework --- DeltaMath]
Homework is DeltaMath unless a lesson ships a printed homework component; 1.1
does not. Put the assignment name and due date on the board while students fill
the cover row. Read the report by item type, not overall score: evaluating at a
negative input and \textit{solving} $f(x) = c$ fail for different reasons, and
only the second one means the lesson missed. Bracket notation is the item most
likely to have gone unpracticed in class --- if the DeltaMath set does not carry
it, spend the first two minutes of 1.2 on it rather than assuming it landed. The
extension is the only formula-domain problem with a coefficient on $x$; if Part
4 ran long, or problem 18 was cut, assign it explicitly rather than
optionally.
\end{teachernote}
