\smallskip
\textbf{This is the tightest period in the unit.} Four ideas --- notation,
evaluating, the two directions, and domain/range --- share one block, so the guided
phase runs 22 minutes. The whole release lives in the guided notes; there is no
activity sheet to raid when it overruns. If the clock slips, cut \textbf{practice

direction table in \S3: it is the one thing the exit ticket cannot be passed
without.

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
Read and write function notation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $f(x)$ is read ``$f$ of $x$'' --- never ``$f$ times $x$.''
  \item One statement $f(3) = 7$ carries three facts: input 3, output 7,
        and the point $(3, 7)$ on the graph.
  \item An input need not be a number --- only something the rule turns into
        exactly one output.
\end{itemize} \\
Evaluate a function from a formula, a table, or a graph. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item The slot takes anything: $f(\square) = 5(\square) - 8$. Write the
        parentheses first, then fill them.
  \item Negative inputs are the error magnet: $(-4)^2 \ne -4^2$.
\end{itemize} \\
Tell an \textit{evaluate} question from a \textit{solve} question. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item ``Find $f(2)$'' gives an input, asks for the output --- exactly one
        answer.
  \item ``Solve $f(x) = 2$'' gives an output, asks for the input(s) --- zero,
        one, or many answers, and many is still a function.
\end{itemize} \\
State the domain and range of a function. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Domain = every input the function uses; range = every output it
        produces (a repeat listed once).
  \item From a graph, both are read as inequalities --- $0 \le x \le 8$ --- or
        with brackets, $[\,0, 8\,]$.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.24\linewidth} X @{}}
Function notation & The symbol $f(x)$: $f$ names the rule, $x$ is the input, $f(x)$ is the output at $x$. Read ``$f$ of $x$.'' \\
Evaluate          & Find the output a function produces for a given input. \\
Solve             & Find the input(s) that produce a given output --- the opposite direction. \\
Domain            & The set of all inputs a function uses. \\
Range             & The set of all outputs a function produces; a repeated output is listed once. \\
Undefined         & What we call an expression with no value, such as a division by zero --- inputs causing it are excluded from the domain. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review (5 min)]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: evaluating a linear expression at a negative value,
    order of operations with a squared negative,
    solving a two-step linear equation,
    and the function decision from Lesson 1.0 (many-to-one vs.\ one-to-many).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook (2 min)]{lilac}
    \textbf{The machine gets a name.} Yesterday's trustworthy vending machine
    comes back, this time labeled. Let $P(b)$ be the price in dollars of the
    item at button $b$.
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item On the board, write only: $P(\text{B4}) = 1.25$. Ask what the
              equation is \textit{saying}. Push for a full sentence --- ``the
              item at button B4 costs \$1.25.''
        \item Follow up: what is the input here? It is \textbf{B4} --- a button,
              not a number. Inputs do not have to be numbers; they only have to
              produce exactly one output.
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} one short symbol just packed in the machine, the
    input, and the output. That efficiency is the whole point of function
    notation --- and it is why every lesson from here on is written in it.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- I Do / We Do / You Do]{lilac}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{I DO --- Part 1: Notation and the Empty Slot} (10 min)

\textit{Guided notes \S1 and the first example of \S2. Teacher works; students
watch and annotate. Nothing is cold-called in this phase.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Name the three parts of $f(x)$: $f$ is the machine's name, $x$ is the
        input slot, $f(x)$ is the output. Insist on ``$f$ of $x$,'' and confront
        the misreading head-on --- those parentheses hold the input; they do not
        multiply.
  \item Context example: $F(m) = 2.50 + 1.75m$ for a rideshare fare. Name, input,
        and output all mean something. Say what $F(4)$ is asking for before
        computing it.
  \item Model the three-facts reading aloud: $f(3) = 7$ says input 3, output 7,
        point $(3,7)$.
  \item Then establish the empty-slot habit by doing it, not asking for it:
        $f(x) = 5x - 8$ becomes $f(\square) = 5(\square) - 8$. Write the
        parentheses first, \textit{then} fill them, narrating both moves.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 2: Evaluating, Negatives Included} (6 min)

\textit{Rest of notes \S2. Cold-call every blank.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Find $f(-2)$ together, then $g(x) = x^2 + 3x$ at $g(-4)$ --- two
        negatives to track in one substitution.
  \item The classic trap $-4^2$ vs.\ $(-4)^2$: make the error visible on the
        board once, on purpose, and let the class catch it.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 3: Two Directions} (9 min)

\textit{Guided notes \S3 and \S4. The spine of the lesson.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fill the direction table together \textit{before} any reading: given the
        input vs.\ given the output; asked for the output vs.\ asked for the
        input(s); one answer vs.\ possibly many.
  \item Table read both ways: $s(4)$, then solve $s(x) = 9$ --- two answers,
        because 9 appears twice in the row.
  \item Then the pre-drawn graph. $f(2)$ and $f(6)$ by walking \textit{up};
        solve $f(x) = 3$ by walking \textit{across}. Finish with $f(x) = 5$,
        which has \textbf{three} answers --- many-to-one from 1.0, alive again.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 4: Domain and Range} (7 min)

\textit{Guided notes \S5. Still together --- this is new vocabulary, not
practice.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Definitions first: domain = all inputs, range = all outputs. From a list
        of points both are just lists, with the repeated 6 written once.
  \item Reuse the Part 3 graph deliberately --- do not redraw it, or students
        parse a second figure instead of thinking about domain. Read
        $0 \le x \le 8$ and $1 \le y \le 7$, then show $[\,0, 8\,]$ and say
        plainly that both forms are acceptable all year.
  \item Formulas, first look: $k(x) = 8/(x+3)$ has no output at $x = -3$. One
        example only; the general treatment belongs to Unit 3.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{YOU DO --- Part 5: Practice} (11 min)

\textit{The practice box --- the whole independent phase. Students work alone;
circulate and say nothing a neighbor could say instead.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Evaluating $f(x) = 3x - 7$ at a positive and a negative input, then a
        table read in \textit{both} directions with its domain and range --- the
        whole lesson in one problem.
  \item Item 2(c) flips the machine: $p$ has two inputs sharing the output 6, so
        running it backward is not a function. Many-to-one from 1.0, and the
        first quiet hint of 1.6.
  \item Item 3 is the finisher's question --- a formula domain,
        $m(x) = 20/(x - 6)$, where the excluded input has to be found rather
        than read off.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the \textbf{You Do} phase --- the eleven minutes where students
are working without you --- and check:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} reading $f(x)$ as multiplication --- watch for
        students ``solving for $f$'' or dividing both sides by $f$. Cold-call:
        ``In $F(m)$, what is the machine's name? What goes in the slot?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} dropping parentheses on negative inputs, so
        $g(-4) = -4^2 + 3(-4)$. Ask the student to say the substitution aloud
        using the word ``quantity.''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} answering ``solve $f(x) = 3$'' with an output.
        Push: ``Is the 3 going \textit{into} the machine or coming
        \textit{out}?'' Point at the direction table they filled in.
  \item \textbf{Domain/range:} students listing a repeated output twice, or
        swapping the two sets. Cold-call: ``Domain --- inputs or outputs?''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Differentiation --- During You Do (11 min)]{redbox}
There is no separate activity sheet: students work the notes' practice box alone
while you circulate. Differentiate by \textit{where you stand}, not by handing
out different paper.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Support.} Sit with a struggling student on \textbf{item 1} and
        make them write $f(\square) = 3(\square) - 7$ before substituting. The
        empty-slot habit from Part 1 is the whole fix for $f(-1)$; if they write
        the substitution straight down with no parentheses, the sign error is
        already in.
  \item \textbf{On level.} \textbf{Item 2(a)} is the direction check ---
        $p(2)$ is a lookup, ``solve $p(x) = 3$'' is the reverse. Do not answer
        it; point at the direction table they filled in during Part 3 and walk
        away.
  \item \textbf{Extend.} \textbf{Item 2(c)} and \textbf{item 3} are the
        finisher's questions. 2(c) is many-to-one from 1.0 seen backward --- a
        student who says ``two inputs share 6, so running it backward gives one
        input two outputs'' has previewed 1.6 without being told. Item 3 asks for
        an excluded input that must be \textit{found}; a student who solves
        $x - 6 = 0$ rather than pattern-matching has the idea. Either answer
        presents in the Debrief.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (5 min)]{redbox}
Whole class, teacher-led, packets still open. Three moves, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Share out the You Do} (3 min) --- one answer per practice item,
        not a full review. Item 1: $f(-1)$, said aloud with the parentheses.
        Item 2(a): both directions, the lookup and the solve. Item 2(c):
        whether running $p$ backward is a function. Item 3: the excluded input
        and why. If a student settled 2(c) cleanly, they present it --- it
        previews inverse functions in 1.6, and it is the only exposure the rest
        of the class gets to it.
  \item \textbf{Name the headline} (1 min) --- say it, then make the class say it
        back: \textit{evaluate hands you the input and asks for the output ---
        exactly one answer. Solve hands you the output and asks for the
        input(s) --- possibly several, and several is still a function.} Point at
        the direction table while you say it.
  \item \textbf{Point forward} (1 min) --- 1.2 stops asking what the output
        \textit{is} at a given input and starts asking what it \textit{does} as
        the input climbs.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Do not} re-teach domain and range here. If the exit tickets say it did
not land, it is the 1.2 warm-up's job, not this minute's.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment (5 min)]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (final 5 minutes, individual, silent, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Evaluate $f(x) = 6x - 5$ at $x = 3$ (worked space provided) and at
        $x = -1$.
  \item From a small table, read $g(2)$ and then solve $g(x) = 8$.
  \item State the domain and range of a function given as four ordered pairs,
        with one repeated output.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} Item 2(b) is the direction check --- a student who
answers it with an output has missed the lesson's central distinction. Sort
tickets by 2(b) and reteach ``into the machine vs.\ out of the machine'' in the
1.2 warm-up window if more than a handful miss it. On item 3, listing the
repeated 8 twice is a minor slip; swapping domain and range is not.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework --- DeltaMath.} This lesson ships no printed homework; assign the
DeltaMath set and have students record its name, due date, and score in the
homework row of the packet cover. Target coverage: evaluating a function at
positive \textit{and} negative inputs, reading a table or a graph in both
directions (evaluate and solve), and stating domain and range from a list of
points and from a graph. If the DeltaMath set offers bracket notation as its own
item type, include it --- it is the one piece of \S5 with no in-class practice.

\textbf{Extension (optional):} Why does $y = 6/(2x - 8)$ forbid an input?
Students name the excluded value and write the domain in words. This is practice
item 3 with a coefficient on $x$ --- assign it to anyone who cleared item 3
early, and to anyone who did not reach it at all.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson (1.2, Change in Tandem) asks what the output
\textit{does} as the input climbs --- increasing, decreasing, or holding
steady --- and how a graph shows it.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Items 1--3 are the exact algebra
today's evaluating runs on --- a student who misses item 1 or 2 will make the
same sign error inside $f(-2)$ within the hour, so note names. Item 4 is the
promised revisit of the 1.0 function decision and should be quick; the ZIP-code
rule is the one-to-many case.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
Part 1 lives or dies on the language: make students \textit{say} ``$f$ of $x$''
aloud several times, and translate $f(3) = 7$ into a sentence before anyone
computes. In Part 2 the $\square$ line is the whole point --- write the empty
parentheses first, every time. Part 3 is the new spine of this lesson: fill the
direction table \textit{before} touching the table or graph, and treat the
three-answer $f(x) = 5$ as the payoff, not a curiosity. Part 4 reuses the Part 3
graph on purpose --- do not redraw it or students will parse a second figure
instead of thinking about domain. If time runs short, read the bracket-notation
paragraph aloud without stopping for questions and flag it for the DeltaMath
set; do not cut the direction table.

The practice box is the entire independent phase and carries eleven minutes, so
treat it as the lesson's third act. Item 2(c) is the hinge: two inputs share the
output 6, so running $p$ backward is not a function --- many-to-one from 1.0,
seen from the other side, and the first quiet hint of 1.6. If the clock slips,
cut item 3 (the formula domain) and let the Debrief carry it; it returns as the
DeltaMath extension either way.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Debrief]
Five minutes, and on this lesson they are genuinely at risk --- the guided phase
runs 22 minutes and will try to borrow them. Borrow from practice item 3 instead;
it is the one piece of the period that returns as homework. The share-out is one
answer per practice item so that a student who stalled on item 1 still hears the
two-direction result in 2(a), which is the exact shape of exit-ticket item 2(b)
and the only version of it they will have seen otherwise.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Collected, completion credit only. Scan item 2(b) first --- an answer of 11 (an
output) instead of 1 (the input) is the diagnostic miss, and it is the one worth
reteaching. Item 3: accept the range with or without braces, but a doubled 8
earns a margin note, not a deduction.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework --- DeltaMath]
Homework is DeltaMath unless a lesson ships a printed homework component; 1.1
does not. Put the assignment name and due date on the board while students fill
the cover row. Read the report by item type, not overall score: evaluating at a
negative input and \textit{solving} $f(x) = c$ fail for different reasons, and
only the second one means the lesson missed. Bracket notation is the item most
likely to have gone unpracticed in class --- if the DeltaMath set does not carry
it, spend the first two minutes of 1.2 on it rather than assuming it landed. The
extension is the only formula-domain problem with a coefficient on $x$; if Part
4 ran long, or practice item 3 was cut, assign it explicitly rather than
optionally.
\end{teachernote}
