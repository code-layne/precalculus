\textbf{AP Skills} &

\midrule
\textbf{MP 2.B} --- Construct equivalent graphical, numerical,
analytical, or verbal representations of a function, and identify
information from one to apply to another. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item A function maps each input to exactly one output. (1.1.A.1)
  \item Input-output values vary in tandem according to the function rule. (1.1.A.2)
  \item A graph displays all input-output pairs and visual trends. (1.1.B.1)
  \item A verbal description can serve as the basis for constructing a graph. (1.1.B.2)
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{MP 3.A} --- Describe characteristics of a function using
appropriate mathematical language and notation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item If $a < b \Rightarrow f(a) < f(b)$, the function is increasing. (1.1.A.3)
  \item If $a < b \Rightarrow f(a) > f(b)$, the function is decreasing. (1.1.A.4)
  \item Concave up: rate of change is increasing on that interval. (1.1.B.3)
  \item Concave down: rate of change is decreasing on that interval. (1.1.B.4)
  \item Zeros occur where the output equals 0. (1.1.B.5)
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}

function          & A rule that assigns each input exactly one output; written $f(x)$. \\
domain            & The set of all allowable inputs (independent variable values). \\
range             & The set of all possible outputs (dependent variable values). \\
independent var.  & The input variable; plotted on the horizontal axis. \\
dependent var.    & The output variable; plotted on the vertical axis. \\
increasing        & $f$ is increasing on an interval if $a < b \Rightarrow f(a) < f(b)$. \\
decreasing        & $f$ is decreasing on an interval if $a < b \Rightarrow f(a) > f(b)$. \\
concave up        & The rate of change is increasing on that interval (bowl opens up). \\
concave down      & The rate of change is decreasing on that interval (bowl opens down). \\
zeros             & Input values where $f(x) = 0$; the graph crosses or touches the $x$-axis. \\
\end{tabularx}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{sky}
    A car pulls away from a stop sign. \textbf{Think about two different graphs:}
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item The car's \textit{speed} vs.\ time --- does it start fast or slow? Does it keep accelerating?
        \item The car's \textit{distance from the stop sign} vs.\ time --- does it grow quickly at first or slowly?
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} Without drawing, describe in words what each graph would look like.
    Which graph is concave up? Which might eventually concave down? How does one graph's shape
    relate to the behavior of the other?
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{sky}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{Part 1: Functions and Covariation} (12 min)

Introduce the function definition using a water-filling-a-tank context:
as time increases, the volume of water in the tank increases.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Ask: Is volume a function of time? (Yes -- for each moment there is
        exactly one volume.)
  \item Identify: \textit{independent variable} = time, \textit{dependent variable} = volume.
  \item Domain: $[0, T]$ where $T$ = fill time; Range: $[0, V]$ where $V$ = tank capacity.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Worked Example 1 (numerical).} Present a table:

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$V$ (gal)  & 0 & 8 & 16 & 24 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

Ask: As $t$ goes from 0 to 6, does $V$ increase or decrease?
Is the rate of gain constant? (Yes here --- linear.)

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 2: Increasing, Decreasing, \& Concavity} (12 min)

Modify the context: now the tank fills \textit{quickly at first} (drain partially
open, then closes), so:

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$V$ & 0 & 14 & 20 & 24 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}
  \item $V$ is increasing on $[0, 6]$: as $t$ grows, $V$ grows.
  \item From $t=0$ to $2$: gains 14 gal; from $t=2$ to $4$: gains 6 gal;
        from $t=4$ to $6$: gains 4 gal.
  \item Rate of increase is \textit{slowing down} $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{concave down}.
\end{itemize}

Now suppose water drains slowly at first then faster:
rate of change increasing $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{concave up}.

\textbf{Zeros:} The graph of $V$ hits the $x$-axis when $V = 0$ (tank empty).
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
While students work on guided notes and practice, circulate and check:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} Students may confuse "function is decreasing" with "output is negative."
        Cold-call: "Can a function be decreasing but have positive outputs?" (Yes --- falling from 10 to 2.)
  \item \textbf{Common error:} Concavity confused with increasing/decreasing.
        Ask: "Can a function be increasing AND concave down at the same time?" (Yes --- the fast-fill tank.)
  \item \textbf{Notation check:} Are students writing intervals correctly, e.g.\ $(1, 3)$ vs.\ $[1, 3]$?
  \item \textbf{Cold-call:} "Give me an interval where the hiker's elevation is increasing but the rate of change is decreasing."
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
Students work on the \textbf{Group Activity: Hiker's Elevation} in leveled tiers.
Each tier uses the same context (time vs.\ elevation) but increases in demand.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Completed table provided; students identify domain, range,
        and whether elevation is increasing or decreasing on two labeled intervals.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Same table plus a verbal description; students determine
        increasing/decreasing intervals and whether the rate of change is increasing or decreasing
        (concavity) by comparing table differences. No freehand drawing required --- students
        match descriptions.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Analyze a second function (hiker's speed vs.\ time), compare
        to elevation, and explain what concavity of the elevation graph reveals about the hiker's pace.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (last 5--7 minutes, individual, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Given a verbal description of function behavior, state domain, range, and intervals of
        increase/decrease.
  \item Interpret concavity: what does concave up vs.\ concave down tell you about the rate of change?
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} Look for correct use of interval notation and the formal increasing/decreasing
language ($a < b \Rightarrow f(a) \lessgtr f(b)$). Concavity explanations should reference rate of change,
not just shape.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} 5 problems covering domain/range from a table, increasing/decreasing from a verbal
description, a first look at average rate of change (teaser for Lesson 1.2), concavity reasoning, and
one AP-style multiple-choice item.

\textbf{Extension (optional):} A piecewise context (hiker rests, then resumes) asks students to
identify behavior on each piece and predict overall concavity.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson, we formalize the idea of \textit{rate of change} --- how fast
the output changes per unit of input --- and compute it precisely.
\end{skillbox}
