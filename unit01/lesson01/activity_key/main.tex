\begin{scenariobox}[The Ridge Hike]{plumlight}
\small
A hiker leaves the trailhead, climbs to a false summit, drops into a saddle,
climbs to the true summit, and starts back down. The function $E$ gives the
hiker's elevation above the trailhead --- in \textbf{hundreds of feet} ---
$t$ hours after starting. Every tier reads this same graph; nothing is drawn.

\begin{center}

  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (8.6,0) node[below right] {\small $t$ (hours)};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,7.6) node[above] {\small $E(t)$ (hundreds of ft)};
  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8} \node[below] at (\x,0) {\small \x};
  \foreach \y in {1,3,5,7} \node[left] at (0,\y) {\small \y};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,1) -- (2,5);

\boxguard
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Read outputs from the graph.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $E(0) = $ \ans{1} \quad (how high the trailhead sits)
          \item $E(3) = $ \ans{4}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item The statement $E(1) = 3$ is a full sentence in disguise. Write it in
        words, using the units.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{One hour after starting, the hiker is 300 feet above the trailhead.}

  \item The true summit is the highest point on the whole hike.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        It happens at $t = $ \ans{6} hours, at an elevation of
        \ans{7} hundred feet.

  \item For this function: \quad Input quantity: \ans{time in hours} \quad
        Output quantity: \ans{elevation, in hundreds of ft}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Evaluate $E(6)$ from the graph. \quad $E(6) = $ \ans{7}

  \item Solve $E(t) = 5$. \quad $t = $ \ans{2}, \ans{5}, and
        \ans{7}

        Why is it fine for this equation to have three answers, even though $E$
        is a function?

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{The rule restricts inputs, not outputs: each time still gives exactly one elevation. Three different times sharing the elevation 500 ft is many-to-one, which a function allows.}

  \item State the domain and range of $E$ as inequalities.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        Domain: \ans{0} $\le t \le$ \ans{8} \quad
        Range: \ans{1} $\le E \le$ \ans{7}

  \item Use the graph to explain how we know $E$ \textbf{is} a function of $t$.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{Every vertical line through the graph hits it once --- each time has exactly one elevation.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Flip the machine: is \textbf{time} a function of \textbf{elevation} for
        this hike? Use the three moments the hiker is 500 feet up in your
        explanation.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{No. The input 500 ft would have to produce three outputs at once --- $t = 2$, $t = 5$, and $t = 7$. One input with several outputs is one-to-many, so time is not a function of elevation.}

  \item A different function is given by the formula $m(x) = \dfrac{20}{x - 6}$.
        Which input must be excluded from the domain, and why?

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{$x = 6$ must be excluded: it makes the denominator $6 - 6 = 0$, and division by zero is undefined, so the rule produces no output there. The domain is all real numbers except 6.}

  \item Arithmetic does not stop at $t = 8$ --- you could put $t = 20$ into any
        formula. Yet we say the domain of $E$ is only $0 \le t \le 8$. What is
        it about the \textit{context} that sets those two endpoints?

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{The hike itself: it begins at $t = 0$ and the recorded walk ends at $t = 8$. Times outside that window describe no hiker on this trail, so they are not inputs this function uses.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
