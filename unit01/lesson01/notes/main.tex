\begin{notesbox}{1. Function Notation --- Naming the Machine}
Yesterday's trustworthy machine gets a name. The notation $f(x)$ has three
parts:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $f$ is the \blank{3cm} of the function (the machine itself),
  \item $x$ is the \blank{3cm} (what goes in the slot),
  \item $f(x)$ is the \blank{3cm} (what comes out).
\end{itemize}

$f(x)$ is read ``$f$ \blank{1.4cm} $x$'' --- it is \textbf{not} $f$ times $x$.

\medskip
\textbf{In context.} A phone plan charges by the minute: $C(m) = 0.10m + 5$.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item The function's name is \blank{1.4cm}; the input $m$ stands for
        \blank{3.4cm}; the output $C(m)$ stands for \blank{3.4cm}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{One statement, three facts.} The single statement $f(3) = 7$ tells us:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item the input is \blank{1.4cm}, \quad the output is \blank{1.4cm},
  \item and the point ( \blank{1.2cm} , \blank{1.2cm} ) is on the graph of $f$.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. Evaluating from a Formula}
To \textbf{evaluate} a function, substitute the input into the formula ---
\textit{always inside parentheses} --- then do the arithmetic.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 1.} Let $f(x) = 2x + 5$. Find $f(4)$.

\begin{work}
  f(4) &= 2(4) + 5 \\
       &= 13
\end{work}

\textbf{Example 2.} Same $f$. Find $f(-3)$.

\begin{work}
  f(-3) &= 2(-3) + 5 \\
        &= -1
\end{work}

\textbf{Example 3.} Let $g(x) = x^2 - 4$. Find $g(-2)$.

\begin{work}
  g(-2) &= (-2)^2 - 4 \\
        &= 4 - 4 \\
        &= 0
\end{work}

\textbf{Watch the trap:} $(-2)^2 = $ \blank{1.4cm}, but $-2^2 = $ \blank{1.4cm}.
The parentheses habit is what saves you.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. Evaluating from a Table and a Graph}
A table \textit{is} a function: each column is an input--output pair.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\
\hline
$g(x)$ & 3 & 7 & 11 & 15 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $g(4) = $ \blank{1.4cm} \quad (input 4, read the output)
  \item Solve $g(x) = 3$: \quad $x = $ \blank{1.4cm} \quad (output 3, find the
        input)
\end{itemize}

\begin{notesbox}{4. Domain and Range}
The \textbf{domain} is the set of all \blank{1.8cm} a function uses; the
\textbf{range} is the set of all \blank{1.8cm} it produces. A repeated output
is listed \blank{1.4cm}.

\medskip
\textbf{From a list of points.} Let $f = \{(1, 3),\ (2, 5),\ (4, 5),\ (6, 8)\}$.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Domain: \blank{3.4cm} \quad Range: \blank{3.4cm}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From a graph.} Look back at the graph in Part 3. The graph runs from
$x = 0$ to $x = 9$ and its heights run from $1$ to $4$.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Domain: \blank{1.4cm} $\le x \le$ \blank{1.4cm} \quad
        Range: \blank{1.4cm} $\le y \le$ \blank{1.4cm}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From a formula (first look).} Let $g(x) = \dfrac{10}{x - 2}$.
At $x = 2$ the denominator is \blank{1.2cm}, and division by zero is
\blank{2.4cm} --- so $2$ is excluded. The domain is all real numbers except
\blank{1.2cm}.
\end{notesbox}
