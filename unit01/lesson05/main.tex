    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students evaluate and construct compositions of functions
    analytically and numerically, distinguish $f \circ g$ from $g \circ f$, and decompose
    a given function into a composition of simpler functions, connecting composition to
    transformations. \hfill\textit{\small Skills: 1.C and 2.B}
\end{tcolorbox}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{AP Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Skill 1.C} --- Construct new functions using compositions. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$: outputs of $g$ become inputs of $f$. (EK 2.7.A.1)
  \item Values can be found from tables, graphs, or formulas. (EK 2.7.A.2)
  \item Composition is not commutative: $f(g(x)) \ne g(f(x))$ in general. (EK 2.7.A.3)
  \item $f(x) = x$ is the identity function; composing with it returns the other function. (EK 2.7.A.4)
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{Skill 2.B} --- Construct equivalent representations. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Substitute $g(x)$ for every $x$ in $f(x)$ to build $f(g(x))$ analytically. (EK 2.7.B.2)
  \item Composition relates quantities not directly linked by one formula. (EK 2.7.B.1)
  \item Decompose complex functions into simpler components; link to transformations. (EK 2.7.C.1–C.3)
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{navy}}p{0.27\linewidth} X @{}}
Composition          & The operation $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$: feed output of $g$ into $f$. \\
Composite function   & The resulting function $f \circ g$ formed by composing $f$ with $g$. \\
Domain of $f \circ g$ & Inputs $x$ in the domain of $g$ whose outputs $g(x)$ are in the domain of $f$. \\
Identity function    & $f(x) = x$; composing it with any $g$ returns $g$: $(f \circ g)(x) = g(x)$. \\
Non-commutativity    & In general, $f(g(x)) \ne g(f(x))$; order matters in composition. \\
Decomposition        & Writing a complex function $h$ as $f(g(x))$ using simpler $f$ and $g$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{sky}
    Spiral review (warm-up) covers evaluating a function at a given input
    ($f(4)$ and $f(-2)$ for $f(x) = 3x-1$), and walking through a step-by-step
    composition numerically: compute $f(g(1))$ and $g(f(1))$ and compare them.
    Students also begin to notice that order produces different results.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{sky}
Write on the board: \textbf{``Your cell-carrier charges \$0.10 per mile of data used, and
your daily driving distance $d$ miles converts to data use as $u(d) = 0.5d$ GB. The charge
is $C(u) = 10u$ dollars. What is your daily charge as a function of miles driven?''}

After brief class discussion, reveal: $C(u(d)) = C(0.5d) = 10(0.5d) = 5d$.
We used \textit{composition} to link two quantities not directly related by a single formula.
\textbf{Today: how to evaluate, build, and decompose compositions.}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{sky}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Part 1 (12 min): Evaluate from a Table and Analytically.}

Show the table:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
$x$ & $f(x)$ & $g(x)$ \\ \hline
1 & 4 & 2 \\
2 & 7 & 1 \\
3 & 10 & 3 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Walk through: $f(g(1)) = f(2) = 7$; $f(g(2)) = f(1) = 4$; $g(f(1)) = g(4) = $ (undefined here,
so note domain restriction). Stress: use the inner function's output as the outer function's input.

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 2 (12 min): Construct Analytically.}

Given $f(x) = 2x + 1$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 3$.

Build $f(g(x))$: substitute $g(x)$ for every $x$ in $f$:
$f(g(x)) = 2(x^2-3)+1 = 2x^2-5$.

Build $g(f(x))$: substitute $f(x)$ for every $x$ in $g$:
$g(f(x)) = (2x+1)^2-3 = 4x^2+4x-2$.

Compare: $f(g(x)) \ne g(f(x))$ --- composition is \textbf{not} commutative.
\end{multicols}

\medskip
\textbf{Part 3 (12 min): Decompose and Connect to Transformations.}

Decompose $h(x) = (3x+2)^4$: natural split is $f(u) = u^4$ and $g(x) = 3x+2$, so $h(x) = f(g(x))$.
Verify: $f(g(x)) = (3x+2)^4$ --- correct.

Connect to transformations (EK 2.7.C.2–C.3): $g(x) = f(x+k)$ is composition of $f$ with
$h(x) = x+k$; $g(x) = f(kx)$ is composition of $f$ with $h(x) = kx$. Every horizontal
shift/stretch is secretly a composition.

Identity: $(f \circ \text{id})(x) = f(x+0) = f(x)$. The identity function ``does nothing.''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item After Part 1: cold-call ``Why can't we find $g(f(1))$ from that table?'' Expect:
        $f(1) = 4$, and 4 is not in the table as an input for $g$.
  \item During Part 2: watch for students who substitute $g(x)$ into only part of $f$; confirm
        they replace \textit{every} $x$.
  \item After Part 2: cold-call ``If $f(g(2)) = 5$, does that mean $g(f(2)) = 5$?'' Expect: not
        necessarily; show with the example.
  \item During Part 3: look for students who reverse $f$ and $g$ in the decomposition; test by
        substituting back.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Evaluate $f(g(0))$, $f(g(2))$, $g(f(0))$ for given
        linear functions; decide if $f \circ g = g \circ f$ for these values.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Fill a table for $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$ from a value table;
        write analytic formulas for $f \circ g$ when $f(x) = x^2$, $g(x) = 2x-1$; state domain.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Decompose $h(x) = (3x+2)^4$ two different ways; verify each;
        explain which decomposition is $f(g(x))$ and which is $g(f(x))$ --- or explain why a
        swap is impossible.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
Exit ticket (3 questions, 1 page): (1) find $f(g(2))$ for $f(x) = x+4$, $g(x) = 3x$;
(2) write the formula for $(f \circ g)(x)$ and simplify; (3) write $(g \circ f)(x)$ and
compare.

Sort by: students who miss (1) need review of evaluating the inner function first (LO 2.7.A);
students who miss (2) need re-teaching of analytic substitution (EK 2.7.B.2);
students who miss (3) need work on non-commutativity (EK 2.7.A.3).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
Homework (6 problems): numerical composition from a table; analytic $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$
for linear and quadratic; evaluate $f \circ g$ at a negative input; decompose a cubic; real-world
profit composition; AP-style MC on $f \circ g$.

Preview of Lesson 2.8 (Inverse Functions): if we compose a function with its inverse, we get the
identity function, $f(f^{-1}(x)) = x$. Decomposing a function is the first step toward finding what
``undoes'' it --- today's decomposition skill leads directly to inverses.
\end{skillbox}
