\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can evaluate a composite function such as $f(g(3))$ from a table or
        from formulas, working from the \textit{inside out}.
  \item I can build $(f \circ g)(x)$ and $(g \circ f)(x)$ by substitution, and
        show that changing the order usually changes the function.
  \item I can decompose a function into an inner and an outer piece --- and
        recognize a horizontal shift as a composition in disguise.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
Last lesson one function's graph moved. Today \textbf{two functions work in a
row}: the output of the first becomes the input of the second, written
$(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$. Two sentences carry the lesson --- work from the
\textit{inside out}, and the \textit{order} is part of the answer. A \$10
coupon and a 6\% sales tax do not cost the same in both orders.
\end{remindbox}
