\begin{scenariobox}[The Summit Cable Car]{plumlight}
\small
A cable car climbs from the valley station to the summit in 10 minutes. Two
instruments are running on the mountain, and \textbf{neither one measures the
same thing}:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item $E(t)$ --- the car's \textbf{elevation}, in feet above the valley
        station, $t$ minutes after departure.
  \item $T(e)$ --- the air \textbf{temperature}, in $^\circ$F, at elevation $e$
        feet above the valley station.
\end{itemize}

Nothing on this mountain reports the temperature as a function of \textit{time}
--- but chaining the two instruments does exactly that.

\begin{center}

  \draw[linegray, very thin, xstep=1, ystep=4] (0,0) grid (10,20);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (11,0) node[below right] {\small $t$ (min)};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,22.5) node[above] {\small $E(t)$ (ft)};
  \foreach \x in {2,4,6,8,10} \node[below, font=\small] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y/\lab in {4/400, 8/800, 12/1200, 16/1600, 20/2000}
    \node[left, font=\small] at (0,\y) {\lab};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,0) -- (10,20);
  \fill[plum] (0,0) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (4,8) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (10,20) circle (2.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=6pt]
  \item Read each one straight off its own table.

        $E(4) = $ \ans{800} feet \qquad\qquad
        $T(800) = $ \ans{58} $^\circ$F

  \item Those two lines were really one question: \textbf{how cold is it four
        minutes into the ride?} Put them together.

        \begin{work}
          T(E(4)) &= T(800) \\
                  &= 58
        \end{work}

        $T(E(4)) = $ \ans{58} $^\circ$F

  \item Now do the whole ride. Fill the \textbf{handoff} row all the way across
        \textit{before} you look up a single temperature.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        $t$ (min) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\
        \hline
        handoff: $E(t)$ & \ans{0} & \ans{400} & \ans{800} & \ans{1200} & \ans{1600} \\
        \hline
        $T(E(t))$ ($^\circ$F) & \ans{62} & \ans{60} & \ans{58} & \ans{56} & \ans{54} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \end{center}

  \item The handoff is not a number of minutes and it is not a number of
        degrees. It is a number of \ans{feet}.

  \item Which instrument runs first, $E$ or $T$? \ans{$E$}

        Say in one sentence why it has to be that one.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{$T$ only accepts an elevation, and $E$ is the one that turns a time into an elevation.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

The engineers publish formulas for both instruments:
\[
  E(t) = 200t \qquad\qquad T(e) = 62 - 0.005e
\]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Build the composite $T(E(t))$ by handing $E(t)$ to $T$.

        \begin{work}
          T(E(t)) &= T(200t) \\
                  &= 62 - 0.005(200t) \\
                  &= 62 - t
        \end{work}

        $T(E(t)) = $ \ans{$62 - t$}

  \item That formula says something a rider can feel. Finish the sentence:
        the temperature drops \ans{1} $^\circ$F for every
        \ans{minute} of the ride.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The ride lasts 10 minutes, so the summit temperature is
                \ans{52} $^\circ$F.
          \item Check that against the tables in the scenario box. Do they
                agree? \ans{yes}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item A rider puts a jacket on when it hits $55^\circ$F.

        \begin{work}
          62 - t &= 55 \\
               t &= 7
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item That happens \ans{7} minutes into the ride.
          \item The handoff at that moment --- the elevation --- is
                $E(7) = $ \ans{1400} feet.
        \end{enumerate}

  \item No instrument on this mountain reports temperature as a function of
        time. Explain in two sentences why composing $E$ and $T$ answers a
        question neither one can answer alone.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{$E$ turns a time into an elevation, and $T$ turns an elevation into a temperature.}
        \ansline{Elevation is the handoff that links them, so the chain reports degrees from minutes.}

  \item \textbf{Now reverse the order.} Someone writes $E(T(t))$ instead.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The handoff would now be a number of \ans{degrees}.
          \item But $E$ only accepts a number of \ans{minutes}, so
                $E(T(t))$ is \ans{meaningless}.
          \item This is a second reason $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$ are different:
                sometimes the reversed order does not even \ans{make sense}.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Three machines in a row.} The summit kiosk sells hot drinks,
        and its sales depend on the temperature: $D(T) = 40 - 0.5T$ drinks per
        hour when the air is $T^\circ$F.

        \begin{work}
          D(T(E(t))) &= D(62 - t) \\
                     &= 40 - 0.5(62 - t) \\
                     &= 40 - 31 + 0.5t \\
                     &= 9 + 0.5t
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $D(T(E(t))) = $ \ans{$9 + 0.5t$}
          \item At the summit ($t = 10$) the kiosk sells \ans{14}
                drinks per hour.
          \item This chain has \textbf{two} handoffs. Name them, with units.

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{The elevation in feet ($E$ to $T$), then the temperature in $^\circ$F ($T$ to $D$).}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{The late departure.} One morning the car leaves three minutes
        behind schedule, so its elevation is $L(t) = E(t - 3)$.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item Write $L$ as a composition: $L(t) = E(h(t))$ where
                $h(t) = $ \ans{$t - 3$}, and the handoff is a number of
                \ans{minutes}.
          \item Simplify: $L(t) = 200($ \ans{$t-3$} $) = $ \ans{$200t - 600$}
          \item In Lesson 1.4 language, the graph of $E$ moved \ans{3}
                units to the \ans{right} --- and now you can say why: the
                handoff is what $E$ gets \ans{asked}.
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Undo the chain.} The ride's composite is $T(E(t)) = 62 - t$.
        A control-room display reads the temperature and prints how many
        minutes the car has been climbing --- so it applies some function $m$
        with $m\big(T(E(t))\big) = t$.

        \begin{work}
          m(62 - t) &= t \\
              m(y)  &= 62 - y
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $m(y) = $ \ans{$62 - y$}
          \item Test it at $t = 4$: the handoff into $m$ is \ans{58},
                and $m$ of that is \ans{4}.
          \item Composing the whole chain with $m$ gives back the number you
                started with --- the \ans{identity} function. That is exactly
                what tomorrow's lesson is about.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
