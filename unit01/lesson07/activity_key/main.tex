\begin{scenariobox}[The Courtyard Committee]{plumlight}
\small
The school's courtyard committee has \textbf{60 feet of metal edging} for a new
rectangular flower bed. One long side of the bed runs against the building wall,
so that side needs no edging at all --- only the two ends and the front are
edged. The committee wants the largest possible planting area.

\smallskip
Let $w$ be the width of the bed in feet (the two short ends) and $\ell$ the
length along the wall. The committee measured five trial layouts before anyone
did any algebra:

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$w$ (ft) & 5 & 10 & 15 & 20 & 25 \\
\hline
$A$ (ft$^2$) & 250 & 400 & 450 & 400 & 250 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Check the input row first.} Are the widths equally spaced?
        \ans{Yes} \quad By how much each step? \ans{5 ft}

  \item \textbf{Take the differences.} Fill in both rows.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        $w$ (ft) & 5 & 10 & 15 & 20 & 25 \\
        \hline
        $A$ (ft$^2$) & 250 & 400 & 450 & 400 & 250 \\
        \hline
        1st difference & --- & \ans{150} & \ans{50} & \ans{$-50$} & \ans{$-150$} \\
        \hline
        2nd difference & --- & --- & \ans{$-100$} & \ans{$-100$} & \ans{$-100$} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \end{center}

  \item \textbf{Name it.} The first differences are \ans{not constant}, but the second
        differences are all \ans{$-100$}, so this data is best modeled by a
        \ans{quadratic} function.

  \item \textbf{Match each context to its model type.} Write a letter in each
        blank: \textbf{L} linear, \textbf{Q} quadratic, \textbf{C} cubic,
        \textbf{R} rational, \textbf{P} piecewise.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The volume of a cube-shaped bin, as a function of its edge
                length. \ans{C}
          \item A phone plan costs \$30 a month plus \$5 for each extra
                gigabyte. \ans{L}
          \item The brightness of a lamp, which is inversely proportional to
                the square of your distance from it. \ans{R}
          \item A garage charges \$4 for the first hour and \$2 for every hour
                after that. \ans{P}
          \item The area of a rug whose length is always twice its width, as a
                function of the width. \ans{Q}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

The committee wants a formula, not five measurements.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Write the constraint.} Two ends and one front use all 60 feet
        of edging.

        \ans{2} $w \; + \;$ \ans{$\ell$} $= 60$ \qquad so \quad $\ell = $
        \ans{$60 - 2w$}

  \item \textbf{Substitute.} Area is $A = w \cdot \ell$. Build the model.

        \begin{work}
          A(w) &= w(60 - 2w) \\
               &= 60w - 2w^2
        \end{work}

        $A(w) = $ \ans{$60w - 2w^2$}

  \item \textbf{Restrict the domain.} The width and the length must both be
        positive:

        \ans{0} $< w <$ \ans{30}

        Why that upper end? \ans{At $w = 30$ no edging is left for the front.}

  \item \textbf{Check against the committee's table.} Evaluate the model at two
        of the measured widths.

        \begin{work}
          A(10) &= 60(10) - 2(10)^2 \\
                &= 600 - 200 \\
                &= 400 \\
          A(15) &= 60(15) - 2(15)^2 \\
                &= 900 - 450 \\
                &= 450
        \end{work}

        $A(10) = $ \ans{400} \qquad $A(15) = $ \ans{450} \qquad
        Do these match the table? \ans{Yes}

  \item \textbf{Find the largest bed.} For $A(w) = aw^2 + bw$ the vertex is at
        $w = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.

        \begin{work}
          w &= -\frac{60}{2(-2)} \\
            &= 15
        \end{work}

        Best width $= $ \ans{15} ft, \quad length $= $ \ans{30} ft,
        \quad area $= $ \ans{450} ft$^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Two average rates of change --- with units.} Use
        $A(w) = 60w - 2w^2$.

        \begin{work}
          \frac{A(10) - A(5)}{10 - 5} &= \frac{400 - 250}{5} \\
                                      &= 30 \\
          \frac{A(20) - A(15)}{20 - 15} &= \frac{400 - 450}{5} \\
                                        &= -10
        \end{work}

        On $[5, 10]$: \ans{30} \qquad On $[15, 20]$: \ans{$-10$}

        Units: \ans{square feet of area per foot of width}

        Explain what the change of sign tells the committee.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{Area is still growing on $[5,10]$ but shrinking on $[15,20]$, so the largest bed lies between them.}

  \item \textbf{A code rule changes the answer.} The building code requires a
        walkway, so the bed's width must be \textbf{at least 18 feet}. The new
        domain is $18 \le w < 30$.

        \begin{work}
          A(18) &= 60(18) - 2(18)^2 \\
                &= 1080 - 648 \\
                &= 432
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item Is the old best width $w = 15$ still allowed? \ans{No}
          \item $A(18) = $ \ans{432} ft$^2$
          \item On $18 \le w < 30$ the area only \ans{decreases} as $w$ grows, so
                the largest legal bed is at $w = $ \ans{18}.
          \item In one sentence: what changed the answer --- the model, or the
                domain?

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{The domain. $A(w)$ is the same function; the code just removed the input where its maximum lived.}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{A different kind of model (a look ahead to Unit 3).} A lamp's
        brightness $L$ is \textbf{inversely proportional to the square} of the
        distance $d$ in meters: $L = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$. At $d = 2$ m the meter
        reads $L = 90$.

        \begin{work}
          90 &= \frac{k}{2^2} \\
           k &= 360 \\
        L(6) &= \frac{360}{6^2} \\
             &= 10
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $k = $ \ans{360} \qquad so $L(d) = $ \ans{$360/d^2$}
          \item $L(6) = $ \ans{10}
          \item Domain restriction: $d$ \ans{$> 0$}, because
                \ans{distance cannot be zero or negative}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
