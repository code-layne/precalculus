\begin{scenariobox}[The Cardboard Bin]{plumlight}
\small
The recycling club has a stack of flat cardboard sheets, each
\textbf{24 inches by 18 inches}. To make an open-top bin they cut a square of
side $x$ inches out of each corner, fold the four flaps up, and tape the
corners. They want the bin that holds the most.

\smallskip
The only thing the club can actually change is \textbf{how big a square they
cut}. Every other measurement of the bin follows from that one decision.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.19cm, y=0.19cm]
  \fill[lilac] (0,0) rectangle (24,18);
  \fill[goldbg] (0,0)  rectangle (3,3);
  \fill[goldbg] (21,0) rectangle (24,3);
  \fill[goldbg] (0,15) rectangle (3,18);
  \fill[goldbg] (21,15) rectangle (24,18);
  \draw[goldacc, thick] (0,0)  rectangle (3,3);
  \draw[goldacc, thick] (21,0) rectangle (24,3);
  \draw[goldacc, thick] (0,15) rectangle (3,18);
  \draw[goldacc, thick] (21,15) rectangle (24,18);
  \draw[dashed, slate] (3,0) -- (3,18);
  \draw[dashed, slate] (21,0) -- (21,18);
  \draw[dashed, slate] (0,3) -- (24,3);

  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,0) rectangle (24,18);

\end{tikzpicture}

\smallskip
The club built six test bins and measured the volume of each.

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[18]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Name the question.} In the club's table, which quantity is the
        question (the input) and which is the answer (the output)?

        Question: \ans{the cut $x$} \qquad Answer: \ans{the volume $V$}

  \item \textbf{Check the question row first.} Are the cut sizes equally
        spaced? \ans{Yes} \quad By how much each step? \ans{1 inch}

  \item \textbf{Take the differences.} Keep going until a row is constant.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        $x$ (in) & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
        \hline
        $V$ (in$^3$) & 352 & 560 & 648 & 640 & 560 & 432 \\
        \hline
        1st & --- & \ans{208} & \ans{88} & \ans{$-8$} & \ans{$-80$} & \ans{$-128$} \\
        \hline
        2nd & --- & --- & \ans{$-120$} & \ans{$-96$} & \ans{$-72$} & \ans{$-48$} \\
        \hline
        3rd & --- & --- & --- & \ans{24} & \ans{24} & \ans{24} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \end{center}

  \item \textbf{Name it.} The \ans{third} differences are the first constant
        row, so the volume is modeled by a \ans{cubic} function --- and that
        matches the context, because a volume multiplies \ans{three}
        lengths.

  \item \textbf{Match each context to its model type.} Write a letter in each
        blank: \textbf{L} linear, \textbf{Q} quadratic, \textbf{C} cubic,
        \textbf{R} rational, \textbf{P} piecewise.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The volume of a cube-shaped storage bin, as a function of its
                edge length. \ans{C}
          \item A phone plan costs \$30 a month plus \$5 for each extra
                gigabyte. \ans{L}
          \item The brightness of a lamp, which is inversely proportional to
                the square of your distance from it. \ans{R}
          \item A garage charges \$4 for the first hour and \$2 for every hour
                after that. \ans{P}
          \item The area of a rug whose length is always \textbf{twice} its
                width, as a function of the width. \ans{Q}
          \item The area of a rug whose length is \textbf{fixed at 9 feet}, as
                a function of the width. \ans{L}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[24]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

Six measurements are not a model. Build one.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Make every dimension answer to the cut.} The question is $x$,
        the size of the corner square. Write each dimension of the finished bin
        in terms of $x$.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tabularx}{0.9\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{0.30\linewidth} X @{}}
        \hline
        height & \ans{$x$} \\
        \hline
        base length & \ans{$24 - 2x$} \\
        \hline
        base width & \ans{$18 - 2x$} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}

  \item \textbf{Substitute into the quantity you want.} Volume is
        height $\times$ length $\times$ width.

        \begin{work}
          V(x) &= x(24 - 2x)(18 - 2x) \\
               &= x(432 - 84x + 4x^2) \\
               &= 4x^3 - 84x^2 + 432x
        \end{work}

        $V(x) = $ \ans{$4x^3 - 84x^2 + 432x$}

  \item \textbf{Check it against the club's table.}

        \begin{work}
          V(3) &= 3(24 - 6)(18 - 6) \\
               &= 3(18)(12) \\
               &= 648
        \end{work}

        $V(3) = $ \ans{648} in$^3$ \qquad Does it match the table?
        \ans{Yes}

  \item \textbf{Which questions is the club allowed to ask?} The cut must be
        positive, and both base dimensions must stay positive.

        \ans{0} $< x <$ \ans{9}

        Which of the two base dimensions set the upper end, and why?

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{The 18-inch side: $18 - 2x$ hits 0 at $x = 9$, while $24 - 2x$ is still 6.}

  \item \textbf{Find the best bin.} From the table, the largest volume is
        somewhere between $x = $ \ans{3} and $x = $ \ans{4}. Test the
        halfway cut.

        \begin{work}
          V(3.5) &= 3.5(24 - 7)(18 - 7) \\
                 &= 3.5(17)(11) \\
                 &= 654.5
        \end{work}

        $V(3.5) = $ \ans{654.5} in$^3$, which beats \textbf{both} table
        entries. A model answers questions nobody measured.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[24]
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Two average rates of change --- with units.} Use the club's
        table.

        \begin{work}
          \frac{V(3) - V(1)}{3 - 1} &= \frac{648 - 352}{2} \\
                                    &= 148 \\
          \frac{V(6) - V(4)}{6 - 4} &= \frac{432 - 640}{2} \\
                                    &= -104
        \end{work}

        On $[1, 3]$: \ans{$+148$} \qquad On $[4, 6]$: \ans{$-104$}

        Units: \ans{cubic inches per inch of cut}

        What does the change of sign tell the club about the best cut?

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{Volume rises, then falls --- so the best cut sits between $x = 3$ and $x = 4$.}

  \item \textbf{Same sheet, a different answer.} The shipping company does not
        care about volume --- it charges by the \textbf{footprint}, the area of
        the bin's base.

        \begin{work}
          B(x) &= (24 - 2x)(18 - 2x) \\
               &= 432 - 84x + 4x^2
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $B(x) = $ \ans{$4x^2 - 84x + 432$}
          \item $B(3) = $ \ans{216} in$^2$
          \item The volume model was \ans{cubic} and this one is
                \ans{quadratic} --- from the \textbf{same} sheet of cardboard.
                The question is still the cut $x$, so what changed?

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{The answer did: the footprint multiplies two lengths, the volume three.}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Now change the question (a look ahead to Unit 3).} The club
        decides every bin must hold exactly \textbf{648 in$^3$}. The question
        becomes the footprint $B$, and the answer is the height $h$ the bin
        needs.

        \begin{work}
          h \cdot B &= 648 \\
                  h &= \frac{648}{B} \\
             h(162) &= \frac{648}{162} \\
                    &= 4
        \end{work}

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $h(B) = $ \ans{$648/B$} \qquad Model type: \ans{rational}
          \item A sheet cut to give a footprint of 162 in$^2$ needs a height of
                \ans{4} inches.
          \item As the footprint grows, the required height \ans{shrinks},
                because the question sits \ans{underneath}.
          \item Domain restriction: $B$ \ans{$> 0$}, because \ans{a bin needs a base}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
