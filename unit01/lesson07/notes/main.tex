\begin{notesbox}{Rate-of-Change Clues: The Difference-Table Method}
\textbf{Setup:} Inputs must be \blank{2.5cm}-spaced. Compute differences of the output column.

\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $y$ & $\Delta y$ (1st diff.) & $\Delta^2 y$ (2nd diff.) \\
\hline
0 & 1  & ---         & ---         \\
\hline
1 & 4  & \blank{2cm} & ---         \\
\hline
2 & 11 & \blank{2cm} & \blank{2cm} \\
\hline
3 & 22 & \blank{2cm} & \blank{2cm} \\
\hline
4 & 37 & \blank{2cm} & \blank{2cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Decision tree:}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item 1st differences \textbf{constant} $\Rightarrow$ model is \blank{2.5cm} (degree \blank{0.8cm})
  \item 2nd differences \textbf{constant} $\Rightarrow$ model is \blank{2.5cm} (degree \blank{0.8cm})
  \item 3rd differences \textbf{constant} $\Rightarrow$ model is \blank{2.5cm} (degree \blank{0.8cm})
  \item $n$th differences constant $\Rightarrow$ model is degree-\blank{0.8cm} polynomial
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Key idea:} Constant \textit{first} differences mean a \blank{2.5cm} rate of change.
Constant \textit{second} differences mean the rate of change is itself \blank{2.5cm}.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{Piecewise Models}
A piecewise-defined function uses \blank{3cm} formulas over \blank{3cm} intervals.

\medskip
\textbf{Example scenario:} A delivery driver travels at a constant speed for the first
20 minutes, stops for 5 minutes, then accelerates home. This situation requires
\blank{1.5cm} pieces because the \blank{4cm} changes over different intervals.

\medskip
To describe a piecewise model, state:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item The \blank{2.5cm} for each piece.
  \item The type of function for each piece (e.g., \blank{2cm}, \blank{2cm}).
  \item The \blank{3cm} conditions (where each piece applies).
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
