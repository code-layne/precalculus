\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Explain what the \ans{average rate of change} of a function over an
        interval promises, in words.
  \item Compute it from a \ans{formula}, a \ans{table}, or a
        \ans{graph}, with units.
  \item Say what the \ans{sign} of a rate of change tells me about how two
        quantities move together.
  \item Use a list of average rates of change to tell a \ans{linear} function
        from a \ans{quadratic} one and to name \ans{concavity}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Average rate of change:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Over $[a,b]$, the constant rate $\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ that gives the same change in output.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Secant line:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The line through two points of a graph; its slope is the average rate of change there.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Positive rate of change:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{As the input increases the output increases --- the quantities move the same way.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Negative rate of change:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{As the input increases the output decreases --- the quantities move opposite ways.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Constant rate of change:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The same average rate over every interval --- exactly what makes a function linear.}
\end{vocabbox}

\boxguard[24]
\begin{notesbox}{1. The Stand-In}
The \textbf{average rate of change} of a function over an interval is the one
\textbf{steady} rate that would produce the same change in output over the
same change in input.

\medskip
\textbf{Both climbers, over the whole 30 seconds.}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Ravi: change in output $= 60 - 0 = $ \ans{60} steps;
        change in input $=$ \ans{30} seconds; average rate of change
        $=$ \ans{2 steps/s}.
  \item Mira: change in output $=$ \ans{60} steps; change in input
        $=$ \ans{30} seconds; average rate of change $=$ \ans{2 steps/s}.
\end{itemize}

The two climbs look nothing alike, and the averages are \ans{identical}.

\medskip
\textbf{The formula.} For a function $f$ on the interval from $x = a$ to
$x = b$:
\[
  \text{average rate of change} \;=\; \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}
  \;=\; \frac{\text{change in output}}{\text{change in input}}
\]

\medskip
\textbf{What the stand-in promises --- and what it costs.} A steady climber at
2 steps per second starts at the same place, ends at the same place, and takes
the same 30 seconds. The average is pinned only at the \ans{endpoints} of the
interval, so everything that happened \ans{between} them is thrown away
on purpose.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{2. Computing It Three Ways}
\textbf{(a) From a formula.} Let $f(x) = x^2 + 3$. Find the average rate of
change over $[1, 4]$. (You already found $f(1)$ and $f(4)$ on the warm-up.)

\begin{work}
  \text{AROC} &= \frac{f(4) - f(1)}{4 - 1} \\
              &= \frac{19 - 4}{3} \\
              &= 5
\end{work}

\medskip
\textbf{(b) From a table.} $T(t)$ is the temperature in $^\circ$F,
$t$ hours after 6 a.m.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ (hours after 6 a.m.) & 0 & 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\
\hline
$T(t)$ ($^\circ$F) & 52 & 58 & 68 & 74 & 72 \\
\hline
average rate of change & --- & \ans{3} & \ans{5} & \ans{3} & \ans{$-1$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

The morning warmed fastest between $t = $ \ans{2} and $t = $
\ans{4}, at \ans{5 degrees} per hour.

\medskip
\textbf{(c) From a graph.} $g(t)$ is the height of a kite in meters, $t$
minutes after it is let out. The two dashed lines are drawn for you.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.62cm, y=0.46cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,0) grid (8,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (8.7,0) node[below right] {\small $t$ (min)};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,6.6) node[above] {\small $g(t)$ (m)};
  \foreach \x in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {1,3,5} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[dashed, thick, goldacc] (0,1) -- (4,5);
  \draw[dashed, thick, goldacc] (4,5) -- (8,1);
  \draw[very thick, plum] plot[smooth] coordinates
    {(0,1) (1,2.6) (2,3.9) (3,4.7) (4,5) (5,4.7) (6,3.9) (7,2.6) (8,1)};
  \fill[plum] (0,1) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (4,5) circle (2.5pt);
  \fill[plum] (8,1) circle (2.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Over $[0,4]$: the kite goes from \ans{1} m to \ans{5} m, so
        the average rate of change is \ans{$+1$ m/min}.
  \item Over $[4,8]$: the average rate of change is \ans{$-1$ m/min}.
  \item Each dashed line is a \textbf{secant line} --- the straight line
        through two points of the graph. Its \ans{slope} \textit{is} the
        average rate of change over that interval.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Order matters.} Whichever input you subtract first downstairs, its
output must be subtracted \ans{first} upstairs.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{3. What the Sign Says --- and What Zero Does Not Say}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item A \textbf{positive} rate of change: as the input increases, the output
        \ans{increases}. The two quantities move the \ans{same} way.
  \item A \textbf{negative} rate of change: as the input increases, the output
        \ans{decreases}. The two quantities move \ans{opposite} ways.
  \item Every rate carries \textbf{units}: output units \textit{per}
        \ans{input unit}. ``$5$'' is not an answer; ``$5$ degrees per hour'' is.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Zero.} A hiker leaves the trailhead at 900 feet, climbs all afternoon,
and comes back down to the trailhead 4 hours later.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Her average rate of change over the whole afternoon is
        \ans{0} feet per hour.
  \item Did nothing happen? \ans{No.} \; Explain what the average threw
        away.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{She climbed and came back down; the average compares only the start and the end, which are the same elevation.}
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[22]
\begin{notesbox}{4. Chop It Finer}
The average over the whole climb called Ravi and Mira identical. Compute the
average rate of change over each \textbf{10-second split} instead.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
split (seconds) & 0 to 10 & 10 to 20 & 20 to 30 \\
\hline
Ravi's rate (steps/s) & \ans{4} & \ans{1.5} & \ans{0.5} \\
\hline
Mira's rate (steps/s) & \ans{2} & \ans{2} & \ans{2} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Ravi's rates are \ans{shrinking}. In Lesson 1.2's language, his climb is
        concave \ans{down}.
  \item Mira's rates \ans{never change}. Her climb is a straight line.
  \item The vote is settled --- but notice \textit{how}. Nothing new was
        measured. The same tool was used over \ans{smaller} intervals, and
        the story the whole-climb average erased came back.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[12]
\begin{notesbox}{5. The Pattern Names the Function}
Take the average rate of change over each unit interval from $x = 0$ to
$x = 4$ and read the list that comes out.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
\hline
$f(x) = 2x + 1$ & 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 & 9 \\
\hline
AROC of $f$ & --- & \ans{2} & \ans{2} & \ans{2} & \ans{2} \\
\hline
$g(x) = x^2$ & 0 & 1 & 4 & 9 & 16 \\
\hline
AROC of $g$ & --- & \ans{1} & \ans{3} & \ans{5} & \ans{7} \\
\hline
change in the AROC of $g$ & --- & --- & \ans{2} & \ans{2} & \ans{2} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=5pt]
  \item \textbf{Linear.} $f$'s average rate of change is \ans{the same} over
        every interval --- that single number is its \ans{slope}. Its rates
        change at a rate of \ans{0}.
  \item \textbf{Quadratic.} $g$'s rates are \textit{not} constant, but they
        climb by exactly \ans{2} every time. A quadratic's average rates
        of change are themselves \ans{linear}.
  \item \textbf{Concavity, now with a computation behind it.} Rates
        increasing $\Rightarrow$ concave \ans{up}; rates decreasing
        $\Rightarrow$ concave \ans{down}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item A delivery van's odometer is read every hour. $D(t)$ is the distance
        travelled, in miles, after $t$ hours.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        $t$ (hours) & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
        \hline
        $D(t)$ (miles) & 0 & 60 & 100 & 160 & 180 \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \smallskip

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item Find the average rate of change over $[0, 2]$.

                \begin{work}
                  \text{AROC} &= \frac{D(2) - D(0)}{2 - 0} \\
                              &= \frac{100 - 0}{2} \\
                              &= 50
                \end{work}

                Answer, with units: \ans{50 miles per hour}
          \item Over $[2, 4]$ the average rate of change is \ans{40 miles per hour}.
          \item Which two-hour stretch was faster, and by how much?

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{The first two hours, by 10 miles per hour.}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item One function's average rates of change over consecutive equal-length
        intervals are $7,\ 7,\ 7,\ 7$. Another's are $2,\ 6,\ 10,\ 14$.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item The \ans{first} function is linear, with slope \ans{7}.
          \item The other is concave \ans{up}, because its rates are
                \ans{increasing}.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
