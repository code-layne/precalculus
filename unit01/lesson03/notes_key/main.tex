\begin{objectivebox}
\textbf{LO 1.2.A:} Compare the rates of change at two points using average rates of change
near the points.\quad \ansline{AROC over small intervals approximates ROC at a point; compare by computing.}

\textbf{LO 1.2.B:} Describe how two quantities vary together at different points and over
different intervals of a function.\quad \ansline{Sign and magnitude of AROC describe co-variation direction and speed.}
\end{objectivebox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{vocabbox}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{average rate of change (AROC):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The ratio $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ — the constant rate that produces the same net change in output over the interval $[a,b]$; equals the slope of the secant line.}\\[2pt]

\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{rate of change at a point:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The instantaneous rate of change at $x=c$, approximated by computing AROC over successively smaller intervals containing $c$.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{hookbox}
\textit{You're driving on the highway. Your GPS says your average speed over the last
hour was 62 mph. Were you always going exactly 62 mph? What does ``average'' mean here?}

\medskip
The average speed is the \ans{one constant} speed that covers the \ans{same total} distance in the
\ans{same} time. This is the key idea behind average rate of change.
\end{hookbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Average Rate of Change}
The \textbf{average rate of change (AROC)} of a function $f$ over the interval $[a,b]$ is:

\[
    \text{AROC} = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}
    = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}
\]

\vspace{4pt}
Label: the numerator represents \ans{change in output ($\Delta f$)}; the denominator represents \ans{change in input ($\Delta x$)}.

\medskip
Geometrically, the AROC equals the slope of the \ans{secant} line through
$(a,\,f(a))$ and $(b,\,f(b))$.

\medskip\textbf{Worked Example.} The table gives values of a function $h$.

\vspace{4pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|}
\hline
\rowcolor{sky}
$x$    & 1  & 2  & 4  & 7 \\
\hline
$h(x)$ & 3  & 7  & 11 & 20 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{6pt}
Compute the AROC of $h$ over $[1,\,4]$:
\[
    \text{AROC} = \frac{h(4) - h(1)}{4 - 1}
    = \frac{11 - 3}{4 - 1}
    = \frac{8}{3} \approx \ans{2.67}
\]

Interpretation: for every 1-unit increase in $x$, $h$ increases by approximately
\ans{2.67}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Approximating Rate of Change at a Point}
The AROC over a \textbf{small} interval containing $x = c$ approximates the
\textbf{rate of change at the point} $x = c$.

\medskip
\textbf{Example.} Approximate the rate of change of $h$ at $x = 2$.

\vspace{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|C{3.8cm}|C{4.5cm}|C{3.8cm}|}
\hline
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Interval} & \textbf{AROC formula} & \textbf{AROC value} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.5]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.5)-h(2)}{2.5-2}$ & \ans{4} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.1]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.1)-h(2)}{2.1-2}$ & \ans{4} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.01]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.01)-h(2)}{2.01-2}$ & \ans{4} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{teachernote}
$h(2.5)=9$: AROC $=(9-7)/0.5=4$. $h(2.1)=7.4$: AROC $=0.4/0.1=4$. $h(2.01)=7.04$: AROC $=0.04/0.01=4$. All equal 4, consistent with $h$ having slope 4 near $x=2$.
\end{teachernote}

\vspace{4pt}
As the interval shrinks, the AROC appears to approach \ans{4}.
This value approximates the rate of change of $h$ \textbf{at} $x = 2$.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Sign of Rate of Change}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.46\linewidth}p{0.46\linewidth}@{}}
\textbf{Positive AROC:} & \textbf{Negative AROC:} \\[2pt]
As $x$ \ans{increases}, $f(x)$ \ans{increases}. & As $x$ \ans{increases}, $f(x)$ \ans{decreases}. \\[4pt]
The function is \ans{increasing} on the interval. & The function is \ans{decreasing} on the interval. \\[4pt]
\textit{Example:} \ans{temperature rising} & \textit{Example:} \ans{elevation decreasing} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{practicebox}
\textbf{1.} Compute the AROC over $[2,\,7]$:
$\dfrac{h(7)-h(2)}{7-2} = \dfrac{20-7}{5} = \dfrac{13}{5} =$ \ans{2.6}.
This is \ans{less} than AROC over $[1,4] \approx 2.67$. The function grew slightly faster
on $[1,4]$ than on $[2,7]$.

\writeline

\textbf{2.} \ans{Decreases.} A negative AROC means $f(b)-f(a) < 0$, so $f(b) < f(a)$ —
the output went down as the input went from 0 to 5.

\writeline
\end{practicebox}
