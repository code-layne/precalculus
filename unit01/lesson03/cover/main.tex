\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can compute the average rate of change of a function over an
        interval from a formula, a table, or a graph, and recognize it as the
        slope of a secant line.
  \item I can interpret the sign and units of a rate of change in context ---
        what a positive, negative, or zero rate says about two changing
        quantities.
  \item I can use the pattern of average rates of change to tell a linear
        function from a quadratic one, and to decide whether a graph is
        concave up or concave down.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
Last lesson we described change in words: increasing, decreasing, bending up
or down. Today change gets a \textbf{number} --- the average rate of change,
an upgraded slope that works on any function. Its sign tells the direction,
its size tells the speed, and its pattern will tell linear from quadratic.
\end{remindbox}
