\begin{notesbox}{Exit Ticket --- Key}

\textbf{1.} A function $f$ has the following values:
$f(2) = 10$, \quad $f(5) = 25$, \quad $f(5.1) = 25.3$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=6pt]
    \item Compute the AROC from $x = 2$ to $x = 5$.
    \[
        \text{AROC} = \frac{f(5) - f(2)}{5 - 2}
        = \frac{25 - 10}{3}
        = \frac{15}{3} = \ans{5}
    \]

    \item Approximate the rate of change at $x = 5$.
    \[
        \text{AROC on } [5,\;5.1] = \frac{25.3 - 25}{5.1 - 5}
        = \frac{0.3}{0.1} = \ans{3}
    \]
    The rate of change at $x = 5$ is approximately \ans{3}.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{10pt}

\textbf{2.} \ansline{As $x$ increases from 0 to 3, the output of $g$ \emph{decreases}. An AROC of $-4$ means the output drops by an average of 4 units for every 1-unit increase in $x$.}

\begin{teachernote}
For full credit, students should state (1) that $g$ decreases, and (2) connect the $-4$
to the rate (output drops 4 units per 1 unit of input). Partial credit for identifying
decrease without quantifying.
\end{teachernote}

\end{notesbox}
