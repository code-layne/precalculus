\begin{notesbox}{Average Rate of Change}
The \textbf{average rate of change (AROC)} of a function $f$ over the interval $[a,b]$ is:

\[
    \text{AROC} = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}
    = \frac{\hspace{2.2cm}}{\hspace{2.2cm}}
    = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}
\]

\vspace{4pt}
Label: the numerator represents \blank{5cm}; the denominator represents \blank{5cm}.

\medskip
Geometrically, the AROC equals the slope of the \blank{3.5cm} line through
$(a,\,f(a))$ and $(b,\,f(b))$.

\medskip\textbf{Worked Example.} The table gives values of a function $h$.

\vspace{4pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|C{1.5cm}|}
\hline
\rowcolor{sky}
$x$    & 1  & 2  & 4  & 7 \\
\hline
$h(x)$ & 3  & 7  & 11 & 20 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{6pt}
Compute the AROC of $h$ over $[1,\,4]$:
\[
    \text{AROC} = \frac{h(\phantom{x}) - h(\phantom{x})}{\phantom{x} - \phantom{x}}
    = \frac{\hspace{1cm} - \hspace{1cm}}{\hspace{1cm} - \hspace{1cm}}
    = \hspace{1.5cm}
\]

Interpretation: for every 1-unit increase in $x$, $h$ increases by approximately
\blank{2cm}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{Approximating Rate of Change at a Point}
The AROC over a \textbf{small} interval containing $x = c$ approximates the
\textbf{rate of change at the point} $x = c$.

\medskip
\textbf{Example.} Use successively smaller intervals to approximate the rate of change of
$h$ at $x = 2$, given additional values: $h(2) = 7$, $h(2.5) = 9$, $h(2.1) = 7.4$,
$h(2.01) = 7.04$.

\vspace{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|C{3.8cm}|C{4.5cm}|C{3.8cm}|}
\hline
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Interval} & \textbf{AROC formula} & \textbf{AROC value} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.5]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.5)-h(2)}{2.5-2}$ & \blank{2.5cm} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.1]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.1)-h(2)}{2.1-2}$ & \blank{2.5cm} \\
\hline
$[2,\;2.01]$ & $\dfrac{h(2.01)-h(2)}{2.01-2}$ & \blank{2.5cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{6pt}
As the interval shrinks, the AROC appears to approach \blank{2cm}.
This value approximates the rate of change of $h$ \textbf{at} $x = 2$.
\end{notesbox}
