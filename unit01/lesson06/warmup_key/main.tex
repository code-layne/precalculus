\item A polynomial has zeros at $x = -2$ (multiplicity 1), $x = 1$ (multiplicity 2), and
$x = 4$ (multiplicity 1).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=5pt]
  \item What is the degree of this polynomial? \ans{4}
  \item Write the polynomial in factored form with leading coefficient 1.

  \vspace{0.12in}
  $p(x) =$ \ans{$(x+2)(x-1)^2(x-4)$}
\end{enumerate}

\item True or False (circle one) and explain briefly:

\medskip
\textit{``A polynomial of degree 5 always has exactly 5 real zeros.''}

\medskip
\textbf{True} \quad / \quad \ans{False}

\vspace{0.1in}
\ansline{False. A degree-5 polynomial has exactly 5 zeros counting multiplicity and complex zeros,
but some zeros may be complex (non-real). It is possible to have as few as 1 real zero (with 2 pairs
of complex conjugate zeros). Multiplicity also means a repeated real zero counts more than once.}

\end{enumerate}
