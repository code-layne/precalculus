\begin{hookbox}
A nurse uses the formula $D(w) = 1.5w + 10$ to find a patient's medication dose $D$ (mg)
from their weight $w$ (kg).

\medskip
If a patient received 55 mg, what was their weight? \ans{30 kg}

\medskip
The formula that converts dose back to weight is called the \ans{inverse function} of $D$.
We write it as \ans{$D^{-1}$}.
\end{hookbox}

\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.8.A --- Inverse Functions from Tables (EK 2.8.A.1 \& 2.8.A.2)}
\textbf{Key Idea:} If $f(a) = b$, then $f^{-1}(b) = $ \ans{$a$}.
The inverse reverses every \ans{input-output} pair.

\medskip
\textbf{Step 1: Complete the output table for $f(x) = 4x - 1$.}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$    & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
\hline
$f(x)$ & \ans{3} & \ans{7} & \ans{11} & \ans{15} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Step 2: Fill in the inverse table by reversing every pair.}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$          & \ans{3} & \ans{7} & \ans{11} & \ans{15} \\
\hline
$f^{-1}(x)$  & \ans{1} & \ans{2} & \ans{3}  & \ans{4}  \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Invertible check:} Does $f$ map each input to a \ans{unique} output?
If any two inputs share the same output, the function is \textbf{not} invertible. \ans{Yes, $f$ is invertible.}

\medskip
Domain and range swap: the domain of $f^{-1}$ is the \ans{range} of $f$, and vice versa.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.04in}

\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.8.B --- Analytic Inverse Method (EK 2.8.B.4)}
To find $f^{-1}(x)$ for $f(x) = 4x - 1$:

\medskip
\textbf{Step 1:} Write $y = 4x - 1$.

\medskip
\textbf{Step 2:} Swap $x$ and $y$: \quad $x = $ \ans{$4y - 1$}

\medskip
\textbf{Step 3:} Solve for $y$:
\[
  x + 1 = 4y \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad y = \dfrac{x + 1}{4}
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Step 4:} Write $f^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\dfrac{x+1}{4}$}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.04in}

\begin{notesbox}{Composition Verification (EK 2.8.B.1)}
\textbf{Key property:} $f(f^{-1}(x)) = x$ and $f^{-1}(f(x)) = x$.

\medskip
\textbf{Verify $f(f^{-1}(x)) = x$ for $f(x) = 4x - 1$ and $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x+1}{4}$:}

\[
  f\!\left(f^{-1}(x)\right) = 4 \cdot \left({\color{keyred}\mathbf{\dfrac{x+1}{4}}}\right) - 1
  = {\color{keyred}\mathbf{(x+1) - 1}} = x \quad \checkmark
\]

\medskip
\textbf{Also verify $f^{-1}(f(x)) = x$:}

\[
  f^{-1}\!\left(f(x)\right) = \dfrac{({\color{keyred}\mathbf{4x-1}}) + 1}{4}
  = {\color{keyred}\mathbf{\dfrac{4x}{4}}} = x \quad \checkmark
\]

\medskip
Both compositions must equal $x$; if either fails, the inverse is \ans{incorrect}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.04in}

\begin{notesbox}{Domain Restriction (EK 2.8.A.1, EK 2.8.B.5)}
$f(x) = x^2$ is \textbf{not} invertible on all real numbers because:
$f(2) = $ \ans{4} and $f(-2) = $ \ans{4} --- two different inputs produce the same output.

\medskip
Restrict the domain to $x \ge 0$: then every output comes from exactly \ans{one} input.

\medskip
On the restricted domain $[0, \infty)$: $f^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\sqrt{x}$}

\medskip
The domain of $f^{-1}$ is \ans{$[0,\infty)$}; the range is \ans{$[0,\infty)$}.
\end{notesbox}
