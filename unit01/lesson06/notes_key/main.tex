\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Use $f^{-1}$ to give back the \ans{question} that produced a given
        \ans{answer}, and explain why $f^{-1}(x)$ is not
        \ans{$1/f(x)$}.
  \item Reverse a table of pairs to build $f^{-1}$, and say why the
        \ans{domain} and \ans{range} trade places.
  \item Decide whether a function is \ans{one-to-one}, and \ans{restrict} its
        domain when it is not.
  \item Find $f^{-1}(x)$ by swapping and solving, and check the pair with
        \ans{composition}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Inverse function:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The function $f^{-1}$ that gives back the question: if $f(a) = b$, then $f^{-1}(b) = a$. Read ``$f$-inverse.''}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{One-to-one:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{No two different questions share an answer. A function has an inverse exactly when it is one-to-one.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Domain restriction:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Throwing questions away until the function is one-to-one on what is left, so an inverse exists there.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Identity function:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{$I(x) = x$ --- hands back its own input. A function composed with its inverse gives it, in either order.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Reflection over $y = x$:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Every point $(a,b)$ on $f$ becomes $(b,a)$ on $f^{-1}$, so the two graphs are mirror images across $y = x$.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[22]
\begin{hookbox}
You find a \textbf{\$14 parking receipt} in each of two cars. Neither receipt
says how long the car was parked --- it only says what the driver paid.

\smallskip
\textbf{Garage A} charges $P(x) = 3x + 2$ dollars for $x$ hours.
\textbf{Garage B} posts this price list:

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
hours & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\hline
Garage B charges & \$8 & \$14 & \$14 & \$20 & \$26 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
For one of these cars you can say \textit{exactly} how long it was parked. For
the other you cannot. Which is which --- and what makes the difference?
\ansline{Garage A: \$14 could only have come from 4 hours, since $3(4) + 2 = 14$ and no other number of hours works.}
\ansline{Garage B charged \$14 for 2 hours \textit{and} for 3 hours, so its \$14 receipt does not say which.}
\end{hookbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[28]
\begin{notesbox}{1. Giving Back the Question}
Every time you use a function you ask it a \textbf{question} and it hands back
an \textbf{answer}. Today we run that trip in the other direction.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm]
  \draw[thick, plum, fill=lilac, rounded corners=2pt]
    (-3.4,-0.55) rectangle (-1.6,0.55);
  \node at (-2.5,0) {\large $a$};
  \draw[thick, plum, fill=goldbg, rounded corners=2pt]
    (1.6,-0.55) rectangle (3.4,0.55);
  \node at (2.5,0) {\large $b$};
  \draw[->, very thick, plum] (-1.5,0.32) -- (1.5,0.32);
  \node[above, font=\small] at (0,0.36) {$f$};
  \draw[->, very thick, goldacc] (1.5,-0.32) -- (-1.5,-0.32);
  \node[below, font=\small] at (0,-0.36) {$f^{-1}$};
  \node[below, font=\small\itshape, text=slate] at (-2.5,-0.62)
    {the question};
  \node[below, font=\small\itshape, text=slate] at (2.5,-0.62)
    {the answer};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=lilac, colframe=plum, boxrule=0.8pt, arc=2mm,
    left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, width=0.94\linewidth]
\textbf{The inverse gives back the question.} If $f(a) = b$ --- question $a$,
answer $b$ --- then $f^{-1}(b) = a$. Ask $f^{-1}$ an \textbf{answer} and it
tells you which \textbf{question} produced it.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

\smallskip
Read the garage rule $P(x) = 3x + 2$ both directions.

\medskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{0.34\linewidth} X @{}}
\hline
$P$: question $\to$ answer & $P^{-1}$: answer $\to$ question \\
\hline
$P(4) = 14$ & $P^{-1}(14) = $ \ans{4} \\
\hline
$P(1) = 5$  & $P^{-1}(5) = $ \ans{1} \\
\hline
$P(a) = b$  & $P^{-1}($ \ans{$b$} $) = $ \ans{$a$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\medskip
\textbf{Warning --- the biggest mistake in this lesson.} The $-1$ in $f^{-1}$
is a \textbf{name}, not an exponent:

\begin{center}
$f^{-1}(x) \ne \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$
\end{center}

The reciprocal does arithmetic \textit{to the answer}. The inverse goes back to
the \textit{question}. Those are not the same trip, and the numbers say so:

\smallskip
$P^{-1}(14) = $ \ans{4} \qquad but \qquad $P(14) = $ \ans{44},
so $\dfrac{1}{P(14)} = $ \ans{$1/44$}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{2. The Answers Become the Questions}
\textbf{Example 1.} Fill in what Garage A charges, using $P(x) = 3x + 2$.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ (hours) & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\hline
$P(x)$ (dollars) & \ans{5} & \ans{8} & \ans{11}
  & \ans{14} & \ans{17} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
Now build $P^{-1}$ by \textbf{trading the two rows}. Nothing is computed here.
The answers on the bottom row become the questions on the top row.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ (dollars) & 5 & 8 & 11 & 14 & 17 \\
\hline
$P^{-1}(x)$ (hours) & \ans{1} & \ans{2} & \ans{3}
  & \ans{4} & \ans{5} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
There is the \$14 receipt from Garage A: \ans{4} hours.

\medskip
Nothing here needs memorizing --- it follows from the trade. The questions you
may ask $P^{-1}$ are exactly the answers $P$ gave.

\smallskip
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Domain of $f^{-1}$ $=$ the \ans{range} of $f$. \quad
        Range of $f^{-1}$ $=$ the \ans{domain} of $f$.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{The same swap, drawn.} On a graph the questions live on the $x$-axis
and the answers live on the $y$-axis. Trading them turns each point $(a,b)$
into the point $(b,a)$ --- which is exactly a reflection across the line
$y = x$.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.44cm, y=0.44cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-5,-5) grid (5,5);
  \draw[->, thick] (-5.4,0) -- (5.7,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-5.4) -- (0,5.7) node[above] {\small $y$};
  \foreach \x in {-4,-2,2,4} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-4,-2,2,4} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[dashed, thick, slate] (-5,-5) -- (5,5);
  \node[font=\tiny, slate, above left] at (4.6,4.6) {$y = x$};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (-3,-5) -- (2,5);
  \draw[very thick, goldacc] (-5,-3) -- (5,2);
  \fill[plum] (0,1) circle (2.2pt);
  \fill[goldacc] (1,0) circle (2.2pt);
  \fill[plum] (2,5) circle (2.2pt);
  \fill[goldacc] (5,2) circle (2.2pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
{\small\textcolor{plum}{\textbf{plum}} $= f(x) = 2x + 1$ \qquad
 \textcolor{goldacc}{\textbf{gold}} $= f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x-1}{2}$ \qquad
 \textcolor{slate}{\textbf{dashed}} $= y = x$}

\smallskip
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $f$ answers the question $0$ with $1$, so $f^{-1}$ answers the question
        \ans{1} with \ans{0}: the point $(0,1)$ becomes
        $($ \ans{1} , \ans{0} $)$.
  \item The point $(2, 5)$ on $f$ becomes $($ \ans{5} ,
        \ans{2} $)$ on $f^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{3. When the Answer Gives Back Nothing}
Back to the other receipt. \textbf{Garage B} charged \$14 for a 2-hour stay
\textit{and} \$14 for a 3-hour stay.

\smallskip
\textbf{Example 2.} Try to answer the question the receipt asks.

\smallskip
$B^{-1}(14) = $ \ans{2} \; or \; \ans{3} --- and a function is
only allowed \ans{one} answer per question.

\smallskip
So $B$ has \textbf{no inverse}. Nothing is wrong with $B$ as a function; the
trouble only appears when you try to run it backwards.

\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=lilac, colframe=plum, boxrule=0.8pt, arc=2mm,
    left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, width=0.94\linewidth]
A function is \textbf{one-to-one} when no two different questions share an
answer. \textbf{A function has an inverse exactly when it is one-to-one} ---
that is what makes ``which question produced this answer?'' have a single
reply.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

\smallskip
You already met one today: on the warm-up, $q(1)$ and $q(3)$ were both 3, so
$q$ is \ans{not one-to-one}.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 3.} $f(x) = x^2$ on all real numbers.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$f(x) = x^2$ & 4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 4 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
The answer 4 came from the questions \ans{$-2$} and \ans{2}, so $f$ is
not one-to-one and has no inverse on all real numbers.

\medskip
\textbf{The repair: throw questions away.} Keep only $x \ge 0$. On that piece
no answer repeats, and the inverse exists:

\begin{center}
$f^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\sqrt{x}$} \qquad with domain \ans{$x \ge 0$}
\end{center}

\smallskip
Say it with the domain attached. ``$x^2$ has no inverse'' is false;
``$x^2$ has no inverse \ans{on all real numbers}'' is true.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{4. Swap and Solve}
A table only gives back the questions you already listed. This gets them all at
once.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item Write $y = f(x)$.
  \item \ans{Swap} $x$ and $y$. \quad \textit{(This is the whole inversion:
        the old answer becomes the new question.)}
  \item \ans{Solve} for $y$.
  \item Rename the result $f^{-1}(x)$.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\textbf{Example 4.} Find $P^{-1}(x)$ for the garage rule $P(x) = 3x + 2$.

\begin{work}
              y &= 3x + 2 \\
              x &= 3y + 2 \\
          x - 2 &= 3y \\
  \frac{x-2}{3} &= y
\end{work}

$P^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\dfrac{x-2}{3}$}

\smallskip
Test it on the receipt: $P^{-1}(14) = \dfrac{14 - 2}{3} = $ \ans{4}
hours, matching the table in Part 2.

\smallskip
Step 3 is the warm-up. There you solved $3x + 2 = 14$ and got one question
back. Swapping and solving does it for \ans{every possible} answer at once.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 5.} Find $f^{-1}(x)$ for $f(x) = 4x - 1$.

\begin{work}
              y &= 4x - 1 \\
              x &= 4y - 1 \\
          x + 1 &= 4y \\
  \frac{x+1}{4} &= y
\end{work}

$f^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\dfrac{x+1}{4}$}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{5. The Check: Hand the Answer Back}
Lesson 1.5 gave you the tool. In $f\big(f^{-1}(x)\big)$ the \textbf{handoff} is
whatever $f^{-1}$ produced --- and if $f^{-1}$ really does give back questions,
the number that comes out the far end is the one you started with.

\medskip
\begin{center}
$f$ and $f^{-1}$ are inverses exactly when \quad
$f\big(f^{-1}(x)\big) = f^{-1}\big(f(x)\big) = x$.
\end{center}

The function that hands back its own input, $I(x) = x$, is the
\ans{identity} function from Lesson 1.5. Composing a function with its inverse
--- in \textbf{either} order --- produces it.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 6.} Verify $P(x) = 3x + 2$ against $P^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x-2}{3}$.

\begin{work}
  P\big(P^{-1}(x)\big) &= 3\left(\frac{x-2}{3}\right) + 2 \\
                       &= (x - 2) + 2 \\
                       &= x \\
  P^{-1}\big(P(x)\big) &= \frac{(3x + 2) - 2}{3} \\
                       &= \frac{3x}{3} \\
                       &= x
\end{work}

Both compositions collapsed to \ans{$x$}, so $P$ and $P^{-1}$ really are
\ans{inverses}.

\medskip
\textbf{You already ran this check.} The warm-up handed you $f(x) = 3x+2$ and
$g(x) = \dfrac{x-2}{3}$ and asked for $f(g(11))$ and $g(f(3))$. Both came back
to the starting number, because that $g$ was \ans{$P^{-1}$} all along. The
algebra above does the same check for \ans{every input} at once, not just for 11
and 3.

\medskip
\textbf{Why check both directions, and not just one?}

\ansline{One direction alone can pass by accident on a restricted piece; inverses have to give back the question \textit{and} take it forward again.}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[16]
\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Let $f(x) = 5x - 3$. Find $f^{-1}(x)$, then check it by computing
        $f^{-1}\big(f(x)\big)$.

        \begin{work}
                      y &= 5x - 3 \\
                      x &= 5y - 3 \\
                  x + 3 &= 5y \\
          \frac{x+3}{5} &= y \\
          f^{-1}\big(f(x)\big) &= \frac{(5x - 3) + 3}{5} \\
                               &= \frac{5x}{5} \\
                               &= x
        \end{work}

        $f^{-1}(x) = $ \ans{$\dfrac{x+3}{5}$} \qquad
        $f^{-1}\big(f(x)\big) = $ \ans{$x$}

  \item A function $g$ satisfies $g(-4) = 7$.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $g^{-1}(7) = $ \ans{$-4$}
          \item In one sentence, say why $g^{-1}(7)$ is not $\dfrac{1}{7}$.

                \ansline{$g^{-1}$ gives back the question that produced 7, which was $-4$; $1/7$ is just arithmetic done to the answer.}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
