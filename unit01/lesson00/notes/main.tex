\begin{notesbox}{1. Quantities That Change Together}
This whole unit studies pairs of quantities where one \textbf{responds} to the
other. The quantity we choose or track is the \textbf{input}; the quantity that
responds is the \textbf{output}.

\medskip
Complete the table; the first row is done for you.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.40\linewidth}|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Situation} & \textbf{Input} & \textbf{Output} \\
\hline
Talking on a pay-per-minute phone plan & minutes talked & cost of the bill \\
\hline
A lit candle slowly burning down & \blank{2.4cm} & \blank{2.4cm} \\
\hline
A ball dropped from a rooftop & \blank{2.4cm} & \blank{2.4cm} \\
\hline
Pumping gas into a car & \blank{2.4cm} & \blank{2.4cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

The input is also called the \blank{2.8cm} variable, and the output the
\blank{2.8cm} variable. Ordered pairs are written ( \blank{1.4cm} ,
\blank{1.4cm} ).

\medskip
\textbf{The ``you-control'' test.} When two quantities are tangled together, ask:
\textit{which one do I choose or control?} That quantity is the
\blank{1.8cm}; the one that responds is the \blank{1.8cm}.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. What Is a Function? (First Look)}
A rule is a \textbf{function} when each input produces \blank{3cm} output ---
same input, same output, every time. (The notation arrives in Lesson 1.1.)

\medskip
Decide whether each rule is a function; the first row is done for you.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.58\linewidth}|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Function?} \\
\hline
each student $\to$ their student ID number & yes \\
\hline
each month $\to$ the students born that month & \blank{1.8cm} \\
\hline
each number $\to$ its double & \blank{1.8cm} \\
\hline
each shoe size $\to$ a student who wears that size & \blank{1.8cm} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\textbf{The fine print: many-to-one vs.\ one-to-many.}

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.44\linewidth}|p{0.44\linewidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{Allowed: many-to-one} & \textbf{Broken: one-to-many} \\
\hline
Two inputs \textbf{may share} an output. Both twins $\to$ the same birthday:
still a \blank{1.9cm}. &
One input with \textbf{two outputs}. Shoe size 9 $\to$ two different students:
\blank{1.6cm} a function. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

Notice which side of the pairing the rule controls: it restricts what each
\blank{1.8cm} may do, and says nothing at all about how often an
\blank{1.8cm} may repeat.

\medskip
In one sentence: two inputs may share an output, but one input may never have
\blank{3.4cm}.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. A Graph Tells a Story}
A cyclist rides away from home, rests, and rides back. The graph shows distance
from home as time passes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.65cm, y=0.5cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,0) grid (10,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (10.6,0) node[below right] {\small time (min)};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,6.6) node[above] {\small distance (blocks)};
  \foreach \x in {2,4,6,8,10} \node[below] at (\x,0) {\small \x};
  \foreach \y in {1,3,5} \node[left] at (0,\y) {\small \y};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (0,0) -- (4,5) node[midway, above left] {\small\bfseries A};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (4,5) -- (6,5) node[midway, above] {\small\bfseries B};
  \draw[very thick, plum] (6,5) -- (10,0) node[midway, above right] {\small\bfseries C};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Input quantity: \blank{2.6cm} \quad Output quantity: \blank{3.4cm}
  \item Segment A ($t=0$ to $4$): the distance is \blank{2.6cm} --- the rider is
        \blank{3.4cm}.
  \item Segment B ($t=4$ to $6$): the graph is \blank{2.6cm} --- the rider is
        \blank{3.4cm}.
  \item Segment C ($t=6$ to $10$): the distance is \blank{2.6cm} --- the rider is
        \blank{3.4cm}.
  \item At $t = 5$, the rider is \blank{1.8cm} blocks from home.
  \item A flat segment does \textbf{not} mean ``nothing is happening'': the input
        (time) keeps moving; it is the \blank{2.2cm} that holds steady.
  \item Each time has exactly one distance, so distance \blank{1.2cm} a function
        of time.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{4. A Table Tells the Same Story}
A candle is lit and measured each hour.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
Hours burning & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
\hline
Height (cm) & 20 & 17 & 14 & 14 & 11 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Input quantity: \blank{2.6cm} \quad Output quantity: \blank{3.0cm}
  \item From hour 0 to hour 2, the height is \blank{2.6cm}; from hour 2 to
        hour 3 it is \blank{2.6cm} --- perhaps the candle was blown out and
        relit.
  \item That flat stretch is this table's version of segment \blank{1.2cm} on
        the bike-ride graph.
  \item Read a graph \textit{or} a table the same way: watch what the
        \blank{1.8cm} does as the \blank{1.8cm} moves forward.
  \item Each hour has exactly one height, so the height \blank{1.2cm} a
        function of the hours burning.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}
