\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
Evaluate algebraic expressions at given input values, respecting the
order of operations. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Substituting a negative value requires parentheses: at $x=-2$,
        $x^2$ means $(-2)^2 = 4$, not $-4$.
  \item An expression has a \textit{value}; an equation has a \textit{solution}.
\end{itemize} \\
Solve linear equations and inequalities; express solution sets in
interval notation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Undo operations in reverse order; whatever is done to one side
        is done to the other.
  \item Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number
        reverses the inequality symbol.
  \item Interval notation is the course's default language for sets of
        numbers: $x < -3$ is written $(-\infty, -3)$.
\end{itemize} \\
Read and interpret points in the coordinate plane. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item An ordered pair $(x, y)$ lists input first, output second.
  \item The signs of the coordinates locate the quadrant.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries\color{navy}}p{0.22\linewidth} X @{}}
variable           & A letter standing for a number that can change or is unknown. \\
expression         & A combination of numbers, variables, and operations; it has a value but no equals sign. \\
equation           & A statement that two expressions are equal; it can be solved. \\
solution           & A value of the variable that makes an equation or inequality true. \\
inequality         & A statement comparing two expressions with $<$, $>$, $\le$, or $\ge$. \\
interval notation  & A way to write a set of numbers using endpoints, e.g.\ $(-2, 5]$. \\
ordered pair       & A point $(x, y)$ in the coordinate plane: input first, output second. \\
quadrant           & One of the four regions of the coordinate plane cut out by the axes. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{sky}
    Put the monthly cell-phone cost $C = 25 + 0.10t$ on the board ($t$ = minutes of
    talk time). \textbf{Three rapid-fire questions, no calculators:}
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item What does a 200-minute month cost? \textit{(evaluate)}
        \item A bill came to \$40 --- how many minutes was that? \textit{(solve)}
        \item Which months cost under \$30? \textit{(inequality)}
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} All three questions use the same formula --- what changed
    was whether we were \textit{plugging in} or \textit{working backward}. That
    distinction (evaluate vs.\ solve) is the spine of the whole lesson.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{sky}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{Part 1: Evaluating Expressions} (12 min)

Anchor on the substitution-with-parentheses habit:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Evaluate $3x^2 - 2x + 1$ at $x = -2$ \textit{with} parentheses on the board:
        $3(-2)^2 - 2(-2) + 1 = 17$.
  \item Common wrong answers are $-11$ and $9$ --- both come from dropping the
        parentheses on $(-2)^2$ or mishandling $-2(-2)$.
  \item Name the rule: exponents before multiplication; substitute inside
        parentheses \textit{every} time.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Part 2: Solving Equations \& Inequalities} (15 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item Solve $4x - 7 = 13$ --- narrate ``undo in reverse order'': add 7, divide by 4.
  \item Solve $5 - 2x > 11$ --- the divide-by-$(-2)$ step \textbf{flips} the symbol:
        $x < -3$.
  \item Translate the answer to interval notation: $(-\infty, -3)$. Establish
        interval notation as the course default now, before Lesson 1.1 needs it for
        domains and intervals of increase.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 3: The Coordinate Plane} (10 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item Ordered pair $(x, y)$: input first, output second --- this becomes
        (input, output) for functions starting in Lesson 1.1.
  \item Quadrant sign patterns: I $(+,+)$, II $(-,+)$, III $(-,-)$, IV $(+,-)$;
        points on an axis are in no quadrant.
  \item Fill in a point-to-quadrant table together --- students classify given
        points; no plotting from scratch.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why this lesson exists.} This is a non-honors course opener with no CED
topic. Every skill here is used within two weeks: evaluation feeds function
notation (1.1--1.2), inequalities and interval notation feed domains and
increasing/decreasing intervals (1.1), and coordinate reading feeds every graph
in the course. Treat the day as diagnosis as much as review --- the exit ticket
tells you who needs intervention before Lesson 1.1.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
While students work on guided notes and practice, circulate and check:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} substituting $-2$ into $x^2$ without parentheses and
        getting $-4$. Cold-call: ``What is $(-3)^2$? What is $-3^2$? Why are they different?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} forgetting to flip the inequality when dividing by a
        negative. Ask: ``Is $x = -10$ a solution of $5 - 2x > 11$? Check it against your answer.''
  \item \textbf{Notation check:} interval notation direction --- smaller endpoint always
        first; $\infty$ always gets a parenthesis, never a bracket.
  \item \textbf{Cold-call:} ``Give me a point in Quadrant III. Now give me a point that is in
        \textit{no} quadrant.''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
Students work on the \textbf{Group Activity: Car-Wash Fundraiser} in leveled tiers.
Every tier uses the same profit formula $P = 8c - 40$ ($c$ = cars washed).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Evaluate $P$ for given values of $c$ by completing
        a table, then solve $8c - 40 = 0$ to find the break-even point.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Solve $8c - 40 = 120$ for a profit goal, solve the
        inequality $8c - 40 \ge 200$, and write the answer in interval notation with a
        sentence interpreting it.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Compare against a rival club's plan $P = 6c + 10$:
        solve $8c - 40 = 6c + 10$ to find where the plans tie, and explain which plan
        earns more for larger numbers of cars and why.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (last 5--7 minutes, individual, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Evaluate $2x^2 - 5x$ at $x = -1$.
  \item Solve $3(x - 4) = 21$.
  \item Write the inequality $x > -2$ in interval notation.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} This exit ticket is a diagnostic. Sort collected tickets into
three piles (0--1 correct, 2 correct, 3 correct) and use the piles to seed Tier R
membership for Lesson 1.1. Watch for the $(-1)^2$ sign error on item 1 --- it predicts
trouble with every evaluation in Unit 1.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} 5 problems covering evaluation at a negative input, two-step and
distributed linear equations, an inequality answered in interval notation, quadrant
classification from a table, and one SOL-style multiple-choice item.

\textbf{Extension (optional):} Solve the literal equation $P = 2\ell + 2w$ for $w$ ---
rearranging formulas is a quiet calculus-readiness skill.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson we give the input--output relationship a name ---
\textit{function} --- and study how two quantities change \textit{in tandem}.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. All four items are Algebra 1 level on
purpose --- a student stuck here needs a seat near the front during Part 1. Item 4
(quadrant naming) is the fastest to grade at a glance.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The three worked examples (evaluate, solve, inequality) are in \textit{work} blocks:
students get blank work space, the key shows the steps. Insist on the parentheses
habit in Example 1 even from students who get 17 without them --- the habit is what
transfers to $f(-2)$ notation next lesson. The vocabulary box is light; move through
it quickly and spend the time in Parts 1--2.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Group Activity]
Assign tiers from what you saw during notes, not from last year's grades. Tier R
groups should reach the break-even question --- it is the payoff. If a Tier E pair
finishes early, ask them to find a value of $c$ where the rival plan is better and
defend it with a computation.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Collected and sorted, not graded for accuracy --- completion credit only (``mistakes
happen, blanks don't''). The three-pile sort described in the Assessment box takes
under five minutes and is the whole point of the day.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
Problem 5 is the first SOL-style multiple-choice item of the year --- tell students
to expect one on most homeworks. The extension (solving $P = 2\ell + 2w$ for $w$) is
genuinely optional; do not chase completion on it.
\end{teachernote}
