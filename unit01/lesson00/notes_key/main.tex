\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Identify the \ans{input} and \ans{output} quantities in a real-world
        relationship and decide which depends on the other.
  \item Explain what makes a relationship a function: each input produces
        \ans{exactly one} output.
  \item Read a graph and describe where it is \ans{increasing}, \ans{decreasing},
        or constant.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{quantity:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Something measurable that can change: time, height, cost, temperature.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{input:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The quantity we choose or track; listed first in an ordered pair.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{output:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The quantity that responds to the input; listed second in an ordered pair.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{independent variable:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Another name for the input quantity.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{dependent variable:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Another name for the output quantity --- its value depends on the input.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{navy}{function (informal):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A rule that sends each input to exactly one output.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{hookbox}
A vending machine and a claw machine both take your money. Press B4 on the vending
machine and you get the same bag of chips every single time; drop the claw
and you get\ldots{} who knows.

Which machine can you \textit{trust}, and what does that have to do with math?
\ansline{The vending machine: the same input (button) always gives the same output (snack). A rule that behaves this dependably --- one output for each input --- is what math calls a function.}
\end{hookbox}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{1. Quantities That Change Together}
This whole unit studies pairs of quantities where one \textbf{responds} to the
other. The quantity we choose or track is the \textbf{input}; the quantity that
responds is the \textbf{output}.

\medskip
Complete the table; the first row is done for you.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.40\linewidth}|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Situation} & \textbf{Input} & \textbf{Output} \\
\hline
Talking on a pay-per-minute phone plan & minutes talked & cost of the bill \\
\hline
A lit candle slowly burning down & \ans{hours burning} & \ans{candle height} \\
\hline
A ball dropped from a rooftop & \ans{time since drop} & \ans{height of ball} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

The input is also called the \ans{independent} variable, and the output the
\ans{dependent} variable. Ordered pairs are written ( \ans{input} ,
\ans{output} ).
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{notesbox}{2. What Is a Function? (First Look)}
A rule is a \textbf{function} when each input produces \ans{exactly one} output ---
same input, same output, every time. (The notation arrives in Lesson 1.1.)

\medskip
Decide whether each rule is a function; the first row is done for you.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.58\linewidth}|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Function?} \\
\hline
each student $\to$ their student ID number & yes \\
\hline
each month $\to$ the students born that month & \ans{no} \\
\hline
each number $\to$ its double & \ans{yes} \\
\hline
each shoe size $\to$ a student who wears that size & \ans{no} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

\textbf{The fine print:} two inputs may share an output, but one input may never
have \ans{two (or more) outputs}.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{3. A Graph Tells a Story}
A cyclist rides away from home, rests, and rides back. The graph shows distance
from home as time passes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.65cm, y=0.5cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (0,0) grid (10,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (10.6,0) node[below right] {\small time (min)};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,6.6) node[above] {\small distance (blocks)};
  \foreach \x in {2,4,6,8,10} \node[below] at (\x,0) {\small \x};
  \foreach \y in {1,3,5} \node[left] at (0,\y) {\small \y};
  \draw[very thick, navy] (0,0) -- (4,5) node[midway, above left] {\small\bfseries A};
  \draw[very thick, navy] (4,5) -- (6,5) node[midway, above] {\small\bfseries B};
  \draw[very thick, navy] (6,5) -- (10,0) node[midway, above right] {\small\bfseries C};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item Input quantity: \ans{time} \quad Output quantity: \ans{distance from home}
  \item Segment A ($t=0$ to $4$): the distance is \ans{increasing} --- the rider is
        \ans{riding away from home}.
  \item Segment B ($t=4$ to $6$): the graph is \ans{constant (flat)} --- the rider is
        \ans{resting}.
  \item Segment C ($t=6$ to $10$): the distance is \ans{decreasing} --- the rider is
        \ans{returning home}.
  \item At $t = 5$, the rider is \ans{5} blocks from home.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item A movie theater tracks the number of tickets sold and the money collected
        each night.

        Input: \ans{tickets sold} \quad Output: \ans{money collected}

        Is the money collected a function of the tickets sold? Explain.

        \vspace{0.05in}
        \ansline{Yes --- each number of tickets sold produces exactly one total amount of money collected.}

  \item The table shows quiz scores after different amounts of studying.

        \smallskip
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
        \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
        \hline
        Hours studied & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
        \hline
        Quiz score & 60 & 70 & 80 & 80 \\
        \hline
        \end{tabular}
        \smallskip

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item From 0 to 2 hours, the score \ans{increases}; from 2 to 3 hours it is
                \ans{constant}.
          \item Inputs 2 and 3 share the output 80. Is the score still a function
                of hours studied? Explain.

                \vspace{0.05in}
                \ansline{Yes --- each input still has exactly one output. Two inputs sharing an output is allowed.}
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
