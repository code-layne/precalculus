\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=lilac, colframe=plum, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will read every transformation rule
    $g(x) = af(x+h)+k$ as interference at one of two places --- the
    \textit{question} handed to the parent function or the \textit{answer} it
    hands back; construct and describe additive and multiplicative
    transformations from that reading, explaining the horizontal reversal by
    solving for the input rather than by slogan; follow a landmark point
    through a combination of moves; and determine the domain and range of the
    transformed function from the two columns.
    \hfill\textit{\small (CED Topic 1.12 --- Skills 1.C, 3.A)}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{AP Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{1.C} --- Construct new functions, using transformations, that may be
useful in modeling contexts, criteria, or data. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Using $f$ is a transaction: a rule can change the \textbf{question}
        handed to $f$ or the \textbf{answer} $f$ returns, and nothing else.
  \item $g(x)=f(x)+k$ is an \textbf{additive} transformation of the answer: a
        vertical translation by $k$ units. (1.12.A.1)
  \item $g(x)=f(x+h)$ is an \textbf{additive} transformation of the question:
        a horizontal translation by $-h$ units --- the direction reverses
        because you solve $x+h=p$ for the input. (1.12.A.2)
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{3.A} --- Describe the characteristics of a function with varying levels
of precision, depending on the representation available. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $g(x)=af(x)$ is a \textbf{multiplicative} transformation of the
        answer: a vertical dilation by a factor of $|a|$; if $a<0$ it also
        reflects over the $x$-axis. (1.12.A.3)
  \item $g(x)=f(bx)$ is a \textbf{multiplicative} transformation of the
        question: a horizontal dilation by a factor of $1/|b|$; if $b<0$ it
        also reflects over the $y$-axis. (1.12.A.4)
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{Synthesis} --- combine transformations and read off the consequences. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Additive and multiplicative transformations combine as
        $g(x)=af(x+h)+k$, and the parent's point $(p,q)$ lands at
        $(p-h,\;aq+k)$ --- two independent computations, so no order of
        operations has to be memorized. (1.12.A.5)
  \item The domain is a fact about the input column and the range a fact
        about the output column, so question changes move the domain and
        answer changes move the range. (1.12.A.6)
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.27\linewidth} X @{}}
Parent function & The simplest function of a family --- the one every other graph in the family is built from. Today it is $f(x)=x^2$. \\
Transformation  & A rule that changes the question handed to a function, the answer it hands back, or both. \\
Translation     & A slide with no resizing: $f(x)+k$ moves the graph vertically by $k$; $f(x+h)$ moves it horizontally by $-h$. \\
Dilation        & A resize: $af(x)$ scales the outputs by $|a|$ (vertical); $f(bx)$ scales the inputs by \TallMath{\frac{1}{|b|}} (horizontal). \\
Reflection      & A flip: $-f(x)$ over the $x$-axis (an answer change); $f(-x)$ over the $y$-axis (a question change). \\
Landmark point  & A point of the parent worth tracking --- a vertex, an endpoint, an intercept. Under $g(x)=af(x+h)+k$ it lands at $(p-h,\;aq+k)$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: evaluating $f(x)=x^2$ at three inputs with the
    parentheses habit from 1.1, producing the exact reference values the
    parent table uses all period; solving three one-step equations; and
    reading domain, range, and the location of the smallest output off a
    table. Item 2 is load-bearing --- ``for what $x$ is $x+3=0$?'' and ``for
    what $x$ is $2x=6$?'' are the entire explanation of the horizontal
    reversal and of the horizontal squeeze, and both return verbatim in
    Part 3.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{lilac}
    \textbf{Two harbors, three hours apart.} Project the North Harbor tide
    table from the guided notes. South Harbor rises and falls exactly the same
    way, and the harbormaster publishes its depth as $S(t) = D(t+3)$.
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item Take a vote first: the rule \textit{adds} three hours, so does
              South Harbor's high tide come \textbf{before} or \textbf{after}
              North Harbor's? Do not let anyone justify it yet.
        \item Then compute two values off the table. $S(3) = D(6) = 12$ --- so
              South Harbor peaks at 3 a.m., three hours \textit{ahead}. The
              plus sign moved it earlier.
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} the $+3$ was never done to the tide. It was
    done to the \textbf{question}. Asking $S$ about 3 a.m.\ means asking $D$
    about 6 a.m., so everything $D$ does, $S$ already did. That is the whole
    lesson: a rule interferes either with the question you hand a function or
    with the answer it hands back, and those two places behave differently.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{lilac}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{Part 1: The Question and the Answer} (7 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item Name the \textbf{parent function} $f(x)=x^2$ and put its five
        reference points on the board: $(-2,4)$, $(-1,1)$, $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$,
        $(2,4)$. Everything today is that parabola, relocated or resized.
  \item Say the spine once, in these words, and repeat it verbatim all period:
        \textbf{using $f$ is a transaction --- you hand it a question, it
        hands back an answer. A rule can only interfere in one of those two
        places.} Outside the function is the answer: vertical, and it does
        what it says. Inside the parentheses is the question: horizontal, and
        it runs opposite.
  \item Do the four-row sorting table before computing anything. Insist on the
        sort out loud: ``question or answer?'' then ``horizontal or
        vertical?''
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Part 2: Rewriting the Answer} (10 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item Fill the three rows $f(x)+3$, $2f(x)$, $-f(x)$ from the parent row.
        The input column is never touched --- point at it.
  \item $k>0$ up, $k<0$ down. $|a|>1$ stretches, $0<|a|<1$ compresses; say
        ``stretched away from the $x$-axis,'' not ``bigger.'' $a<0$ carries a
        flip over the $x$-axis.
  \item Read the pre-drawn figure with all three curves, then close: the
        questions were untouched, so the domain is unchanged and the range
        moves.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 3: Rewriting the Question} (14 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item The hard one --- budget the time. Fill the ``handed to $f$'' row
        \textit{first}, every time; that row is the whole method.
  \item For $g(x)=f(x+3)$, let students discover the output list is unchanged.
        Then locate the vertex by solving $x+3=0$ --- the warm-up's own
        question --- and tie it back to the harbor with the sentence
        ``everything $D$ does, $S$ did three hours earlier.''
  \item $f(2x)$ at exposure depth (1.12.A.4): to hand $f$ its $2$ you supply
        $x=1$, so every landmark arrives at half the $x$ --- a squeeze by
        $1/2$, the opposite of what the $2$ suggests. Name $f(-x)$ as the
        $y$-axis flip and move on.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Part 4: Follow the Landmark} (12 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item State the tool: for $g(x)=af(x+h)+k$, the parent's $(p,q)$ lands at
        $(p-h,\,aq+k)$. New input: solve $x+h=p$. New output: do what the
        outside says. Two separate computations --- there is no order to
        memorize.
  \item Work $g(x)=-2f(x+1)-3$ across three parent points in the table, then
        say the moves in words. Finish on the vertex: the lowest point is now
        the highest, because $a<0$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Part 5: Domain and Range} (9 min)

\begin{itemize}
  \item Domain is a fact about the input column; range is a fact about the
        output column. That single sentence is the whole part.
  \item Same $g$: domain $[-2,4] \to [-3,3]$ by solving the inequality; range
        $[0,9] \to [-21,-3]$, endpoints trading places because $a<0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
While students work on the guided notes and practice, circulate and check:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} shifting $f(x+3)$ to the \textit{right}.
        Cold-call: ``What does the rule hand $f$ when $x=-3$? And what did $f$
        do when it was handed that?'' Do not accept ``because it's
        backwards.''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} in the table for $2f(x)$, doubling the $x$
        column. Ask the sorting question: ``Is that 2 changing the question or
        the answer? So which column can it touch?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} a range under a negative factor left in the old
        order, e.g.\ $[-3,-21]$. ``Which of those two \textit{is} smaller?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} moving the domain for a vertical change, or the
        range for a horizontal one. Send them back to the two columns.
  \item \textbf{Spine check:} when a student explains a horizontal move by
        saying ``you flip the sign,'' stop the group and make them solve
        $x+h=p$ instead. The slogan is what fails them on $f(2x)$ and on
        anything in 1.5.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
Students work on the \textbf{Group Activity: The Fountain Deck} in leveled
tiers. Every tier uses the same arc table and the same pre-drawn parabola ---
no tier draws anything. The physical setup carries the spine: moving a nozzle
along the deck is a question change; raising it on a pedestal is an answer
change.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Read $f(0)$, $f(4)$, and the high point
        off the table; fill the rows for the pedestal nozzle $P(x)=f(x)+3$ and
        the doubled-height nozzle $Q(x)=2f(x)$; sort the pedestal as a
        question or answer change; then say why both high points are still at
        $x=4$.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Build the ``handed to $f$'' row for the
        relocated nozzle $B(x)=f(x-4)$, notice the height list is unchanged,
        and explain why a move to the \textit{right} is written with a
        \textit{minus}. Then follow three landmarks through
        $C(x)=f(x-4)+3$ and state its domain $[4,12]$ and range $[3,19]$.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Reverse-engineer $M(x)=f(x+3)$ from a table;
        build the reflecting-pool row $R(x)=-f(x)$ and locate the low point of
        $-f(x-4)$; then compare the scale model $f(2x)$ against the
        double-pressure spec $2f(x)$ at the same inputs --- they agree only at
        $x=0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (last 5--7 minutes, individual, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For $g(x) = f(x+5)-2$: name the question change with its direction,
        the answer change with its direction, and then land the parent's
        lowest point $(0,0)$.
  \item Complete a table row for $g(x) = -f(x)+1$ from four given values of
        $f$.
  \item Given domain $[-2,6]$ and range $[0,4]$ for $f$, state the domain
        \textit{and} the range of $g(x) = f(x-4)$, with a one-phrase reason
        for the range.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} item 1(c) is the diagnostic --- a landing point of
$(5,-2)$ is the lesson's signature error and means Part 3 needs a reteach in
tomorrow's warm-up window. Item 3(b) is the second diagnostic and a different
miss: a student who changes the range under a horizontal move has the
direction right and the two columns confused. Item 2 is arithmetic and should
be near-universal.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} 5 problems --- sorting and describing four single
transformations, two transformation tables where the horizontal one reproduces
$f$'s output list unchanged, reading a pre-drawn parent-and-image pair and
writing the rule, a full landmark-plus-domain-plus-range pass through
$g(x)=2f(x+1)-5$, and one AP-style multiple-choice range item under a negative
dilation.

\textbf{Extension (optional):} $g(x)=2f(x)+6$ against $h(x)=2(f(x)+6)$ with
$f(3)=5$. The two differ ($16$ versus $22$), so the order in which the outside
operations act is part of the rule --- an idea 1.5 will formalize.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson (1.5, Composition of Functions) picks up exactly
what made every horizontal move work: handing $f$ something other than $x$.
1.5 gives that its name and notation and lets the thing handed over be any
function, not just $x+h$.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Item 2 is the one that pays off:
``for what $x$ is $x+3=0$?'' is a throwaway algebra question here and the
entire explanation of the horizontal reversal in Part 3, and $2x=6$ does the
same job for the horizontal squeeze --- so when you reach them, say out loud
that the class already answered both. Item 1 produces the exact parent values
($4$, $1$, $9$) the notes tables run on. Item 3(c) asks where the smallest
output happens, not just what it is: that is landmark language, and Part 4
needs students already used to naming a point rather than a number.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
Take the hook vote before any computing --- if the class does not commit
first, ``it moved earlier'' lands as a table fact instead of a provocation.
Say ``question'' and ``answer'' every time you say ``inside'' and ``outside''
for the whole period; those two words are doing all the conceptual work, and
they are what make Part 4 need no order-of-operations rule at all. In Part 3
fill the ``handed to $f$'' row first every single time, including on the board
--- students who skip it are the ones who shift the wrong way. Part 3(c),
horizontal dilation, is exposure only (CED 1.12.A.4): the table showing
$f(2x)$ hitting the same heights at half the $x$ is enough, and no student
needs to produce one from scratch today. If time runs short, compress 3(c)
into a single read --- do not cut Part 4, which both the exit ticket and the
activity's Tier A depend on.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Group Activity]
All three tiers read the same arc table and the same parabola, so groups can
be re-formed mid-period with no new setup. The fountain is chosen so the
spine is physical: sliding a nozzle along the deck changes the question,
putting it on a pedestal changes the answer, and students can point at which
one happened. Tier R's item 4 --- why are both high points still at $x=4$? ---
is that tier's whole point; make them say it aloud. Tier A's item 2 is the
hinge of the lesson: the nozzle moved \textit{right} and the rule says
\textit{minus}. Give it a whole-class share in the closing minutes even if no
other tier work gets aired. Tier E's $f(2x)$ versus $2f(x)$ table is the best
second share if you have three more minutes; the two rules agree at exactly
one input, and seeing that is worth more than any restatement of the rule.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Collected, completion credit only. Sort by item 1(c): $(-5,-2)$ is the target
and the $(5,-2)$ pile is your reteach group --- read that one entry first, it
is the fastest scan of the three. Accept any wording in 1(a) and 1(b) that
names both a direction and a number of units; do not accept ``moves 5'' with
no direction. Item 3(b) is a separate diagnostic from 1(c) and should be
tallied separately: a student who wrote a moved range under a horizontal
transformation did not misread the direction, they lost the two columns, and
the fix is Part 5 rather than Part 3. A correct $[0,4]$ with an empty reason
still gets credit, but note the name.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
Problem 2 is deliberately paired: the vertical row changes the numbers, the
horizontal row reproduces $f$'s output list exactly, and the follow-up
question asks which one and why. Scan that sentence first --- it is the
cheapest read on whether the spine landed. Problem 3 is the only
graph-to-rule item and the best predictor of success in 1.7's modeling work.
On problem 5, distractor A is the range computed with $a=+2$ instead of $-2$,
C drops the $+3$, and D is the correct pair of endpoints left in the wrong
order after multiplying by a negative --- D in particular is worth thirty
seconds of autopsy, since it is the Part 5 warning made into a wrong answer.
The extension is the only place the order of two outside operations is
examined; if the class handled Part 4 quickly, assign it and open 1.5 with a
student's answer.
\end{teachernote}
