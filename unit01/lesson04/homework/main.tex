\item The highest point on the graph of $f$ is $(2, 7)$. The domain of $f$ is
      $[-4, 2]$ and its range is $[-1, 7]$. Let $g(x) = 2f(x + 1) - 5$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=6pt]
  \item Follow the landmark $(2, 7)$.

        \begin{work}
          \text{new input} &= 2 - 1 = 1 \\
          \text{new output} &= 2(7) - 5 = 9
        \end{work}

        It lands at ( \blank{1.4cm} , \blank{1.4cm} ).

  \item Domain of $g$:

        \begin{work}
          -4 &\le x + 1 \le 2 \\
          -5 &\le x \le 1
        \end{work}

        Domain of $g$: \blank{3cm}

  \item Range of $g$:

        \begin{work}
          2(-1) - 5 &= -7 \\
           2(7) - 5 &= 9
        \end{work}

        Range of $g$: \blank{3cm}

  \item Which piece of the rule moved the domain, and which moved the range?

        \vspace{0.05in}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{extensionbox}
A student writes both of these and says they are the same function:
\[
  g(x) = 2f(x) + 6 \qquad\qquad h(x) = 2\bigl(f(x) + 6\bigr)
\]
Suppose $f(3) = 5$. Compute $g(3)$ and $h(3)$, then say in one sentence what
each rule does to the answer $f$ hands back, and in what order.

\begin{notesbox}{Preview of Lesson 1.5}
Every horizontal move today worked by handing $f$ something other than $x$ ---
$f(x+3)$ handed it $x+3$, and $f(2x)$ handed it $2x$. Lesson 1.5,
\textit{Composition of Functions}, gives that move its name and its notation,
and lets the thing you hand over be \textit{any} function, not just $x + h$.
