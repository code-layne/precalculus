\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\ldots
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Tell a change made \ans{outside} a function from a change made
        \ans{inside} it, and predict which way the graph moves.
  \item Build a table and describe the graph of $g(x) = af(x+h)+k$ using
        \ans{translations} (shifts) and \ans{dilations} (stretches and flips).
  \item Find the \ans{domain} and \ans{range} of a transformed function from
        those of its parent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Parent function:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The plain, untransformed function a whole family of graphs is built from --- here, $f(x)=x^2$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Transformation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{An operation that moves, resizes, or flips a graph without changing its basic shape.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Translation (shift):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A slide: $f(x)+k$ moves the graph up or down; $f(x+h)$ moves it left or right.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Dilation (stretch or compress):}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A resize: $af(x)$ scales the outputs by $|a|$; $f(bx)$ scales the inputs by $1/|b|$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Reflection:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A flip: $-f(x)$ flips the graph over the $x$-axis; $f(-x)$ flips it over the $y$-axis.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{hookbox}
Here is $f(x) = x^2$, and three tables built from it. One has every output 3
larger. One has every output doubled. And one has the \textit{exact same
outputs} as $f$ --- just at different inputs.

\smallskip
What could I have done to $f$ that leaves every output untouched but still
moves the graph?

\ansline{Change the inputs, not the outputs --- feed $f$ a different number than the $x$ we started with.}
\ansline{The whole graph slides sideways: same heights, new addresses.}
\end{hookbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{1. The Parent Function and the Outside/Inside Rule}
A \textbf{parent function} is the plain, untransformed function a whole family
of graphs is built from. Today's parent is $f(x) = x^2$, and these five points
are our reference frame:

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$f(x) = x^2$ & 4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 4 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every rule today changes $f$ in exactly one of two places, and \textit{where}
it changes decides everything:

\medskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{0.94\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{0.30\linewidth} X @{}}
\hline
Outside $f$ & like $f(x) + 3$ or $2f(x)$: it changes the
  \ans{outputs}, so the graph moves \ans{vertically} --- and it does
  \ans{exactly what} you expect. \\
\hline
Inside $f$ & like $f(x+3)$ or $f(2x)$: it changes the
  \ans{inputs}, so the graph moves \ans{horizontally} --- and it does the
  \ans{opposite} of what you expect. \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\medskip
Before you compute anything with a new rule, say out loud: \textit{is this
change outside $f$, or inside $f$?}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{2. Vertical Translation: $g(x) = f(x) + k$}
The $+k$ sits \textbf{outside} $f$, so it changes every output. The graph
slides \textbf{up} when $k > 0$ and \textbf{down} when $k < 0$, by $|k|$
units.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 1.} $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = f(x) + 3$. Complete the table.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$f(x)$ & 4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 4 \\
\hline
$g(x) = f(x) + 3$ & \ans{7} & \ans{4} & \ans{3}
  & \ans{4} & \ans{7} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.52cm, y=0.34cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-3,0) grid (3,8);
  \draw[->, thick] (-3.3,0) -- (3.5,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,8.6) node[above] {\small $y$};
  \foreach \x in {-3,-2,-1,1,2,3} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {2,4,6,8} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[thick, linegray] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2.8,7.84) (-2,4) (-1,1) (0,0) (1,1) (2,4) (2.8,7.84)};
  \draw[very thick, plum] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2.2,7.84) (-2,7) (-1,4) (0,3) (1,4) (2,7) (2.2,7.84)};
  \fill[plum] (0,3) circle (2pt);
  \fill[linegray] (0,0) circle (2pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
{\small\textcolor{linegray}{\textbf{gray}} $= f(x) = x^2$ \qquad
 \textcolor{plum}{\textbf{plum}} $= g(x) = f(x)+3$}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item The lowest point moved from $(0,0)$ to $($ \ans{0} ,
        \ans{3} $)$ --- straight \ans{up} by 3.
  \item \textbf{Domain} of $g$: \ans{all real numbers} (unchanged --- the
        inputs never moved).
  \item \textbf{Range} of $g$: $f$ had range $[0, \infty)$, so $g$ has range
        \ans{$[3, \infty)$}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Horizontal Translation: $g(x) = f(x + h)$}
The $+h$ sits \textbf{inside} $f$, so it changes the inputs --- and the graph
moves the \textit{opposite} way from what the sign suggests.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 2.} $g(x) = f(x + 3)$, where $f(x) = x^2$. Complete the table.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-5$ & $-4$ & $-3$ & $-2$ & $-1$ \\
\hline
$x + 3$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$g(x) = f(x+3)$ & \ans{4} & \ans{1} & \ans{0}
  & \ans{1} & \ans{4} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
\textbf{Look at what you wrote.} The output list is \ans{identical} to $f$'s
list --- nothing happened to the outputs at all. What moved were the
\ans{inputs} they happen at.

\medskip
\textbf{Why left?} Remember the warm-up. $f$ hits its lowest point when its
input is $0$, so $g$ hits its lowest point when $x + 3 = 0$, that is, when
$x = $ \ans{$-3$} --- which is 3 units to the \ans{left}.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.42cm, y=0.34cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-6,0) grid (3,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (-6.3,0) -- (3.5,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,6.6) node[above] {\small $y$};
  \foreach \x in {-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,1,2,3} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {2,4,6} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[thick, linegray] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2.45,6) (-2,4) (-1,1) (0,0) (1,1) (2,4) (2.45,6)};
  \draw[very thick, plum] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-5.45,6) (-5,4) (-4,1) (-3,0) (-2,1) (-1,4) (-0.55,6)};
  \fill[plum] (-3,0) circle (2pt);
  \fill[linegray] (0,0) circle (2pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
{\small\textcolor{linegray}{\textbf{gray}} $= f(x) = x^2$ \qquad
 \textcolor{plum}{\textbf{plum}} $= g(x) = f(x+3)$}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $h > 0$ (as in $f(x+3)$): shift \ans{left}. \quad
        $h < 0$ (as in $f(x-2)$): shift \ans{right}.
  \item \textbf{Domain} shifts by the same amount; \textbf{range}
        \ans{is unchanged} --- the outputs never changed.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{4. Dilations and Reflections}
\textbf{Vertical dilation: $g(x) = af(x)$.} The $a$ is \textbf{outside}, so it
multiplies every output. $|a| > 1$ \textbf{stretches} the graph away from the
$x$-axis; $0 < |a| < 1$ \textbf{compresses} it toward the $x$-axis. If
$a < 0$, the graph also \textbf{flips} over the $x$-axis.

\medskip
\textbf{Example 3.} Complete both rows for $f(x) = x^2$.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$f(x)$ & 4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 4 \\
\hline
$g(x) = 2f(x)$ & \ans{8} & \ans{2} & \ans{0}
  & \ans{2} & \ans{8} \\
\hline
$p(x) = -f(x)$ & \ans{$-4$} & \ans{$-1$} & \ans{0}
  & \ans{$-1$} & \ans{$-4$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.62cm, y=0.26cm]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-2.4,-4) grid (2.4,8);
  \draw[->, thick] (-2.7,0) -- (2.9,0) node[below right] {\small $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-4.4) -- (0,8.6) node[above] {\small $y$};
  \foreach \x in {-2,-1,1,2} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-4,-2,2,4,6,8} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[thick, linegray] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2,4) (-1,1) (0,0) (1,1) (2,4)};
  \draw[very thick, plum] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2,8) (-1,2) (0,0) (1,2) (2,8)};
  \draw[very thick, goldacc] plot [smooth] coordinates
    {(-2,-4) (-1,-1) (0,0) (1,-1) (2,-4)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
{\small\textcolor{linegray}{\textbf{gray}} $= f(x)$ \qquad
 \textcolor{plum}{\textbf{plum}} $= 2f(x)$ \qquad
 \textcolor{goldacc}{\textbf{gold}} $= -f(x)$}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt]
  \item $g(x) = 2f(x)$ is a vertical \ans{stretch} by a factor of
        \ans{2}.
  \item $p(x) = -f(x)$ is a \ans{reflection} over the \ans{$x$}-axis.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Horizontal dilation: $g(x) = f(bx)$.} The $b$ is \textbf{inside}, so
--- opposite again --- the graph is squeezed by a factor of $\dfrac{1}{|b|}$.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-1$ & $-0.5$ & 0 & $0.5$ & 1 \\
\hline
$2x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
\hline
$g(x) = f(2x)$ & \ans{4} & \ans{1} & \ans{0}
  & \ans{1} & \ans{4} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
$g$ reaches the same heights as $f$, but at \ans{half} the $x$-value ---
so the graph is \ans{compressed} horizontally, by a factor of \ans{$1/2$}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{5. Combining Them --- and Domain \& Range}
Every transformation today fits one template:

\begin{center}
$g(x) = a\,f(x + h) + k$
\end{center}

\textbf{Example 4.} Describe every move that turns $f$ into
$g(x) = -2f(x+1) - 3$.

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{0.16\linewidth} >{\itshape}p{0.20\linewidth} X @{}}
\hline
Parameter & Inside or out? & What it does to the graph \\
\hline
$h = 1$   & inside  & \ans{Shifts the graph 1 unit to the left.} \\
\hline
$a = -2$  & outside & \ans{Stretches it vertically by 2 and flips it over the $x$-axis.} \\
\hline
$k = -3$  & outside & \ans{Shifts the graph 3 units down.} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\medskip
\textbf{Domain and range.} Horizontal changes move the \ans{domain};
vertical changes move the \ans{range}.

\smallskip
Suppose $f$ has domain $[-2, 4]$ and range $[0, 9]$. For the same
$g(x) = -2f(x+1) - 3$:

\begin{work}
  \text{Domain: } -2 &\le x + 1 \le 4 \\
                  -3 &\le x \le 3 \\
  \text{Range: } 0 &\le y \le 9 \\
              -18 &\le -2y \le 0 \\
              -21 &\le -2y - 3 \le -3
\end{work}

Domain of $g$: \ans{$[-3, 3]$} \qquad Range of $g$: \ans{$[-21, -3]$}

\smallskip
\textbf{Careful:} multiplying by a negative \textit{swaps} which endpoint is
smaller --- always rewrite the interval with the smaller number first.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard
\begin{practicebox}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt]
  \item Describe, in words, each transformation of the graph of $f$.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=4pt]
          \item $g(x) = f(x) - 6$: \ans{shift down 6 units}
          \item $g(x) = f(x + 5)$: \ans{shift left 5 units}
          \item $g(x) = -f(x)$: \ans{reflect over the $x$-axis}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item The domain of $f$ is $[1, 7]$ and its range is $[-4, 2]$. Find the
        domain and range of $g(x) = f(x - 3) + 5$.

        \begin{work}
          \text{Domain: } 1 &\le x - 3 \le 7 \\
                          4 &\le x \le 10 \\
          \text{Range: } -4 &\le y \le 2 \\
                          1 &\le y + 5 \le 7
        \end{work}

        Domain: \ans{$[4, 10]$} \qquad Range: \ans{$[1, 7]$}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}
