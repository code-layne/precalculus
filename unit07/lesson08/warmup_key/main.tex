\textbf{1.}\quad The average rate of change of $f$ over $[a, b]$ is
$\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$.
The table below gives values of a polar function $r = f(\theta)$.
Compute the average rate of change over each interval.

\vspace{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\theta$ & $0$ & $\pi/2$ & $\pi$ & $3\pi/2$ \\
\hline
$r = f(\theta)$ & $4$ & $2$ & $0$ & $2$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{8pt}
(a)\quad Average rate of change from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \pi/2$:

\vspace{4pt}
$\dfrac{f(\pi/2) - f(0)}{\pi/2} = \dfrac{{\color{keyred}\mathbf{2}} - {\color{keyred}\mathbf{4}}}{\pi/2} = $ \ans{$-4/\pi \approx -1.27$}

\vspace{6pt}
(b)\quad Average rate of change from $\theta = \pi/2$ to $\theta = \pi$:

\vspace{4pt}
$\dfrac{f(\pi) - f(\pi/2)}{\pi/2} = $ \ans{$(0-2)/(\pi/2) = -4/\pi \approx -1.27$}

\vspace{0.14in}
\textbf{2.}\quad Evaluate $r = 2\sin\theta$ at each angle.

\vspace{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\theta$ & $0$ & $\pi/6$ & $\pi/2$ & $\pi$ \\
\hline
$r = 2\sin\theta$ & \ans{$0$} & \ans{$1$} & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$0$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.14in}
\textbf{3.}\quad Using the table from Problem~2, on which interval is
$r = 2\sin\theta$ \textbf{decreasing}?

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
(A)\quad $[0, \pi/6]$ \qquad\qquad & (B)\quad $[\pi/6, \pi/2]$ \\[4pt]
\textbf{(C)}\quad $[\pi/2, \pi]$ & (D)\quad Both (A) and (B) \\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{4pt}
\ans{(C)} --- \ans{$r$ goes from 2 to 0 on $[\pi/2, \pi]$, so $r$ is decreasing.}

\vspace{0.14in}
\textbf{4.}\quad A polar function has $r = 1.8$ at $\theta = 0$ and $r = 0.3$
at $\theta = \pi/4$.

\vspace{4pt}
(a)\quad \ans{Positive and decreasing} ($r > 0$ and $r$ is shrinking from 1.8 to 0.3).

\vspace{4pt}
(b)\quad $\dfrac{0.3 - 1.8}{\pi/4 - 0} = \dfrac{-1.5}{\pi/4}$ \ans{$= -6/\pi \approx -1.91$ per radian}
