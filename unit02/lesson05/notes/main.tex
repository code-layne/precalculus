\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.5.A --- Building a Model from Two Points (EK 2.5.A.3)}
\textbf{Strategy:} Given $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$, write two equations:
\[
  y_1 = a \cdot b^{x_1} \qquad \text{and} \qquad y_2 = a \cdot b^{x_2}
\]
Divide the second by the first to eliminate $a$:
\[
  \frac{y_2}{y_1} = b^{\,x_2 - x_1} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad b = \left(\frac{y_2}{y_1}\right)^{1/(x_2-x_1)}
\]
Then solve for $a$: $a = y_1 / b^{x_1}$.

\medskip
\textbf{Example:} An exponential function passes through $(0, 5)$ and $(3, 40)$.

\smallskip
Step 1 --- Find $b$: \blank{8cm}

\smallskip
Step 2 --- Find $a$: \blank{6cm}

\smallskip
Model: $f(x) = $ \blank{6cm}

\medskip
\textbf{Natural base $e$ (EK 2.5.A.6):} $e \approx $ \blank{2cm}. Used in continuous models: $f(t) = a \cdot e^{rt}$.

Example: $f(t) = 500 e^{0.04t}$ grows \blank{2cm}\% continuously per year.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.5.B --- Equivalent Forms and Predictions (EK 2.5.B.2)}
\textbf{Equivalent forms reveal different time-scale properties.}

$f(d) = 3 \cdot 2^d$ models growth where $d$ = days.

Rewrite for weeks (let $w = d/7$, so $d = 7w$):
\[
  f(d) = 3 \cdot 2^d = 3 \cdot 2^{7w} = 3 \cdot (2^7)^w = 3 \cdot \blank{3cm}^w
\]
The base \blank{3cm} means the quantity multiplies by \blank{4cm} each week.

\medskip
\textbf{Prediction and domain caution:} Use the model for inputs \blank{5cm}.

Predict the town population at $t = 20$: $P(20) = 10000 \cdot (1.03)^{20} \approx $ \blank{3cm}

\textit{Domain note:} Is $t = 200$ realistic? \blank{5cm}
\end{notesbox}
