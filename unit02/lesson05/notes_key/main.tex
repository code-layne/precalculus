\begin{hookbox}
A student wraps rubber bands around a ball. The radius (in mm) grows according to
$r(t) = 2t^3 - 50t^2 + 100t$.

At $t = 1{,}000$ minutes: \quad $2t^3 = $ \ans{$2 \times 10^9$} \quad $-50t^2 = $ \ans{$-5 \times 10^7$}

What percentage of the cubic term is the $-50t^2$ term? \ans{about $2.5\%$}

Which term ``wins'' as $t$ gets very large? \ans{$2t^3$ (the leading term)}
\end{hookbox}

\begin{notesbox}{Leading Term Dominance}
\textbf{Key Idea:} For large $|x|$, the \ans{leading} term of a polynomial dominates
all lower-degree terms.

\medskip
\textbf{Example.} Let $p(x) = x^3 - 100x^2$.
Complete the table by computing $p(x)$ and comparing it to $x^3$:

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $p(x) = x^3 - 100x^2$ & $x^3$ & $\dfrac{-100x^2}{x^3} = \dfrac{-100}{x}$ \\
\hline
$100$   & \ans{$0$} & \ans{$1{,}000{,}000$} & \ans{$-1$} \\
$1{,}000$ & \ans{$9 \times 10^8$} & \ans{$10^9$} & \ans{$-0.1$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
As $x \to \infty$, the ratio $-100/x \to$ \ans{$0$}, so $p(x)$ behaves like \ans{$x^3$}.

\medskip
\textbf{Conclusion:} To find end behavior, look only at the \ans{leading term}.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{notesbox}{The Four End-Behavior Cases}
The end behavior of any polynomial is determined by its \textbf{degree} (even or odd) and the
\textbf{sign} of its leading coefficient.

\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Degree} & \textbf{Leading Coeff.} & $\lim_{x \to \infty} p(x)$ & $\lim_{x \to -\infty} p(x)$ \\
\hline
Even & Positive ($a_n > 0$) & \ans{$\infty$}  & \ans{$\infty$} \\
Even & Negative ($a_n < 0$) & \ans{$-\infty$} & \ans{$-\infty$} \\
Odd  & Positive ($a_n > 0$) & \ans{$\infty$}  & \ans{$-\infty$} \\
Odd  & Negative ($a_n < 0$) & \ans{$-\infty$} & \ans{$\infty$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Memory aid:} Even-degree polynomials have both ends going the \ans{same} direction.
Odd-degree polynomials have ends going \ans{opposite} directions.

\medskip
\textbf{Practice.} State the end behavior of $p(x) = -3x^4 + 2x^2 - 7$.

\smallskip
Leading term: \ans{$-3x^4$} \quad Degree: \ans{4 (even)} \quad Sign: \ans{negative}

\medskip
$\lim_{x \to \infty} p(x) = $ \ans{$-\infty$} \qquad $\lim_{x \to -\infty} p(x) = $ \ans{$-\infty$}
\end{notesbox}
