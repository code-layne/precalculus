\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Transformation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A change to a function's rule that moves, flips, or resizes its graph.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Translation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A slide up, down, left, or right; the shape does not change.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Reflection:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A flip of the graph over the $x$-axis or over the $y$-axis.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Vertical dilation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Every output multiplied by $|a|$: a stretch or squeeze away from the $x$-axis.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Horizontal dilation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Every input scaled by $1/|b|$: a stretch or squeeze away from the $y$-axis.}
\end{vocabbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\boxguard
\begin{hookbox}
A designer has the curve $y = x^2$ on screen and wants it moved \textbf{3 units to the left}.
She types $y = (x - 3)^2$ --- and the curve slides 3 units to the \emph{right}.

Which equation should she have typed? And why would the sign seem to go the ``wrong'' way?
\ansline{$y = (x + 3)^2$. The $+3$ acts on the input before squaring, so the new curve needs $x = -3$ to feed the square a 0 --- the vertex lands at $x = -3$, on the left.}
\end{hookbox}

% ── ROW 1: translations — the I Do anchor ────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Translations]{Shifts} &
A \textbf{translation} slides a graph without changing its shape. A number added
\emph{outside}, $g(x) = f(x) + k$, changes every output by $k$: the graph moves \textbf{up} $k$
(down when $k < 0$). A number added \emph{inside}, $g(x) = f(x - h)$, is a
\textbf{composition}: the inner function $x - h$ adjusts the input \emph{before} $f$ acts, so
the graph moves \textbf{right} $h$ --- and $f(x + 3)$ moves \textbf{left} 3, because $x = -3$
is the input that feeds $f$ a 0.

% ── ROW 2: outside — reflection over the x-axis and vertical dilation ────────
\mainidea[Acts on $y$]{Outside} &
A change \emph{outside} $f$ happens after $f$ has made its output, so it acts on the
$y$-values and does exactly what it says. In $g(x) = a\,f(x)$ every output is multiplied by
$a$: a \textbf{vertical dilation} --- a stretch when $|a| > 1$, a compression when
$0 < |a| < 1$. When $a < 0$ the graph also flips: $-f(x)$ is the \textbf{reflection} of $f$
over the $x$-axis.

% ── ROW 3: inside — reflection over the y-axis and horizontal dilation ───────
\mainidea[Acts on $x$]{Inside} &
A change \emph{inside} $f$ happens to $x$ before $f$ acts --- a composition again --- so it
changes the $x$-values and runs \textbf{backwards}. $f(-x)$ is the reflection of $f$ over the
$y$-axis. $g(x) = f(bx)$ is a \textbf{horizontal dilation} by a factor of $\tfrac{1}{|b|}$: in
$f(2x)$, $x$ only has to be half as big to feed $f$ the same input, so the graph squeezes
toward the $y$-axis by a factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

% ── ROW 4: THE CRUX — all four together, and the inside that runs backwards ──
\mainidea[All four at once]{Combined} &
Every move fits one form, $g(x) = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k$. The inside numbers $b$ and $h$
act on $x$ and run backwards; the outside numbers $a$ and $k$ act on $y$ and do what they say.
A key point $(x, y)$ of $f$ lands at $\left(\tfrac{x}{b} + h,\ a\,y + k\right)$.

\vspace{3pt}
\stepnum{1}\ \textbf{Inside:} divide $x$ by $b$, then add $h$. \quad
\stepnum{2}\ \textbf{Outside:} multiply $y$ by $a$, then add $k$.
