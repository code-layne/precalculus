    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will build a new function from a parent function by
    additive and multiplicative transformations --- translations $f(x) + k$ and $f(x - h)$,
    reflections $-f(x)$ and $f(-x)$, and dilations $a\,f(x)$ and $f(bx)$ --- describe the
    result in words and in order, map a parent's key points with
    $(x, y) \to \left(\tfrac{x}{b} + h,\ a\,y + k\right)$, write the equation of a pre-drawn
    transformed graph, and explain why a change \emph{inside} the function runs backwards.
    \hfill\textit{\small (CED 1.12 --- LO 1.12.A)}
\end{tcolorbox}

\smallskip
\textbf{This lesson does two days' work in one.} The original plan gave translations a day of
their own and every other transformation a second day; here they share one period, so the
guided phase runs 23 minutes and the You Do gets 15. Translations are the anchor ---
they get the I Do, and every later row reuses their one idea: \emph{inside acts on $x$ and
runs backwards; outside acts on $y$ and does what it says.} If the clock slips, cut
\textbf{problem 12} (describe in order) first --- the DeltaMath combined-description topic and
You Do problem 14 ask it again --- then problem 6. Protect \textbf{problems 10 and 11}: they
are the crux, and You Do problem 16 and AP item 2 cannot be read without them. Never cut the
You Do below 14 minutes --- circulating it is the period's only formative read.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{AP Skill 1.C} --- Construct new functions using transformations. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $g(x) = f(x) + k$ moves the graph of $f$ vertically by $k$ (EK 1.12.A.1).
  \item $g(x) = f(x + h)$ moves it horizontally by $-h$ units: the inner function adjusts the
        input before $f$ acts, so $f(x + 3)$ moves \emph{left} 3 (EK 1.12.A.2).
  \item The moves combine: $g(x) = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k$, and a key point
        $(x, y)$ lands at $\left(\tfrac{x}{b} + h,\ a\,y + k\right)$ (EK 1.12.A.5).
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 3.A} --- Describe the characteristics of a function. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item $a\,f(x)$ is a vertical dilation by $|a|$; $a < 0$ adds a reflection over the
        $x$-axis (EK 1.12.A.3).
  \item $f(bx)$ is a horizontal dilation by $\tfrac{1}{|b|}$; $b < 0$ adds a reflection over
        the $y$-axis (EK 1.12.A.4).
  \item A transformation can change the domain and range: $-\sqrt{x + 2} + 5$ has domain
        $x \ge -2$ and range $y \le 5$ (EK 1.12.A.6).
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} reading the inside forwards --- $f(x + 3)$ as
        ``right 3,'' and $f(2x)$ as a horizontal \emph{stretch} by 2. Both run backwards: solve
        for the $x$ that feeds $f$ its old input.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.24\linewidth} X @{}}
Transformation      & A change to a function's rule that moves, flips, or resizes its graph. \\
Translation         & A slide up, down, left, or right: $f(x) + k$ (vertical), $f(x - h)$ (horizontal). The shape does not change. \\
Reflection          & A flip: $-f(x)$ over the $x$-axis, $f(-x)$ over the $y$-axis. \\
Vertical dilation   & $a\,f(x)$: every output multiplied by $a$ --- a stretch when $|a| > 1$, a compression when $0 < |a| < 1$. \\
Horizontal dilation & $f(bx)$: every input scaled by $\tfrac{1}{|b|}$ --- a compression when $|b| > 1$, a stretch when $0 < |b| < 1$. \\
Point-mapping rule  & For $g(x) = a\,f\big(b(x - h)\big) + k$: \TallMath{(x, y) \to \left(\tfrac{x}{b} + h,\ a\,y + k\right)} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review (5 min)]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: evaluating the 2.1 parent functions ($\sqrt{x}$, $|x|$, $x^3$,
    $\tfrac{1}{x}$) at a given input; a composition from 1.3, $f(g(x))$ with inner function
    $x + 3$ --- the exact mechanism of today's horizontal shift; solving a one-step equation for
    the input that makes an expression a given value; and checking whether a point satisfies
    a function's rule.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook (2 min)]{lilac}
    \textbf{The sign that goes the wrong way.} A designer has $y = x^2$ on screen and wants it
    moved 3 units to the \emph{left}. She types $y = (x - 3)^2$, and the curve slides 3 units
    to the \emph{right}.
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item Ask: which equation should she have typed? Take a show of hands for $(x - 3)^2$
              and $(x + 3)^2$ --- do not settle it.
        \item Ask: \emph{why} would the sign seem to go the wrong way? Leave it hanging; the
              SHIFTS row answers it with the warm-up's composition.
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} by the end of today you will be able to prove which equation
    is right with one input, not a rule of thumb.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- I Do / We Do / You Do]{lilac}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{I DO --- Part 1: Shifts, and the Outside} (10 min)

\textit{Notes rows SHIFTS and OUTSIDE: read each definition, mark up the display, work
problems 1 and 5. Teacher works; students watch and annotate. Nothing is cold-called in this
phase.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the SHIFTS sentences. Outside first --- $f(x) + k$ adds to the output, easy. Then
        say the key word for the inside: \textbf{composition}. Point back at warm-up item 2:
        $f(g(-3))$ was 0 because the inner function $x + 3$ handed $f$ a 0.
  \item Mark up the display: the table's middle column is ``what $f$ sees.'' The vertex of
        $(x + 3)^2$ is where $f$ sees 0, at $x = -3$. Fill both label boxes yourself ---
        \emph{left}, and $0$ --- and settle the hook.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 1} aloud: $x^2 - 4$ is down 4; $(x - 4)^2$ is right 4 because the
        inside is 0 at $x = 4$. Say the sentence ``solve for the inside'' every time.
  \item Read the OUTSIDE row. On the display, trace $(4, 2)$ straight up to $(4, 4)$ and
        straight down to $(4, -2)$ --- the $x$ never moves. Fill the two label boxes, then work
        \textbf{problem 5}: $-3|x|$ multiplies $y$ by $-3$, so $(2, 2) \to (2, -6)$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 2: Shifts and Stretches Together} (6 min)

\textit{Problems 2--4 and 6. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 2}: $|x + 2| + 1$ --- make the student say ``the inside is 0 at
        $-2$'' before they say ``left.''
  \item \textbf{Problem 3} follows a single point; \textbf{problem 4} runs the idea backwards,
        from words to an equation: left 5 is $+5$ inside.
  \item \textbf{Problem 6}: $\tfrac{1}{2}x^2$ --- the $x$ stays put, the $y$ halves.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 3: The Inside} (7 min)

\textit{Notes row INSIDE. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one. Teacher works
problem 7; the class works 8.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the sentences, then the display: on $\sqrt{2x}$ the output 2 arrives at $x = 2$,
        not 4. Read the table's ``what $f$ sees'' column aloud. Fill the label boxes ---
        \emph{compression}, $\tfrac{1}{2}$ --- and say why: the 2 inside makes $x$ do half the
        work.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 7}: $\sqrt{-x}$ needs $-x = 9$, so $x = -9$.
  \item \textbf{Problem 8} puts both dilations side by side: $f(3x)$ squeezes by
        $\tfrac{1}{3}$, $f(\tfrac{1}{3}x)$ stretches by 3. Insist on solving $3x = 6$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 4: Combined, and the Trap} (10 min)

\textit{Notes row COMBINED --- the crux. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one.
Teacher fills the first line of the problem-9 table; the class does the rest and 10--13.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the general form and the two steps. Fill the $(0, 0)$ line of the
        \textbf{problem 9} table yourself, then cold-call the other three lines.
  \item \textbf{Problems 10 and 11 are the crux}: each states the naive reading, and the
        answer disagrees with it. Ask for the \emph{input that settles it}, not the rule:
        $x + 3 = 0$ at $-3$; $2x = 4$ at $2$.
  \item \textbf{Problem 12} describes in order; \textbf{problem 13} reads Graph A ---
        vertex first, then ``one right, two down'' gives $a = -2$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{YOU DO --- Part 5: Map \& Match} (15 min)

\textit{Notes row MAP \& MATCH, problems 14--17 --- the whole independent phase. Students work
alone; circulate and say nothing a neighbor could say instead.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 14} is a translation of $\sqrt{x}$ and one point; \textbf{15} maps three
        points of $x^3$ through a stretch and two shifts.
  \item \textbf{Problem 16} is the crux transferred --- $f(x + 6)$ and $f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)$, with
        no formula for $f$ at all: only solving the inside works.
  \item \textbf{Problem 17} is the finisher's question: write Graph B's equation, a reflected,
        stretched, shifted $\sqrt{x}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the \textbf{You Do} phase --- the fifteen minutes where students are working
problems 14--17 without you --- and check. The crux is \textbf{problems 10 and 11} (the inside
read forwards); its You Do transfer is \textbf{problem 16} ($(6, 5)$ on $f(x + 6)$ and on
$f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)$, with no formula to lean on).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error (the crux):} $f(x + 6)$ sends $(6, 5)$ to $(12, 5)$ --- the sign
        read forwards. Ask: ``What does $f$ need to see? Which $x$ makes $x + 6$ equal 6?''
  \item \textbf{Common error (the crux):} $f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)$ sends $(6, 5)$ to $(3, 5)$ ---
        the $\tfrac{1}{2}$ applied to $x$ directly. Same question: ``Solve
        $\tfrac{1}{2}x = 6$.''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} applying an outside number to $x$ or an inside number to $y$ ---
        in problem 15, writing $(-1, -1) \to (5, -1)$. Ask: ``Is the 2 inside or outside the
        cube? Then which coordinate does it touch?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} in problem 14, ``right 4, down 2.'' Point at the SHIFTS display
        and the label box they filled: where is the inside 0?
  \item \textbf{Graph B (17):} reading $a$ as $+2$ because the curve is ``stretched'' --- ask
        which way the graph goes from its start point.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Differentiation --- During You Do (15 min)]{redbox}
There is no separate activity sheet: students work the notes' You Do row (problems 14--17)
alone while you circulate. Differentiate by \textit{where you stand}, not by handing out
different paper.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Support.} Sit with a struggling student on \textbf{problem 14} and make them
        write ``$x + 4 = 0$'' before they write a direction. The one move from the I Do ---
        solve for the inside --- is the whole fix; if they skip it, the sign error is already
        in.
  \item \textbf{On level.} \textbf{Problems 15 and 16} are the class's target. Do not answer
        16; have them say what $f$ needs to see, solve the inside, and walk away.
  \item \textbf{Extend.} \textbf{Problem 17} is the finisher's question --- writing the
        equation means choosing $h$, $k$, and the \emph{sign} of $a$ from three dots. A student
        who finishes early: ``Write Graph B a second way, as a reflection of $\sqrt{-x}$'' ---
        then let them present 17 in the Debrief.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (5 min)]{redbox}
Whole class, teacher-led, packets still open. Three moves, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Share out the You Do} (2 min) --- one answer per problem, not a full review.
        Problem 14: left 4, down 2, $(-4, -2)$. Problem 15: the three images. Problem 16:
        $(0, 5)$ and $(12, 5)$, each with the equation that settled it. Problem 17:
        $y = -2\sqrt{x + 3} + 4$, from a student who reached it.
  \item \textbf{Name the headline} (1 min) --- say it, then make the class say it back:
        \textit{outside the function, a change acts on the outputs and does exactly what it
        says; inside the function, it acts on the inputs and runs backwards --- so solve for
        the $x$ that feeds $f$ its old input.} Point at the hook's $(x - 3)^2$ while you say it.
  \item \textbf{Point forward} (2 min) --- the DeltaMath assignment, \textit{Lesson 2.2}, and its
        due date: write it on the board and have students copy it into cover row 4. Then 2.3
        brings $f(-x)$ back: when it gives the same function, the graph is its own mirror
        image.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Do not} re-teach dilations here or take new questions. There is no exit ticket: the
You Do was the period's formative read. If circulating it showed the inside
did not land, that is the 2.3 warm-up's job, not this minute's.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Translations of Functions} (4 problems) --- matches notes problems 1--2.
  \item \textbf{Write the Equation of a Translated Function} (3 problems) --- matches notes
        problem 4.
  \item \textbf{Reflections and Dilations of Functions} (4 problems) --- matches notes problems
        5--8.
  \item \textbf{Transformations of Points} (5 problems) \textit{(diagnostic)} --- matches notes
        problems 3, 8, 9, 11, and 15--16: where a point lands when the change is inside.
  \item \textbf{Describe a Combination of Transformations} (4 problems) --- matches notes
        problems 10, 12, and 14.
  \item \textbf{Write the Equation of a Transformed Graph} (3 problems) --- matches notes
        problems 13 and 17.
\end{enumerate}
\textit{Leave out:} Even and Odd Functions and Inverse Functions (2.3 teaches them); any
\emph{sketch the transformed graph} topic (the lesson pre-draws every graph); and
transformations of trigonometric, exponential, or logarithmic functions (later units).

\textbf{AP Practice --- extra credit (2 pages).} Optional, last in the packet.
Section I is five AP-style multiple-choice items whose distractors are the lesson's errors: the
inside sign read forwards (1, A and D), $f(3x)$ read as a stretch or applied to $y$ (2, A and B),
the asymptote moved the wrong way (3, A), the inside or outside sign flipped or $a$ inverted
(4, B--D), and a range that forgets the reflection (5, A) or the transformation entirely (5, B).
Section II is one free-response question on two greenhouses, $g(t) = 2f(t - 3) + 1$: map a
table (a), evaluate and interpret in context (b), describe in order (c), refute a forwards
reading of $f(2t)$ (d), and build a reflected function (e) --- the finisher's question.

\textbf{Preview:} Next lesson (2.3, Symmetry and Inverse Functions) brings $f(-x)$ back as a
test: when $f(-x) = f(x)$ the graph is its own reflection over the $y$-axis. Notes problem 7
has already shown the move: $\sqrt{-x}$ sends $(9, 3)$ to $(-9, 3)$, and on $x^2$ the same
reflection would land $(3, 9)$ on a point already on the graph.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Item 1 re-activates the 2.1 library --- every
row of the notes reuses those five shapes. Item 2 is the one that matters: $f(g(-3)) = 0$
because the inner function $x + 3$ handed $f$ a 0, and that is the whole explanation of the
horizontal shift. Say so when you go over it, and keep it on the board for the SHIFTS row.
Item 3 rehearses ``solve for the inside,'' the move the crux depends on. On item 4, a student
who circles $(4, 2)$ evaluated $\sqrt{4}$ instead of $\sqrt{4 - 1}$ --- the inside ignored,
which is the misconception before it has a name. Flag them.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The notes are one Main Ideas / Notes table: four instruction rows and a You Do row. In each
row you read the printed sentences, mark up the display, and work the first problem (1, 5, 7,
and the first line of the problem-9 table); the class works the rest, every problem
cold-called. The whole lesson rests on one sentence said in the SHIFTS row and repeated in
every row after it: \emph{inside acts on $x$ and runs backwards; outside acts on $y$ and does
what it says.} The SHIFTS table is the proof --- its middle column, ``what $f$ sees,'' makes
the composition visible --- so do not skip it for the graph.

This is two days condensed into one. Move briskly through OUTSIDE --- students find vertical
changes natural --- and spend the saved minutes in INSIDE and COMBINED. Problems 10 and 11 are
written so the naive reading and the answer disagree; make the student who reads them forwards
\emph{test} the claim with a point rather than hear the correction. If time is short, cut 12
before anything in the crux, and never hand out the ``move opposite the sign'' trick: it fails
on $f(2x)$ and on $f(-x)$, and solving the inside never does.

The You Do (14--17) carries fifteen minutes. Problem 16 is the crux with the formula taken
away; problem 17 inverts the lesson --- writing an equation from a graph --- and returns in the
last DeltaMath topic.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Debrief]
Five minutes, and in a condensed lesson they are the first thing the guided phase tries to
borrow. Borrow from problem 12 instead; the DeltaMath combined-description topic asks it again.
Never borrow from the You Do either: with no exit ticket, circulating it is the last look you get before the work leaves the room. The share-out is one answer per You
Do problem so that a student who stalled on 15 still hears problem 16's two points and the
equations that settled them --- the exact shape of the diagnostic DeltaMath topic.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[AP Practice]
Extra credit, optional, and not a substitute for the DeltaMath homework --- say so when you
hand out the packet. Multiple-choice answers are \textbf{C, D, B, A, C}. Item 2 is the one to glance at: a
student who circles (A) $(36, -6)$ read $f(3x)$ as a stretch by 3, and one who circles (B)
$(12, -18)$ applied the inside 3 to the output --- the two halves of the lesson's
misconception. Item 1's (A) is the hook's error again. In the free response, accept any order
in (c) that puts the stretch before the vertical shift; part (d) is the crux in context ---
credit any answer that solves $2t = 4$. Part (e) is the finisher's: $j(t) = f(-t) + 4$, with
$(4, 5) \to (-4, 9)$.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
DeltaMath, graded, due on the date you set (cover row 4). \textbf{Transformations of Points} is
the diagnostic: a miss on an inside change --- $(6, 5)$ on $f(x + 6)$ sent to $(12, 5)$, or
$f(3x)$ treated as a stretch by 3 --- means the student read the inside forwards, the target
misconception. A student who misses it but clears \textbf{Translations of Functions} has
memorised ``opposite the sign'' for shifts without solving the inside, and that breaks on
$f(bx)$. DeltaMath can grade the answer but not the reason: it cannot ask a student to explain
\emph{why} the inside runs backwards. You Do \textbf{problem 16} carries that --- $(6, 5)$ on
$f(x + 6)$ and $f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)$ with no formula, checked with the inside --- so read it over
shoulders during the You Do. \textbf{Reflections and Dilations} predicts 2.3: a student who
confuses $-f(x)$ with $f(-x)$ there will struggle with the even/odd test. If either topic shows
a slip across the class, make the 2.3 warm-up's first item ``$(3, 9)$ is on $y = x^2$: where
does it land on $f(-x)$, and where is the inside of $f(x + 6)$ zero?'' and reuse the SHIFTS
table.
\end{teachernote}
