\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can read the \textbf{domain} and \textbf{range} of a function from its graph ---
        including filled dots, open circles, and arrows --- and write them in
        \textbf{interval notation}.
  \item I can find the domain of a function from its formula by keeping every denominator
        away from 0 and every even-root \textbf{radicand} at 0 or above.
  \item I can find a function's \textbf{$y$-intercept} by computing $f(0)$ and its
        \textbf{zeros} ($x$-intercepts) by solving $f(x) = 0$.
  \item I can describe a function's \textbf{end behavior} in limit notation from a graph or
        from a polynomial's leading term, and recognize when the outputs level off at a
        \textbf{horizontal asymptote}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
Every characteristic today is a question about \textbf{inputs} or about \textbf{outputs} ---
never both at once. The \textbf{domain} and the $x$ in ``$x \to \infty$'' are about inputs;
the \textbf{range}, the 0 that marks an $x$-intercept, and the number a limit equals are
about outputs.
When a graph levels off along a horizontal asymptote, it is the \emph{output} that settles
down while the \emph{input} keeps going forever.
\end{remindbox}
