\begin{vocabbox}
Fill in each term as we define it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Interval notation:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Writing a set of numbers by its endpoints: $[\ ]$ includes an endpoint, $(\ )$ leaves it out.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Radicand:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The expression under a root sign; under an even root it must be $\ge 0$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{$y$-intercept:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{Where the graph crosses the $y$-axis: the point $(0, f(0))$, where the input is 0.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Zero of a function:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{An input $a$ with $f(a) = 0$; the graph has an $x$-intercept at $(a, 0)$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{End behavior:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{What the outputs do as the inputs increase or decrease without bound.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Horizontal asymptote:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A line $y = b$ the graph levels off toward as $x \to \infty$ or $x \to -\infty$.}
\end{vocabbox}

% ── ROW 1: domain and range read off a graph, in interval notation ──────────
\mainidea[Reading a graph]{Domain \& Range} &
Read the \textbf{domain} left to right along the $x$-axis: every input the graph uses. Read the
\textbf{range} bottom to top along the $y$-axis: every output it reaches. Write both in
\textbf{interval notation}: a square bracket $[\ ]$ includes an endpoint, a round parenthesis
$(\ )$ leaves it out, and $\infty$ or $-\infty$ always gets a round parenthesis --- no graph
ever reaches it.
\\

% ── ROW 2: domain from a formula — the two forbidden moves ──────────────────
\mainidea[From a formula]{Domain} &
Unless the formula forbids it, every real number is in the domain. Two things forbid an input:
a \textbf{denominator} equal to 0 (division by zero is undefined), and a negative
\textbf{radicand} under an even root such as $\sqrt{\ }$ (not a real number).

\vspace{3pt}
\stepnum{1}\ Denominator? Set it $\ne 0$ and cross out those inputs. \quad \stepnum{2}\ Even
root? Set the radicand $\ge 0$ and solve. \quad \stepnum{3}\ Keep only the inputs that pass
\emph{both} tests. The symbol $\cup$ (``union'') joins two intervals.
\\
& \begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.95cm, y=1cm, >=stealth]
  \node[font=\normalsize, anchor=west] at (-0.4,1.35) {$k(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{x-3}}{x-5}$};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, anchor=west, align=left] at (3.2,1.55) {radicand: $x - 3 \ge 0$, so $x \ge 3$};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, anchor=west, align=left] at (3.2,1.05) {denominator: $x - 5 \ne 0$, so $x \ne 5$};
  \draw[<->, thick] (-0.3,0) -- (9.3,0);
  \foreach \x in {0,...,9} {\draw (\x,0.1) -- (\x,-0.1); \node[below, font=\scriptsize] at (\x,-0.1) {\x};}
  \draw[line width=3pt, plum] (3,0) -- (4.88,0);
  \draw[line width=3pt, plum, ->] (5.12,0) -- (9.25,0);
  \fill[plum] (3,0) circle (3.5pt);

\end{tikzpicture}

Domain of $k$: \labelbox{3.4cm}{$[\,3, 5) \cup (5, \infty)$}

% ── ROW 3: intercepts — input 0 versus output 0 ─────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Where the graph meets an axis]{Intercepts} &
The \textbf{$y$-intercept} is where the graph crosses the $y$-axis. There the input is 0, so
the point is $(0, f(0))$ --- and a function has at most one. An \textbf{$x$-intercept} is where
the graph meets the $x$-axis. There the output is 0, so solve $f(x) = 0$; each solution $a$ is
a \textbf{zero} of $f$, and the point is $(a, 0)$.
\\
& \begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.6cm, y=0.24cm, >=stealth]
  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-3,-5) grid (5,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (-3,0) -- (5.5,0) node[right] {\scriptsize $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-5) -- (0,6.5) node[above] {\scriptsize $f(x)$};
  \foreach \x in {-2,-1,1,2,3,4} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-4,-2,2,4} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[very thick, plum, <->, samples=60, domain=-1.85:3.85] plot (\x, {\x*\x-2*\x-3});
  \fill[goldacc] (-1,0) circle (3pt);
  \fill[goldacc] (3,0) circle (3pt);
  \fill[redacc] (0,-3) circle (3pt);
  \node[font=\scriptsize, above left] at (-1,0) {$(-1, 0)$};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, above right] at (3,0) {$(3, 0)$};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, left] at (-0.1,-3) {$(0, -3)$};
  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries, anchor=west] at (5.6,4) {$f(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{2pt}
At an $x$-intercept the \labelbox{1.8cm}{output} is 0. \qquad At the $y$-intercept the \labelbox{1.8cm}{input} is 0.

% ── ROW 4: THE CRUX — end behavior in limit notation ────────────────────────
\mainidea[Off the edges]{End Behavior} &
\textbf{End behavior} describes what the \emph{outputs} do as the \emph{inputs} run off the
edges of the graph: to the right ($x \to \infty$) and to the left ($x \to -\infty$). We write
$\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = \infty$ and read it ``as $x$ increases without bound,
$f(x)$ increases without bound.'' The value after the equals sign is $\infty$, $-\infty$, or a
number $b$ --- and then the graph levels off along the \textbf{horizontal asymptote} $y = b$.
\\
& \begin{center}

  \draw[linegray, very thin, step=1] (-3,-5) grid (3,6);
  \draw[->, thick] (-3,0) -- (3.4,0) node[right] {\tiny $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-5) -- (0,6.4);
  \draw[very thick, plum, <->, samples=40, domain=-2.75:2.75] plot (\x, {\x*\x-3});
  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (0,-6.6) {A: $p(x) = x^2 - 3$};
\end{tikzpicture}

For graph C: \quad $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} r(x) = $ \labelbox{1.0cm}{$2$} \qquad
$\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} r(x) = $ \labelbox{1.0cm}{$2$}
\\
& For a \textbf{polynomial}, the \textbf{leading term} --- the term with the highest power ---
decides both ends, because far from 0 it outgrows every other term. In $4x^2 - 2x^3$ at
$x = 100$, the term $-2x^3$ is $-2{,}000{,}000$ while $4x^2$ is only $40{,}000$.
\\
& \begin{center}
