\smallskip
\textbf{Four ideas, one through-line.} Every row of the notes computes the same ratio ---
change in output over change in input --- first from a table, then as a slope, then as a
pattern, then in general on $[x, x + h]$. The difference quotient is the newest and the
hardest, so it gets the last eight minutes of the guided phase and two of the four You Do
problems. If the clock slips, cut \textbf{problem 8} (a second secant line from a formula)
first, then \textbf{problem 10} (the linear table), and protect \textbf{problems 12--14}:
the crux and its check are what You Do problems 17 and 18 and the DeltaMath
\textit{Evaluate $f(x + h)$} and \textit{Difference Quotient} topics cannot be done without.
Never cut the You Do below 14 minutes --- with no exit ticket, circulating it is the only
formative read left before the unit review.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{AP Skill 2.A} --- Identify information from graphical, numerical, analytical, and
verbal representations: compute an average rate of change from a table, a graph, and a
formula. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item The average rate of change on $[a, b]$ is $\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$: change in
        output over change in input (1.2.A.1).
  \item It is the one constant rate that would produce the same total change over the
        interval --- 50 mph does not mean the speedometer read 50 the whole way.
  \item Its units are output units \emph{per} input unit: dollars per month.
  \item \textbf{Second trap:} dividing by the change in \emph{output}. The units come out
        upside down (months per dollar), which is how to catch it.
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 3.A} --- Describe the characteristics of a function: read increasing,
decreasing, and concavity from average rates. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item A positive rate means the two quantities rise together; a negative rate means one
        rises as the other falls (1.2.B.2--3) --- the increasing/decreasing of 2.5, measured.
  \item Over equal consecutive intervals, rates that grow mean concave up; rates that shrink
        mean concave down (1.3.B.3). A linear function's rate never changes (1.3.A.1); a
        quadratic's rates change by the same amount each step (1.3.A.2, 1.3.B.2).
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 3.B / 3.C} --- Apply numerical results in context and support conclusions: the
secant line and its equation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item The average rate of change on $[a, b]$ is the slope of the secant line through
        $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$ (1.3.A.3); its equation is $y - f(a) = m(x - a)$.
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 1.B} --- Express functions in analytically equivalent forms: build
$f(x + h)$ and simplify the difference quotient. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item The difference quotient $\frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$ is the average rate of change on
        $[x, x + h]$. For $3x - 2$ it is 3 (the slope, on every interval); for $x^2$ it is
        $2x + h$.
  \item $f(x + h)$ is a composition (1.3): replace every $x$ with $(x + h)$.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} $f(x + h) = f(x) + h$, and its cousin
        $(x + h)^2 = x^2 + h^2$. Students add $h$ to the output instead of feeding $x + h$
        in as the input. The notes place it where the answers disagree: for $x^2$ at $x = 3$,
        $h = 2$, the three versions give 25, 11, and 13.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.24\linewidth} X @{}}
Average rate of change & On $[a, b]$: \TallMath{\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}}, change in output over change in input, in output units per input unit. \\
Secant line            & A line through two points of a graph; its slope is the average rate of change between them. \\
Point-slope form       & $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$: the line with slope $m$ through $(x_1, y_1)$. \\
Difference quotient    & \TallMath{\frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}}, $h \ne 0$: the average rate of change on $[x, x + h]$. \\
Concave up / down      & (From 2.5.) Over equal intervals, average rates that grow / shrink. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review (5 min)]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: the slope of a line through two points; writing a line in point-slope
    form; squaring a binomial, $(x + 4)^2$ --- the exact move the difference quotient of
    $x^2$ depends on, and the first place the $x^2 + 16$ error shows; building $f(g(x))$ by

\begin{skillbox}[Hook (3 min)]{lilac}
    \textbf{What does ``50 mph'' mean?} On the board: a car passes mile marker 30 at
    1:00\,pm and mile marker 180 at 4:00\,pm.
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item Ask for the average speed. Someone will say 50 mph; make them say where it
              came from --- 150 miles over 3 hours.
        \item Follow up: was the speedometer reading 50 the whole time? (No --- traffic
              lights, a highway stretch.) Then what does 50 \emph{tell} you?
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} 50 is the one steady speed that would cover the same 150 miles
    in the same 3 hours. That is exactly what an average rate of change is --- and today we
    compute it for any function, then draw it, then write it in general.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- I Do / We Do / You Do]{lilac}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{I DO --- Part 1: The Ratio and Its Picture} (10 min)

\textit{Notes rows AVERAGE RATE and SECANT LINE: read each definition, mark up the display,
work problems 1 and 5. Teacher works; students watch and annotate. Nothing is cold-called in
this phase.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the AVERAGE RATE sentences and connect them to the hook: 150 miles over 3 hours.
        Fill the three label boxes yourself --- top is change in \textit{output}, bottom is
        change in \textit{input}, units are \textit{dollars per month}.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 1} aloud from the $B$ table: $\frac{260 - 100}{4 - 0} = 40$
        dollars per month, then say the sentence --- ``over the first four months the balance
        grew \$40 a month on average.''
  \item Read the SECANT LINE sentences. On the graph, trace the dashed line, then the red run
        and rise; fill the boxes: run 3, rise 9, slope 3.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 5}: the rate on $[1, 4]$ is 3, and the secant line is
        $y - 1 = 3(x - 1)$ --- point-slope with the left endpoint.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 2: Rates and Secants} (7 min)

\textit{Problems 2--4 and 6--8. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 2}: 80 dollars per month --- twice the rate of problem 1, the first
        hint that the rates are changing.
  \item \textbf{Problem 3} moves to a formula: evaluate both ends first, then divide.
  \item \textbf{Problem 4 is the second trap}: Jordan divided by the change in output. Ask for
        the units of 0.025 --- ``months per dollar'' gives it away.
  \item \textbf{Problems 6--7} on the graph: a negative rate (decreasing), then a zero rate on a
        curve that is not flat --- net change, not constant. \textbf{Problem 8} is the same
        work from a formula.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 3: What the Rates Say} (5 min)

\textit{Notes row WHAT THE RATES SAY. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one.
The teacher fills the first rate in the table.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fill the $B$ table: 30, 50, 70, 90. Positive, so increasing (2.5); growing, so concave
        up. Point out that they grow by 20 each time --- a quadratic's rates change at a
        constant rate.
  \item \textbf{Problem 9}: rates 8, 6, 4, 2 --- increasing but concave down.
        \textbf{Problem 10}: $-3$ every time --- decreasing and linear.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 4: The Difference Quotient} (8 min)

\textit{Notes row DIFFERENCE QUOTIENT --- the crux. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call
every one. Teacher works problem 11; the class works 12--14.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the definition and mark the display: run $h$, rise $f(x + h) - f(x)$. It is the
        same ratio with letters for endpoints.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 11} with the three steps: $f(x + h) = 3(x + h) - 2$, top $3h$,
        quotient 3. Say it: a line's rate is its slope on any interval.
  \item \textbf{Problem 12}: write $(x + h)^2 = (x + h)(x + h)$ before expanding --- the warm-up's
        item 3 again.
  \item \textbf{Problem 13 is the crux}: compute all three numbers aloud --- 25, 11, 13 --- and
        ask which one is $f(5)$. \textbf{Problem 14} checks $2x + h$ against a plain average
        rate: both are 8.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{YOU DO --- Part 5: Your Turn} (17 min)

\textit{Notes last row, problems 15--18 --- the whole independent phase. Students work alone;
circulate and say nothing a neighbor could say instead.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 15} is an oven table: a rate with units (25 degrees per minute) and a
        concavity read from shrinking rates.
  \item \textbf{Problem 16}: rate 6 on $[1, 3]$ and the secant line $y - 3 = 6(x - 1)$.
  \item \textbf{Problem 17} is the difference quotient of $x^2 + 2x$: $2x + h + 2$.
  \item \textbf{Problem 18} is the finisher's question --- the crux transferred: check 17
        against 16 (both 6), then diagnose a classmate's $f(x + h) = x^2 + 2x + h$, whose
        quotient is 1.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the \textbf{You Do} phase --- the seventeen minutes where students are working
problems 15--18 without you --- and check. The crux is \textbf{problem 13} ($f(x + h)$ versus
$f(x) + h$ versus $x^2 + h^2$); its You Do transfer is \textbf{problem 17}, with
\textbf{problem 18} naming the error outright.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Watch for (the target misconception):} on problem 17, a student writing
        $f(x + h) = x^2 + 2x + h$ or $x^2 + h^2 + 2x + 2h$. Do not correct it --- point to
        their problem 13 and ask, ``What did you put into the slot? Where is the
        $(x + h)$?''
  \item \textbf{Watch for (the second trap):} on problem 15, $\frac{10}{250}$ instead of
        $\frac{250}{10}$. Ask: ``Say your units out loud.''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} forgetting to distribute the minus sign when subtracting $f(x)$
        in problem 17, leaving $2x^2$ on top. Ask: ``Every term left on top should have an
        $h$ in it --- does yours?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} a secant line written with the slope and the point mixed up,
        $y - 1 = 3(x - 3)$. Cold-call: ``Which number is the slope, and which point is on the
        line?''
  \item \textbf{Concavity:} students calling the oven ``concave down because it is going up.''
        Push: ``Increasing is the sign. Concavity is whether the rates grow or shrink.''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Differentiation --- During You Do (17 min)]{redbox}
There is no separate activity sheet: students work the notes' You Do row (problems 15--18)
alone while you circulate. Differentiate by \textit{where you stand}, not by handing out
different paper.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Support.} Sit with a struggling student on \textbf{problem 16} and make them
        write $f(1)$ and $f(3)$ as two separate lines before any fraction. Then have them
        write the fraction with the words ``output'' on top and ``input'' on the bottom
        first. The secant line follows from their own two points.
  \item \textbf{On level.} \textbf{Problem 17} is the difference quotient alone. Do not build
        $f(x + h)$ for them; ask them to write $f(\square) = (\square)^2 + 2(\square)$, fill
        every box with $(x + h)$, and walk away.
  \item \textbf{Extend.} \textbf{Problem 18} is the finisher's question: the check against 16
        rewards a student who trusts the algebra, and the classmate's quotient of 1 is the
        misconception seen from outside. Hand a finisher one more: ``Without computing, what
        is the difference quotient of $5x + 9$? Why?'' Either answer presents in the Debrief.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (5 min)]{redbox}
Whole class, teacher-led, packets still open. Three moves, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Share out the You Do} (3 min) --- one answer per problem, not a full review.
        Problem 15: 25 degrees per minute, concave down. Problem 16: 6, and
        $y - 3 = 6(x - 1)$. Problem 17: $2x + h + 2$. Problem 18: 6 again --- and the
        classmate's 1, with the reason. If a student found the classmate's error in words,
        they present it; it is the lesson's misconception named by a student.
  \item \textbf{Name the headline} (1 min) --- say it, then make the class say it back:
        \textit{an average rate of change is change in output over change in input --- the
        slope of the secant line. The difference quotient is the same ratio on $[x, x + h]$,
        and $f(x + h)$ means replace every $x$, never add $h$.}
  \item \textbf{Point forward} (1 min) --- the DeltaMath assignment, \textit{Lesson 2.6}, and
        its due date: write it on the board and have students copy it into cover row 4. Then:
        this is the last new lesson of Unit 2. The review puts 2.1--2.6 side by side; then the
        test; then the next unit.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Do not} re-teach the difference quotient here or take new questions. If the You Do
showed it did not land, it is the review day's job, not this minute's.

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Items 1 and 2 are the slope and point-slope skills
every secant line in the notes runs on. Item 3 is the one to watch: a student who writes
$(x + 4)^2 = x^2 + 16$ will write $(x + h)^2 = x^2 + h^2$ in problem 12 within the half hour
--- note names, and stand near them in Part 4. Item 4 is composition from Lesson 1.3 on
purpose: ``replace every $x$'' is the exact sentence the difference quotient row uses to build
$f(x + h)$. Item 5 rehearses dividing every term by $h$, so the third step of the difference
quotient is not new when it arrives.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The notes are one Main Ideas / Notes table, four instruction rows and a You Do row; in each
instruction row you read the printed definition, mark up the display, and work the first
problem of the grid (1, 5, the first rate of the $B$ table, 11), and the class works the rest,
every problem cold-called. The AVERAGE RATE row lives on units: make students say ``dollars
per month'' every time, because the units are what catch the second trap in problem 4. The
SECANT LINE row is the same number drawn: trace the run and rise before anyone computes.
Problem 7's zero rate on a curve that is not flat is worth a full minute --- an average rate
measures net change, not constancy. WHAT THE RATES SAY ties back to 2.5: increasing is the
sign, concavity is the pattern.

DIFFERENCE QUOTIENT is the spine and the reason the teacher reserves it for honors elsewhere ---
pitch it gently. Keep it to the three steps on the page, and only to $3x - 2$ and $x^2$. The
display's run $h$ and rise $f(x + h) - f(x)$ are the whole idea; the algebra only follows the
picture. Problem 13 is built so that the three ``versions'' of $f(5)$ disagree (25, 11, 13):
ask for the one that \emph{is} $f(5)$, not for the algebra rule. Problem 14 then shows the
quotient doing what it claims --- $2x + h$ at $x = 3$, $h = 2$ is the plain average rate on
$[3, 5]$.

The You Do row (15--18) is the entire independent phase and carries seventeen minutes. Problem
17 is the crux transferred to $x^2 + 2x$; problem 18 is the crux inverted --- checking it, then
diagnosing someone else's $f(x) + h$. If the clock slips, cut problem 8 in Part 2, not anything
in Part 4.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Debrief]
Five minutes, and on the last new lesson of the unit they matter more than usual: the headline
is the sentence students will carry into the review. The guided phase runs twenty minutes with
new algebra in its last eight, and it will try to borrow from here --- borrow from problem 8
or problem 10 instead; both ideas return in the DeltaMath secant-line and linear-vs-quadratic
topics. Never borrow from the You Do: with no exit ticket, circulating it is the last look you
get before the review day. The share-out is one answer per You Do problem so that a student who
stalled on 16 still hears problem 18's check --- the move the DeltaMath difference quotients
reward.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[AP Practice]
Extra credit, optional, and not a substitute for the DeltaMath assignment --- say so when you
hand out the packet; the cover gives it a $+$ cell outside the total. Multiple-choice answers are B, C, D,
C, A. Item 2 is the most useful one to glance at: (A) is $f(x) + h$ and (B) is
$(x + h)^2 = x^2 + h^2$ --- the two halves of the lesson's misconception --- and (D) forgot
that the second $x$ also gets $(x + h)$. Item 5 is the same misconception one step later: (B)
1 comes from $f(x) + h$, (C) $h$ from $x^2 + h^2$, (D) $2x$ from dropping $h^2$. In item 1, (A)
is the inverted fraction, (C) is $100 \div 5$, and (D) never divided. In the free response,
accept any correct equivalent secant line in (b); part (e) is the stretch --- full credit needs
$8 - 2t - h$ \emph{and} the check that both routes give 4. Credit (c) only if the explanation
separates ``still increasing'' from ``more slowly.''
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
DeltaMath, graded, assigned as \textit{Lesson 2.6}, due on the date you set. \textbf{Evaluate
$f(x + h)$ is the diagnostic}: it tests the day's central distinction, building $f(x + h)$ by
replacing every $x$. A miss there is one of two errors --- $f(x) + h$ (adding $h$ to the
output) or $(x + h)^2 = x^2 + h^2$ (squaring each piece) --- and DeltaMath's wrong-answer
record shows which; the first gets a one-minute check-in at the door on review day with notes
problem 13 open, the second a review-day item on squaring a binomial. What DeltaMath cannot
test is the error \emph{named}: whether a student can say why $f(x) + h$ fails. \textbf{You Do
problem 18} carries that --- read it over shoulders during the You Do. The Difference Quotient
scores then say whether the three steps hold up past one problem; watch for $2(x + h)^2$
expanded as $2x^2 + 2h^2$, the cousin error inside a coefficient. Unit 2 ends here, so the
topics predict the review and the unit test rather than a next lesson: the Average Rate of
Change topics (a fraction inverted is the units trap again) predict whether a student divides
the right way on the test, and Rates of Change in Linear and Quadratic Functions predicts the
sign-versus-pattern read that ties this lesson to 2.5. If either slips, put it on the
review-day warm-up.
\end{teachernote}
