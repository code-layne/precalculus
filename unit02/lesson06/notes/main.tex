% ── ROW 1: average rate of change — table and formula ────────────────────────
\mainidea[Change over an interval]{Average Rate} &
The \textbf{average rate of change} of $f$ on the interval $[a, b]$ is the change in output
divided by the change in input. It is the one constant rate that would produce the same total
change over that interval, and its units are \emph{output units per input unit}.
\[ \text{average rate of change on } [a, b] \;=\; \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} \]
\vspace{-4pt}

% ── ROW 2: the secant line — the picture of the average rate ─────────────────
\mainidea[The picture of it]{Secant Line} &
A \textbf{secant line} is a line through two points on a graph. The secant line through
$(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$ has slope equal to the average rate of change on $[a, b]$, so its
equation in point-slope form is $y - f(a) = m(x - a)$, where $m$ is that average rate.

% ── ROW 3: what the rates say — sign, concavity, linear ──────────────────────
\mainidea[Sign and pattern]{What the Rates Say} &
Over equal-width intervals placed side by side: a \textbf{positive} average rate means the
function is increasing there, a \textbf{negative} one that it is decreasing. If the average
rates \textbf{grow} from one interval to the next, the graph is \textbf{concave up}; if they
\textbf{shrink}, it is \textbf{concave down}; if they \textbf{stay the same}, the function is
\textbf{linear}.
\\

% ── ROW 4: THE CRUX — the difference quotient and f(x + h) ───────────────────
\mainidea[The rate on any interval]{Difference Quotient} &
Shrink the interval to $[x, x + h]$: it starts at $x$ and is $h$ wide. The average rate of
change over it is the \textbf{difference quotient},
$\dfrac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$, with $h \ne 0$.
To build $f(x + h)$, compose as in Lesson 1.3: replace \emph{every} $x$ in the rule with
$(x + h)$. \textbf{$f(x + h)$ runs the rule on the input $x + h$; $f(x) + h$ runs it on $x$ and
then adds $h$ --- they are different numbers.}

\vspace{3pt}
\stepnum{1}\ Build $f(x + h)$ and simplify. \quad \stepnum{2}\ Subtract $f(x)$. \quad
\stepnum{3}\ Divide by $h$ --- every term left on top has a factor of $h$.
\\
