\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=18pt]

\item State the domain and range of $f(x) = 3\log_4 x$. Then write the equation of the
      vertical asymptote.

\vspace{6pt}
Domain: \ans{$x > 0$, i.e., $(0, \infty)$} \qquad Range: \ans{all reals, $(-\infty, \infty)$}

\vspace{8pt}
Vertical asymptote: $x = $ \ans{$0$}

\begin{teachernote}
The factor $a = 3$ is a vertical dilation and does not affect the domain, range, or asymptote.
Students who write a shifted domain have confused this with a horizontal translation, which is
covered in Lesson 2.12.
\end{teachernote}

\vspace{10pt}

\item For $f(x) = \log_2 x$, complete each statement.

\vspace{6pt}
As $x \to 0^+$, \quad $f(x) \to $ \ans{$-\infty$}

\vspace{8pt}
As $x \to \infty$, \quad $f(x) \to $ \ans{$+\infty$}

\vspace{8pt}
$f$ is \hspace{0.3cm} \ans{increasing} \hspace{0.3cm} (increasing / decreasing) on its domain.

\vspace{8pt}
The graph of $f$ is \hspace{0.3cm} \ans{concave down} \hspace{0.3cm} (concave up / concave down).

\begin{teachernote}
Students who write $+\infty$ for the left limit ($x \to 0^+$) are likely confusing $\log_2 x$
with $\log_{1/2} x$. Remind them: base $b = 2 > 1$ means the log is increasing, so as $x$
approaches the asymptote from the right, outputs go to $-\infty$, not $+\infty$.
\end{teachernote}

\end{enumerate}
