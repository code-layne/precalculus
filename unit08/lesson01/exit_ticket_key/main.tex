\begin{notesbox}{Parametric Function Check --- Key}

The parametric function $f(t) = (2t - 1,\; 3 - t)$ is defined for
$0 \le t \le 3$.

\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{1.}\quad Complete the table of values.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$t$ & $x(t) = 2t - 1$ & $y(t) = 3 - t$ & $(x,\; y)$ \\
\hline
$0$ & \ans{$-1$} & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$(-1,\;3)$} \\
\hline
$1$ & \ans{$1$}  & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$(1,\;2)$}  \\
\hline
$2$ & \ans{$3$}  & \ans{$1$} & \ans{$(3,\;1)$}  \\
\hline
$3$ & \ans{$5$}  & \ans{$0$} & \ans{$(5,\;0)$}  \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{2.}\quad Start and end points:

Start point: \ans{$(-1,\,3)$} \qquad End point: \ans{$(5,\,0)$}

\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{3.}\quad When does the curve cross the $y$-axis?

\ans{Set $x(t) = 2t - 1 = 0$. Solving: $2t = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; t = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
Then $y\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 3 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{5}{2}$.
The curve crosses the $y$-axis at $\left(0,\,\tfrac{5}{2}\right)$.}

\begin{teachernote}
  Sort exit tickets into three piles: (1) correct table + correct
  crossing point, (2) correct table but did not solve for crossing,
  (3) table errors. Use this sorting to plan the opening of Lesson 4.2.
  Students in group (3) likely need another pass at evaluating
  component functions at a non-integer $t$-value.
\end{teachernote}

\end{notesbox}
