\begin{notesbox}{Direction of Motion and Slope Check --- Key}

A particle moves with $f(t) = (2t - t^2,\; t^2 - 4t + 3)$ for $0 \le t \le 4$.

\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{1.}\quad At $t = 0.5$, is the particle moving right or left?
Up or down?

\vspace{6pt}
$x(0) = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad $x(1) = 2 - 1 = $ \ans{$1$} \qquad
$x(t)$ is \ans{increasing} near $t = 0.5$ $\Rightarrow$ moving \ans{right}

\vspace{8pt}
$y(0) = $ \ans{$3$} \qquad $y(1) = 1 - 4 + 3 = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad
$y(t)$ is \ans{decreasing} near $t = 0.5$ $\Rightarrow$ moving \ans{down}

\vspace{12pt}
\textbf{2.}\quad ARC of $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ on $[0, 3]$; slope of curve.

\vspace{6pt}
$x(0) = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad $x(3) = 6 - 9 = $ \ans{$-3$} \qquad
$\Delta x = $ \ans{$-3$}

\vspace{6pt}
$y(0) = $ \ans{$3$} \qquad $y(3) = 9 - 12 + 3 = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad
$\Delta y = $ \ans{$-3$}

\vspace{8pt}
Slope of curve $= \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{\ans{-3}}{\ans{-3}} = $ \ans{$1$}

\begin{teachernote}
  Common error: students compute $\Delta y / \Delta t$ or $\Delta x / \Delta t$ and report
  one of those as ``slope.'' Redirect: ``Slope of the curve uses the ratio of the change
  in the $y$-coordinate to the change in the $x$-coordinate, not the change in $t$.''
  Note that the slope here is $1$ even though both $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$ are $-3$;
  students should verify that $(-3)/(-3) = 1$, not $-3$.
\end{teachernote}

\end{notesbox}
