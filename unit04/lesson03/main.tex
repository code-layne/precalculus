\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{AP Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{3.A} --- Identify key characteristics of exponential functions and connect
multiple representations. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item General form $f(x) = ab^x$: initial value $a$, base $b > 0$, $b \ne 1$.
        Growth when $a > 0, b > 1$; decay when $a > 0, 0 < b < 1$. (EK 2.3.A.1)
  \item Outputs are proportional over equal-length input intervals; function is strictly
        monotone, always concave up or always concave down; no extrema or inflection
        points on open intervals. (EK 2.3.A.3)
  \item End behavior: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} ab^x = \pm\infty$ or $0$.
        Horizontal asymptote at $y = 0$. (EK 2.3.A.5)
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{navy}}p{0.27\linewidth} X @{}}
Exponential function   & A function of the form $f(x) = ab^x$ with $a \ne 0$, $b > 0$, $b \ne 1$. \\
Initial value          & The value $a = f(0)$; the $y$-intercept of the exponential function. \\
Base                   & The constant $b$; the multiplicative factor applied for each unit increase in input. \\
Exponential growth     & When $a > 0$ and $b > 1$; outputs increase without bound as $x \to \infty$. \\
Exponential decay      & When $a > 0$ and $0 < b < 1$; outputs approach $0$ as $x \to \infty$. \\
Concave up / down      & Graph curves upward (like a bowl) or downward; exponential functions are always one or the other. \\
End behavior           & The behavior of $f(x)$ as $x \to \infty$ or $x \to -\infty$, described with limit notation. \\
Horizontal asymptote   & A horizontal line the graph approaches but never reaches; $y = 0$ for $f(x) = ab^x$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{sky}
    Spiral review (warm-up) covers: identifying constant output ratios from a table (Lesson 2.2),
    writing an exponential function given initial value and ratio, and reviewing linear function
    writing from two points (Lesson 1.1). Students work three short problems; this activates
    the proportionality idea central to EK 2.3.A.3 and distinguishes exponential from linear tables.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{sky}
Pose the \textbf{doubling penny problem:}

\medskip
\textit{``Suppose you receive 1 penny on Day 1, 2 pennies on Day 2, 4 pennies on Day 3, and so on,
doubling every day. How much total money would you have after 30 days? Write your guess before
calculating.''}

\medskip
After students commit to a guess, reveal: $2^{30} - 1 \approx 1{,}073{,}741{,}823$ pennies
$\approx \$10.7$ million. Ask: \textbf{``What kind of function models this?''} and
\textbf{``What does Day 0 look like? What's the base?''} This motivates $f(x) = 1 \cdot 2^x$
and the general form $ab^x$.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{sky}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Part 1 (15 min): General Form $f(x) = ab^x$.}

Identify $a$ (initial value, $y$-intercept) and $b$ (base, common ratio) from an equation and
from a table. Classify as growth ($b > 1$) or decay ($0 < b < 1$). Use three representations:
(1) equation, (2) table showing constant ratios, (3) verbal description.

Model: given $f(x) = 3 \cdot 2^x$, confirm $a = 3$, $b = 2$, growth; build a table for
$x = 0,1,2,3$ and verify ratios $= 2$. Given table with ratios $= 0.5$, write $f(x) = 8(0.5)^x$.

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 2 (15 min): Key Characteristics.}

Domain: all real numbers (EK 2.3.A.2). Range (for $a > 0$): $(0, \infty)$ with horizontal
asymptote $y = 0$. Monotonicity: strictly increasing if $b > 1$, decreasing if $0 < b < 1$ ---
never has extrema. Concavity: always concave up when $a > 0$ --- no inflection points.

\medskip
\textbf{Part 3 (10 min): End Behavior \& Detecting Exponential (EK 2.3.A.4/A.5).}

Use limit notation: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} 3\cdot 2^x = \infty$ and
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} 3\cdot 2^x = 0$.

EK 2.3.A.4: if $g(x) = f(x) + k$ and outputs of $g$ look non-proportional, subtract $k$ to
recover $f$, then check ratios of $f$. If ratios are constant, $f$ is exponential.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Cold-call after Part 1: ``If $b = 0.8$, is this growth or decay? How do you know?''
        Expect: decay because $0 < 0.8 < 1$.
  \item During Part 1 table work: watch for students who add outputs instead of finding ratios.
  \item Cold-call after Part 2: ``Can an exponential function have a maximum value? Why not?''
        Expect: no, because it is always increasing or always decreasing.
  \item During Part 3: confirm students apply limit notation correctly; watch for confusing
        $\lim_{x\to\infty}$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty}$ when $0 < b < 1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Given three explicit equations $f(x) = ab^x$; read off $a$
        and $b$, classify growth/decay, state domain, $y$-intercept, and both limits.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Given a table; verify exponential by computing output ratios,
        identify $a$ and $b$, write the equation, state the range, interpret base in context.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Given $g(x) = f(x) + 4$ with non-proportional outputs;
        subtract 4, verify constant ratios, write $f(x) = ab^x$, then write $g(x)$; explain
        why adding a constant disrupts the ratio test (EK 2.3.A.4).
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
Exit ticket (3 questions, 1 page): (1) identify $a$, $b$, and growth/decay for
$f(x) = 3\cdot(0.5)^x$; (2) state $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} 5\cdot 2^x$ and
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} 5\cdot 2^x$; (3) write $f(x)$ in $ab^x$ form from the
table with outputs 6, 18, 54, 162.

Collect and sort: students missing item 1 need re-teaching of form identification (LO 2.3.A.1);
missing item 2 need end behavior practice (LO 2.3.A.5); missing item 3 need table-to-equation
work (LO 2.3.A.2).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
Homework (6 problems): identify $a$, $b$, growth/decay, and range from equations; determine
whether a table represents exponential, linear, or neither; write $f(x) = ab^x$ from a table;
state limits for two different exponential functions; AP-style MC on horizontal asymptote and
monotonicity; extension explaining why $b = 1$ is excluded.

Preview of Lesson 2.4: transformations of exponential functions --- how $f(x) = ab^{x-h} + k$
shifts the asymptote and changes domain/range, connecting today's base characteristics to
transformed graphs.
\end{skillbox}
