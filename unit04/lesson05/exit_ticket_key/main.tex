\begin{notesbox}{Exit Ticket --- Key}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=8pt]

\item The equation $4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$ describes an ellipse.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=6pt]
    \item[(a)] For $x = 3$: \quad $4(9) + 9y^2 = 36 \implies 36 + 9y^2 = 36 \implies 9y^2 = 0 \implies y = 0$

    \vspace{0.3in}
    $y = $ \ans{$0$ (only one value)}

    \item[(b)] For $x = 0$: \quad $9y^2 = 36 \implies y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2$

    \vspace{0.3in}
    $y = $ \ans{$\pm 2$}
\end{enumerate}

\item Consider moving along the \textbf{upper half} of the ellipse $4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$
from the point $(3,\, 0)$ toward the point $(0,\, 2)$.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=6pt]
    \item[(a)] As $x$ decreases from 3 to 0, does $y$ increase or decrease?

    \vspace{0.1in}
    \ans{$y$ increases (from 0 to 2).}

    \item[(b)] Is the co-variation between $x$ and $y$ positive or negative? Explain.

    \vspace{0.3in}
    \ans{Negative. As $x$ decreases, $y$ increases --- they change in opposite directions, so $\Delta y/\Delta x < 0$.}
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Q1(a): $(3,0)$ is on the ellipse; at $x=3$ the ellipse just touches the $x$-axis (one $y$-value, not two). Some students may be surprised --- this is a good discussion point.\\[3pt]
Q2: The key insight is that ``opposite directions'' means negative co-variation even when $x$ is decreasing. $\Delta x < 0$ and $\Delta y > 0$, so $\Delta y/\Delta x < 0$. Award full credit for any correct explanation referencing opposite directions or the sign of the ratio.
\end{teachernote}
