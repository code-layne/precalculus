\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.6.B --- Residuals, Error, and Model Validation (EK 2.6.B.1 and B.2)}
\textbf{Residual formula:} $e = y_{\text{actual}} - \hat{y}_{\text{predicted}}$

\medskip
\begin{tabular}{l|l}
If $e > 0$ (positive residual): the model is a(n) \blank{4cm} & the actual value is \emph{above} the prediction. \\[4pt]
If $e < 0$ (negative residual): the model is a(n) \blank{4cm} & the actual value is \emph{below} the prediction. \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Residual plot:} Plot the point $(x_i,\, e_i)$ for each data value.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item Residuals \textbf{scattered without pattern} $\Rightarrow$ model is \blank{4cm} for the data.
  \item Residuals showing a \textbf{curved} or \textbf{systematic pattern} $\Rightarrow$ model is \blank{4cm}.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Example:} An exponential model gives $\hat{y} = 2 \cdot 3^x$.

\vspace{0.04in}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
$x$ & Actual $y$ & Predicted $\hat{y}$ & Residual $e = y - \hat{y}$ \\ \hline
0 & 2.5 & 2 & \blank{3cm} \\
1 & 7   & 6 & \blank{3cm} \\
2 & 17  & 18 & \blank{3cm} \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
Context note: if a model systematically \blank{3cm} the actual values, and this leads to
a safety risk, you may prefer a model that \blank{3cm} instead.
\end{notesbox}
