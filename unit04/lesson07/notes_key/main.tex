Three companies each report 5 years of annual revenue (\$thousands):

\medskip
\begin{center}

\textbf{Year} & \textbf{Company A} & \textbf{Company B} & \textbf{Company C} \\ \hline
1 & 10 & 10 & 10 \\
2 & 14 & 16 & 20 \\
3 & 18 & 26 & 40 \\
4 & 22 & 40 & 80 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\medskip
\textit{Which company's growth looks linear? Quadratic? Exponential?} \\
A: \ans{linear (constant $\Delta_1 = 4$)} \quad B: \ans{quadratic (constant $\Delta_2$)} \quad C: \ans{exponential (constant ratio $= 2$)}
\end{hookbox}

\vspace{0.05in}

\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.6.A --- Reading Data Structure to Choose a Model (EK 2.6.A.1)}
Use the data table below to compute first differences, second differences, and ratios.

\vspace{0.05in}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.8}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c|c}
$x$ & $y$ & $\Delta_1 = y_{\text{next}}-y$ & $\Delta_2 = \Delta_{1,\text{next}}-\Delta_1$ & Ratio $= y_{\text{next}}/y$ \\ \hline
0 &  3 & \ans{3}  & \ans{3}  & \ans{2} \\
1 &  6 & \ans{6}  & \ans{6}  & \ans{2} \\
2 & 12 & \ans{12} & ---      & \ans{2} \\
3 & 24 & --- & --- & --- \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Key:} Constant $\Delta_1$ $\Rightarrow$ \ans{linear} \quad
Constant $\Delta_2$ $\Rightarrow$ \ans{quadratic} \quad
Constant ratio $\Rightarrow$ \ans{exponential}

\medskip
\textbf{Contextual clues (EK 2.6.A.2):} If the data is described as ``growing faster and faster
each period,'' this suggests \ans{exponential} growth. Use both the numbers and the context to decide.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.05in}

\begin{notesbox}{LO 2.6.B --- Residuals, Error, and Model Validation (EK 2.6.B.1 and B.2)}
\textbf{Residual formula:} $e = y_{\text{actual}} - \hat{y}_{\text{predicted}}$

\medskip
\begin{tabular}{l|l}
If $e > 0$ (positive residual): the model is a(n) \ans{underestimate} & the actual value is \emph{above} the prediction. \\[4pt]
If $e < 0$ (negative residual): the model is a(n) \ans{overestimate} & the actual value is \emph{below} the prediction. \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Residual plot:} Plot the point $(x_i,\, e_i)$ for each data value.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item Residuals \textbf{scattered without pattern} $\Rightarrow$ model is \ans{appropriate} for the data.
  \item Residuals showing a \textbf{curved} or \textbf{systematic pattern} $\Rightarrow$ model is \ans{inappropriate}.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Example:} An exponential model gives $\hat{y} = 2 \cdot 3^x$.

\vspace{0.04in}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
$x$ & Actual $y$ & Predicted $\hat{y}$ & Residual $e = y - \hat{y}$ \\ \hline
0 & 2.5 & 2  & \ans{$+0.5$} \\
1 & 7   & 6  & \ans{$+1$} \\
2 & 17  & 18 & \ans{$-1$} \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
Context note: if a model systematically \ans{underestimates} the actual values, and this leads to
a safety risk, you may prefer a model that \ans{overestimates} instead.
\end{notesbox}
