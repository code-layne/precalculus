\begin{teachernote}
Tier R: focus students on sign interpretation of error. Tier A: circulate while students
compute differences/ratios --- accept ratios in the range 2.0--2.2 as ``approximately constant.''
Tier E: scoring rubric: 1 pt each for (1) identifying increasing pattern in linear residuals,
(2) noting no pattern in exponential, (3) correct model choice, (4) valid real-world reason,
(5) complete 3--4 sentence justification.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{scenariobox}[Choosing the Best Model for Online Store Sales]{sky}
\small
An online store tracked monthly sales (in \$thousands) for five months. Three regression
models were fit to the data. In each tier, you will analyze the models and determine which
one is most appropriate for the data and the context.

\medskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c|c}
\textbf{Month ($x$)} & \textbf{Actual $y$} & \textbf{Linear $\hat{y}_L$} & \textbf{Quadratic $\hat{y}_Q$} & \textbf{Exponential $\hat{y}_E$} \\
\hline
1 &  4 & 3.4  & 3.8  & 3.6 \\
2 &  8 & 7.8  & 7.2  & 7.9 \\
3 & 17 & 12.2 & 14.6 & 17.4 \\
4 & 36 & 16.6 & 26.0 & 38.2 \\
5 & 75 & 21.0 & 41.4 & 83.9 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=10pt]
  \item Error (Linear): $17 - 12.2 = $ \ans{$+4.8$}

        Error (Quadratic): $17 - 14.6 = $ \ans{$+2.4$}

        Error (Exponential): $17 - 17.4 = $ \ans{$-0.4$}

  \item Which model has the smallest absolute error at Month 3? \ans{Exponential ($|{-0.4}| = 0.4$)}

  \item Circle the model type that best matches ``sales more than doubled every month'':

        \hspace{2cm} Linear \hspace{2cm} Quadratic \hspace{2cm} \ans{\textbf{Exponential}}

  \item At $x = 5$: error $= 75 - 83.9 = -8.9$ (negative), so the exponential model is a(n)
        \ans{overestimate} because the error is \ans{negative}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier A --- Apply},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=10pt]
  \item
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
        \begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
        $x$ & Actual $y$ & $\Delta_1$ & Ratio \\ \hline
        1 &  4 & \ans{4}  & \ans{2.0} \\
        2 &  8 & \ans{9}  & \ans{2.125} \\
        3 & 17 & \ans{19} & \ans{$\approx 2.12$} \\
        4 & 36 & \ans{39} & \ans{$\approx 2.08$} \\
        5 & 75 & --- & --- \\
        \end{tabular}

  \item Most strongly suggested model: \ans{exponential}

        Explanation: \ansline{The successive ratios are approximately constant (near 2.1), signaling exponential growth.}

  \item $R^2$ values depend on student technology output. Expect exponential $R^2 > 0.99$.

  \item Sample answer: \ansline{The exponential model is most appropriate because the ratios are nearly constant ($\approx 2.1$), the $R^2$ value is highest for exponential regression, and the context states that sales doubled each month --- a hallmark of exponential growth.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.1in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40,
    title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extend},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm]

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=10pt]
  \item Pattern in linear residuals: \ansline{The residuals increase sharply (0.6, 0.2, 4.8, 19.4, 54.0) --- a clear upward curve, not random.}

  \item Exponential residuals: \ansline{The residuals alternate sign and stay small ($+0.4, +0.1, -0.4, -2.2, -8.9$); no clear curve, though they grow slightly at the end.}

  \item Based on the residual plot criterion, the more appropriate model is: \ans{exponential}

  \item Sample real-world reason: \ansline{An overestimate of future sales helps the store plan for extra inventory, reducing the risk of stockouts.}

  \item Sample full justification: \ansline{The exponential model is the most appropriate choice for three reasons.\\[3pt]
  First, the residuals from the linear model show a strongly increasing pattern (from 0.6 to 54.0), indicating the linear model systematically underestimates --- the wrong function type.\\[3pt]
  Second, the exponential residuals show no clear pattern and remain relatively small, satisfying the residual-plot criterion for a good fit.\\[3pt]
  Third, the contextual clue that ``sales more than doubled every month'' is the defining property of exponential growth, confirming the model choice.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
