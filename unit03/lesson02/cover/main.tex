\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can name the degree, the leading term, the leading coefficient, and the constant
        term of a polynomial function --- even when it is not written in standard form or is
        given in factored form.
  \item I can describe the end behavior of a polynomial function from its degree and the sign
        of its leading coefficient, in words and in limit notation, and say whether it has an
        absolute maximum or minimum.
  \item I can state the most turning points ($n - 1$) and the most real zeros ($n$) a
        polynomial of degree $n$ can have, and use a graph's turning points and
        $x$-intercepts to find the least degree it could have.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
Far from zero, one term of a polynomial outgrows all the others: the \textbf{leading term},
the term with the highest power. Its \textbf{degree} and the \textbf{sign} of its coefficient
decide where both ends of the graph go. The degree also sets a ceiling --- at most $n - 1$
\textbf{turning points} and at most $n$ \textbf{real zeros}. A ceiling, not a count: a graph
may have fewer. And the leading term is the highest power, not the term written first.
\end{remindbox}
