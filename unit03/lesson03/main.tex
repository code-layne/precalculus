\textbf{MP 2.A} --- Identify information from graphical, numerical, analytical,

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item A vertical asymptote occurs at $x = a$ when the denominator is zero at $a$ and

        $\lim_{x \to a^+} r(x) = \pm\infty$ and $\lim_{x \to a^-} r(x) = \pm\infty$.

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{navy}}p{0.26\linewidth} X @{}}
Rational function      & A function of the form $r(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ where $p$ and $q$ are polynomials and $q \not\equiv 0$. \\
Vertical asymptote     & A vertical line $x = a$ near which $|r(x)|$ increases without bound. \\
One-sided limit        & The value $r(x)$ approaches as $x$ tends to $a$ from only one side. \\
$\lim_{x \to a^+} r(x)$ & Limit as $x$ approaches $a$ from values \emph{greater} than $a$. \\
$\lim_{x \to a^-} r(x)$ & Limit as $x$ approaches $a$ from values \emph{less} than $a$. \\
Multiplicity condition & A VA at $x = a$ occurs when the multiplicity of $a$ in the denominator exceeds its multiplicity in the numerator. \\
\end{tabularx}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{sky}
A bacterial culture is placed in a dish near a drain. The concentration of bacteria (in thousands
per mL) near the drain is modeled by $C(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 3}$, where $x$ is distance in cm from
the drain. What happens as $x \to 3$?

\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$    & 2.9 & 2.99 & 2.999 & 3.001 & 3.01 & 3.1 \\
\hline
$C(x)$ & $-10$ & $-100$ & $-1{,}000$ & $1{,}000$ & $100$ & $10$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textit{Discussion:} Why can't we evaluate $C(3)$? What does the output doing as $x \to 3$
tell us about the graph? What does $C(x) \to +\infty$ from one side and $C(x) \to -\infty$
from the other side mean physically?
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{sky}
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{Part 1: What Causes a Vertical Asymptote?} (10 min)

A rational function $r(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ has a \textbf{vertical asymptote at $x = a$} when:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $q(a) = 0$ (denominator is zero at $a$), \textbf{AND}
  \item $p(a) \ne 0$ (numerator is \emph{not} zero at $a$).
\end{itemize}
Intuition: near $x = a$, the denominator $\approx 0$ while the numerator stays nonzero, so
the fraction's magnitude grows without bound.

\textbf{Worked Example 1.} $r(x) = \dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Denominator zero: $x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3$.
  \item Numerator at $x = 3$: $3 + 2 = 5 \ne 0$.
  \item \textbf{Vertical asymptote: $x = 3$.}
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{Part 2: Multiplicity Rule (EK 1.9.A.1)} (10 min)

A VA also occurs at $x = a$ when the multiplicity of $a$ in the denominator is
\emph{greater} than its multiplicity in the numerator.

\textbf{Worked Example 2.} $r(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $x = 1$: numerator mult.\ 1, denominator mult.\ 2. Since $2 > 1$: \textbf{VA at $x = 1$}.
  \item $x = -2$: numerator value $= -3 \ne 0$: \textbf{VA at $x = -2$}.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Preview (Lesson 1.10):} If numerator multiplicity $\ge$ denominator multiplicity at $a$,
there is a \textbf{hole}, not a VA.

\end{multicols}

\textbf{Part 3: One-Sided Limits Near a Vertical Asymptote (EK 1.9.A.2)} (10 min)

For $r(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 2}$, examine values approaching $x = 2$ from both sides:

\smallskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$    & 1.9 & 1.99 & 1.999 & 2.001 & 2.01 & 2.1 \\
\hline
$r(x)$ & $-10$ & $-100$ & $-1{,}000$ & $1{,}000$ & $100$ & $10$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

Write conclusions in limit notation:
\[
  \lim_{x \to 2^-} r(x) = -\infty
  \qquad\qquad
  \lim_{x \to 2^+} r(x) = +\infty
\]

\textit{Sign analysis:} when $x > 2$, $(x-2) > 0$, so $r(x) > 0 \to +\infty$; when $x < 2$,
$(x-2) < 0$, so $r(x) < 0 \to -\infty$.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
While students work on guided notes and practice, circulate and check:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error:} Declaring every denominator zero a VA without checking the numerator.
        Cold-call: ``If the numerator is also zero at $x = a$, what happens instead of a VA?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} Writing $\lim_{x\to a} r(x) = \infty$ without specifying direction
        ($+$ or $-$). Emphasize that the two one-sided limits may differ in sign.
  \item \textbf{Notation check:} VA is a vertical line, so it must be $x = a$, not $y = a$.
  \item \textbf{Cold-call:} ``For $r(x) = \frac{1}{(x-1)^2}$, does $r(x)$ go to $+\infty$ or
        $-\infty$ on both sides of $x = 1$? Why?'' (Both $+\infty$ because $(x-1)^2 > 0$.)
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
Students work on \textbf{Group Activity: Analyzing Pressure Gauges} in leveled tiers.
Same four rational functions; increasing demand at each tier.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier R (Remediate):} Set denominators equal to zero; identify VAs (assuming no
        common numerator zeros). Write VA equations $x = a$.
  \item \textbf{Tier A (Apply):} Factor numerators and denominators; distinguish VAs from potential
        holes; write correct VAs and flag holes for Lesson 1.10.
  \item \textbf{Tier E (Extend):} Explain why $x = 1$ is a hole (not a VA) in $r_3$; determine
        $\lim_{x\to -2^+}$ and $\lim_{x\to -2^-}$ for $r_4(x) = x/(x+2)^3$ using sign analysis.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (last 5--7 minutes, individual, collected):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For $r(x) = (x-1)/((x+2)(x-4))$: find denominator zeros, confirm each gives a VA (numerator
        is nonzero at both), and write the VA equations.
  \item Given a table showing $r(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to 3^+$ and $r(x) \to -\infty$ as
        $x \to 3^-$, write the corresponding one-sided limits in proper notation.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Assessment note:} Look for correct VA identification (not just denominator zeros) and
proper one-sided limit notation with direction superscripts ($+$ and $-$).
\end{skillbox}

interpreting limit notation, and an AP-style MC item on the multiplicity rule.

\textbf{Preview:} Lesson 1.10 examines \textit{holes} (removable discontinuities) --- what

The multiplicity preview in today's lesson sets this up directly.
\end{skillbox}
