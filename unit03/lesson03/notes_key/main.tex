\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Zero of a polynomial:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A number $a$ with $p(a) = 0$; for a real $a$, the point $(a, 0)$ is an $x$-intercept.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Root:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A solution of the equation $p(x) = 0$ --- the same number as a zero of $p$.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Multiplicity:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{The number of times the factor $(x - a)$ appears in the factored form.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Fundamental Theorem of Algebra:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{A degree-$n$ polynomial has exactly $n$ complex zeros, counting multiplicity.}\\[3pt]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{plum}{Conjugate pair:}}\\[1pt]
\ansline{$a + bi$ and $a - bi$; with real coefficients, non-real zeros come in these pairs.}
\end{vocabbox}

% ── ROW 1: zero = root = x-intercept = linear factor ─────────────────────────
\mainidea[One fact, four ways]{Zeros} &
A number $a$ is a \textbf{zero} of a polynomial $p$ when $p(a) = 0$. For a real number $a$,
four statements say exactly the same thing: $a$ is a zero of $p$; $a$ is a \textbf{root}
(a solution) of the equation $p(x) = 0$; the point $(a, 0)$ is an \textbf{$x$-intercept} of the
graph; and $(x - a)$ is a \textbf{linear factor} of $p(x)$. Watch the sign: the zero $-2$ goes
with the factor $(x + 2)$.
\\
& \begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.95cm, y=0.12cm, >=stealth, baseline=(current bounding box.center)]
  \draw[linegray, very thin] (-3,-12) grid[xstep=1, ystep=4] (5,12);
  \draw[->, thick] (-3,0) -- (5.4,0) node[right] {\small $x$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,-12) -- (0,13) node[above] {\small $p(x)$};
  \foreach \x in {-2,-1,1,2,3,4} \node[below, font=\scriptsize] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {-8,8} \node[left, font=\scriptsize] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \begin{scope}
    \clip (-3,-12) rectangle (5,12);
    \draw[very thick, plum, domain=-2.5:4.55, samples=120, smooth]
      plot (\x, {(\x+2)*(\x-1)*(\x-4)});
  \end{scope}
  \foreach \x in {-2,1,4} \fill[plum] (\x,0) circle (2.5pt);
  \node[font=\small, fill=white, inner sep=1pt] at (3.2,9.5) {$p(x) = (x + 2)(x - 1)(x - 4)$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\hspace{0.4cm}

\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Zero} $a$ & \textbf{$x$-intercept} & \textbf{Factor} \\ \hline
$1$ & $(1, 0)$ & $(x - 1)$ \\ \hline
$-2$ & \ans{$(-2, 0)$} & \ans{$(x + 2)$} \\ \hline
$4$ & \ans{$(4, 0)$} & \ans{$(x - 4)$} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

% ── ROW 2: multiplicity and the count ────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Counting zeros]{Multiplicity} &
If the factor $(x - a)$ appears exactly $k$ times in the factored form, then $a$ is a zero of
\textbf{multiplicity} $k$. The \textbf{Fundamental Theorem of Algebra} says a polynomial of
degree $n$ has \emph{exactly} $n$ complex zeros, when each zero is counted as many times as its
multiplicity. (Every real number is complex too: $3 = 3 + 0i$.) A constant factor, like the 2
below, makes no zero.
\\
& \begin{center}
{\large $f(x) = 2(x - 3)^2(x + 1)^3$}

\vspace{6pt}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}%
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Factor} & \textbf{Zero} & \textbf{Multiplicity} \\ \hline
$(x - 3)^2$ & $3$ & $2$ \\ \hline
$(x + 1)^3$ & \ans{$-1$} & \ans{3} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{6pt}
Zeros counted with multiplicity: \ $2 + 3 = $ \ans{5} \ $=$ the degree of $f$.
\end{center}
\\
& \notesprompt{Give the degree, then each zero with its multiplicity. Check the count.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{5}{$g(x) = (x + 4)(x - 2)^3$}{2.0cm}{Degree $1 + 3 = 4$. Zero $-4$, multiplicity 1; zero 2, multiplicity 3. Count: $1 + 3 = 4$, exactly the degree.} &
\pcell{6}{$h(x) = -3x^2(x - 5)(x + 1)^2$}{2.0cm}{Degree $2 + 1 + 2 = 5$. Zeros: 0 (mult.\ 2), 5 (mult.\ 1), $-1$ (mult.\ 2). The $-3$ is a constant: no zero.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}

% ── ROW 3: complex zeros come in conjugate pairs ─────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Complex zeros]{Conjugate Pairs} &
Some zeros are not real. A non-real zero $a + bi$ (with $b \ne 0$) is not an $x$-intercept, so
the graph cannot show it --- but it still counts toward the degree. When every coefficient of
$p$ is real, \textbf{non-real zeros come in conjugate pairs}: if $a + bi$ is a zero, then
$a - bi$ is a zero too.
\\
& \begin{center}
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.4\linewidth}
\centering

  \foreach \x in {-2,-1,1,2} \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,0) {\x};
  \foreach \y in {4,8} \node[left, font=\tiny] at (0,\y) {\y};
  \draw[very thick, plum, domain=-2.2:2.2, samples=60, smooth] plot (\x, {\x*\x+4});
  \node[font=\scriptsize, right] at (0.9,8.4) {$y = x^2 + 4$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
The graph of $y = x^2 + 4$ never meets the $x$-axis. Its zeros:
\[ x^2 + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = -4 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm\sqrt{-4} \]
Two zeros, and no $x$-intercepts:

\smallskip
\labelbox{1.4cm}{$2i$} \ and \ \labelbox{1.4cm}{$-2i$} \ --- a conjugate pair.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

% ── ROW 4: THE CRUX — multiplicity on the graph: cross or touch ──────────────
\mainidea[Multiplicity on the graph]{Cross or Touch} &
At a real zero of \textbf{odd} multiplicity the graph \textbf{crosses} the $x$-axis and $p$
changes sign: multiplicity 1 cuts straight through; 3, 5, \ldots\ flatten out as they cross. At
a real zero of \textbf{even} multiplicity the graph \textbf{touches} the axis and turns back ---
it is tangent to the $x$-axis --- and $p$ has the \emph{same} sign on both sides.
\\

\textbf{Not every zero crosses. The three graphs differ only in the exponent on $(x - 2)$:
odd crosses and the sign changes; even touches, turns back, and the sign stays the same.}}}

\textbf{In your own words:} how can $P_2$ have a zero at $x = 2$ and still be positive on both
sides of it?
\writespace{1.3cm}{$(x - 2)^2$ is never negative, so near 2 the sign of $P_2$ comes from $(x + 1)$ alone, which is positive. $P_2$ reaches 0 at $x = 2$ and turns back up.}
