\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item I can write a polynomial in factored form from its zeros: each zero $a$ gives a
        factor $(x - a)$, repeated as many times as its multiplicity, with the leading
        coefficient found from one more point --- given in words or read off a graph.
  \item I can explain why a polynomial with real coefficients that has a non-real zero
        $a + bi$ must also have its conjugate $a - bi$, and multiply the pair into one
        quadratic factor with real coefficients.
  \item I can rewrite a polynomial from factored form into standard form, and say what each
        form shows: the zeros in one, the degree and leading term in the other.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
In Lesson 3.3 you started with a polynomial and read its zeros. Today runs the other way:
start with the zeros and \textbf{build} the polynomial. Every zero hands you a
\textbf{factor}; a repeated zero hands it to you more than once. The zeros still leave the
stretch free, so one more \textbf{point} pins down the leading coefficient. And a zero you
cannot see on the graph --- a non-real one --- never travels alone: with real coefficients,
its \textbf{conjugate} comes with it.
\end{remindbox}
