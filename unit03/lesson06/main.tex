    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will write a polynomial from its zeros --- one factor
    $(x - a)$ for each zero $a$, raised to its multiplicity, with the leading coefficient fixed
    by one more point given in words or read off a graph --- recognize that a polynomial with
    real coefficients and a non-real zero $a + bi$ must also have the zero $a - bi$, and rewrite
    the polynomial from factored form into standard form, saying what each form shows.
    \hfill\textit{\small (CED 1.5.A, 1.11.A)}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Skills} &
\textbf{Key Understandings} \\
\midrule
\textbf{AP Skill 2.B} --- Construct equivalent representations: write the polynomial a set of
zeros describes. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item A zero $a$ and a factor $(x - a)$ are the same fact (EK 1.5.A.1). The factor is $x$
        \emph{minus} the zero: the zero $-4$ gives $(x + 4)$, never $(x - 4)$.
  \item A zero of multiplicity $k$ contributes $(x - a)^k$; the degree is the sum of the
        multiplicities, and a degree-$n$ polynomial has exactly $n$ complex zeros
        (EK 1.5.A.2).
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 2.B} --- Read zeros, multiplicities, and a point off a graph and write the
equation. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Crossing the $x$-axis means odd multiplicity; touching it and turning back --- tangent
        to the axis --- means even multiplicity (EK 1.5.A.5).
  \item The zeros fix only a family $a(x - r_1)\cdots$; one more point fixes $a$. Forgetting
        $a$ when a point is given is the secondary trap.
\end{itemize} \\
\textbf{AP Skill 1.B} --- Express a polynomial in equivalent analytic forms: factored and
standard. &
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item Factored form shows the zeros; standard form shows the degree, the leading term, and
        so the end behavior. Each answers different questions (EK 1.11.A.1--3).
  \item With real coefficients, non-real zeros come in conjugate pairs (EK 1.5.A.4); multiply
        the pair first and the $i$'s cancel into one real quadratic factor.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception --- the forgotten conjugate:} asked for the least-degree
        polynomial with real coefficients and zeros $3$ and $1 + 2i$, students write
        $(x - 3)\bigl(x - (1 + 2i)\bigr)$ --- degree 2, non-real coefficients. The answer is
        $(x - 3)(x^2 - 2x + 5)$, degree 3. Notes problem 13; You Do problem 17.
\end{itemize} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\leavevmode\bfseries\color{plum}}p{0.24\linewidth} X @{}}
Zero and factor & $a$ is a zero of $p$ exactly when $(x - a)$ is a factor of $p$ (the Factor Theorem from 3.5, read backward). \\
Multiplicity    & The number of times a factor $(x - a)$ appears; the degree is the sum of the multiplicities. \\
Factored form   & $a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots$, possibly with an irreducible quadratic factor; it shows the zeros and the leading coefficient. \\
Standard form   & Terms in descending powers of $x$; it shows the degree, the leading term, and the end behavior. \\
Conjugate pair  & $a + bi$ and $a - bi$. A polynomial with real coefficients that has one as a zero has the other. \\
Conjugate pair theorem & \TallMath{\bigl(x - (a + bi)\bigr)\bigl(x - (a - bi)\bigr) = (x - a)^2 + b^2}, a quadratic factor with real coefficients. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review (5 min)]{lilac}
    Please see attached warm-up (spiral review).
    Skills reviewed: multiplying a binomial by a trinomial and collecting like terms (every
    standard form today), multiplying a conjugate pair $(2 + i)(2 - i)$ and using $i^2 = -1$
    (Unit 2 --- the move behind the CONJUGATE PAIRS row), the Factor Theorem from 3.5 (a zero
    $\Leftrightarrow$ a factor, read today in the other direction), and reading crossing versus
    touching --- odd versus even multiplicity --- off a graph (3.3).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook (3 min)]{lilac}
    \textbf{Run 3.3 backward.} On the board: \textit{I am thinking of a polynomial. Its only
    zeros are 1 and 4.}
    \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
        \item Ask for a polynomial that could be mine. Someone will offer $(x - 1)(x - 4)$; write
              it up and check one zero aloud.
        \item Ask whether it is the \emph{only} one. Collect $3(x - 1)(x - 4)$, $-(x - 1)(x - 4)$,
              and, if no one offers it, give $(x - 1)^2(x - 4)$ yourself.
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Discussion prompt:} the zeros told us the factors but not the stretch and not the
    multiplicities. Today we learn what extra information pins down exactly one polynomial ---
    and what happens when one of the zeros is not a real number at all.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson --- I Do / We Do / You Do]{lilac}
\raggedcolumns
\begin{multicols}{2}

\textbf{I DO --- Part 1: Zero to Factor to Standard Form} (10 min)

\textit{Notes rows BUILD IT and FIT THE POINT: read each definition, mark up the display, work
problems 1 and 5. Teacher works; students watch and annotate. Nothing is cold-called in this
phase.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the BUILD IT sentences, then fill the zero-to-factor table yourself, saying the
        check aloud each time: ``$-2 + 2 = 0$, so $(x + 2)$.'' The zero $0$ gives the bare
        factor $x$ --- name it, because students skip it.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 1} along the flow display: factors, then $(x + 2)(x - 1)$ first,
        then that trinomial times $(x - 3)$. Collect like terms in a column; that layout is
        the one students will copy.
  \item Read the FIT THE POINT sentences and annotate the formula display: the zeros fix
        everything but the gold $a$. Fill ``degree $= 4$.''
  \item Work \textbf{problem 5}: write $a(x - 2)^2(x + 1)(x - 3)$ \emph{with the $a$}, substitute
        $(0, 24)$, get $-12a = 24$, $a = -2$. Say that $a$ came out negative --- the point,
        not the zeros, decided that.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 2: Signs, the Zero 0, and the Missing $a$} (8 min)

\textit{Problems 2--4 and 6--8. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problems 2 and 3}: a quadratic, then a cubic with the zero $0$. Cold-call the
        factor for each zero before anyone multiplies.
  \item \textbf{Problem 4} is the sign trap made visible: test $x = -4$ in Jordan's product,
        get 24, and let the class say what went wrong.
  \item \textbf{Problems 6 and 7}: find $a$ first, then (in 6) the standard form. In
        \textbf{problem 8} Kai's answer has the right zeros and the wrong $y$-intercept: the
        forgotten $a$, caught by one substitution.
\end{itemize}

\columnbreak

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 3: Off a Graph} (5 min)

\textit{Notes row OFF A GRAPH. Class fills the blanks together; cold-call every one. Teacher
works problem 9; the class works 10.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the crossing/touching sentence, then cold-call the two label boxes: Graph A
        \textit{touches} at 1, Graph B \textit{crosses} at 0.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 9}: $a(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$, then the gold point $(0, -2)$ gives
        $a = -1$ --- and the right end falling agrees.
  \item \textbf{Problem 10} turns the $y$-intercept useless: $0$ is a zero, so every $a$ gives
        $(0, 0)$. The class must reach for $(1, 2)$ and gets $a = \tfrac12$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{WE DO --- Part 4: Conjugate Pairs} (9 min)

\textit{Notes row CONJUGATE PAIRS --- the hinge of the lesson. Class fills the blanks together;
cold-call every one. Teacher works problem 11; the class works 12--14.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Read the sentences: degree $n$ means $n$ complex zeros, but non-real ones never show on
        a graph. Walk the pre-printed display line by line and cold-call the label box,
        $i^2 = -1$. Give one minute for the \textit{in your own words} line.
  \item Work \textbf{problem 11}: say ``$2 + i$ brings $2 - i$'' \emph{before} writing a
        factor, use the display's $x^2 - 4x + 5$, and land on degree 3.
  \item \textbf{Problem 12}: $3i$ brings $-3i$, and $(x - 3i)(x + 3i) = x^2 + 9$.
  \item \textbf{Problem 13 is the crux}: Ana's degree-2 answer next to Ben's degree-3 one. Ask
        for each degree and which has real coefficients; have a student expand Ana's on the
        board until the $i$ is stuck in a coefficient. \textbf{Problem 14} checks Ben's 5 and
        finishes his standard form.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{YOU DO --- Part 5: Build Four} (15 min)

\textit{Notes row BUILD FOUR, problems 15--18 --- the whole independent phase. Students work
alone; circulate and say nothing a neighbor could say instead.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem 15} carries the sign trap (zero $-4$) into a full standard form;
        \textbf{16} the missing-$a$ trap with a double zero.
  \item \textbf{Problem 17} is the crux transferred: $3i$ must bring $-3i$, so the degree is 3,
        not 2.
  \item \textbf{Problem 18} is the finisher's question: a quartic with two real zeros and one
        non-real one, so the fourth zero has to be found before anything is multiplied.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the \textbf{You Do} phase --- the fifteen minutes where students are working
problems 15--18 without you --- and check. The crux is \textbf{problem 13} (Ana's
$(x - 3)\bigl(x - (1 + 2i)\bigr)$ against Ben's degree-3 answer); its You Do transfer is
\textbf{problem 17} (zeros $-2$ and $3i$), and problem 18 tests it again with the conjugate
to be found.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common error (the crux):} on problem 17, writing $(x + 2)(x - 3i)$ --- degree 2,
        with $i$ in the coefficients. Ask: ``Expand it. Is every coefficient real? What does
        the problem promise about the coefficients?'' Point at Ana in problem 13.
  \item \textbf{Common error:} the factor sign --- $(x - 4)$ for the zero $-4$ in problem 15.
        Ask the student to substitute the zero into the factor: ``Does it make it 0?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} dropping $a$ in problem 16, so $f(x) = (x - 1)^2(x + 3)$. Ask:
        ``What is your $f(0)$? What should it be?''
  \item \textbf{Common error:} in the expansions, losing a term when a trinomial meets a
        binomial. Have them multiply in a column, one term of the binomial per line.
  \item \textbf{Common error:} $(x - 3i)(x + 3i) = x^2 - 9$ from forgetting $i^2 = -1$. Ask:
        ``What is $-9i^2$?''
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Differentiation --- During You Do (15 min)]{redbox}
There is no separate activity sheet: students work the notes' You Do row (problems 15--18)
alone while you circulate. Differentiate by \textit{where you stand}, not by handing out
different paper.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Support.} Sit with a struggling student on \textbf{problem 15} and have them
        write a factor for each zero and check each one by substitution \emph{before}
        multiplying. Then multiply $(x + 4)(x - 1)$ together, and let them finish
        $(x^2 + 3x - 4)(x - 2)$ in a column.
  \item \textbf{On level.} \textbf{Problems 16 and 17} are the two traps. On 16, ask only
        ``where is your $a$?''; on 17, ask only ``how many zeros does it have?'' --- and walk
        away.
  \item \textbf{Extend.} \textbf{Problem 18} is the finisher's question: the fourth zero is not
        given, and the cleanest route multiplies $(x - 2)(x + 2) = x^2 - 4$ and the conjugate
        pair $x^2 - 2x + 2$ before multiplying the two quadratics. A finisher who has it can
        be asked what the standard form says about the end behavior --- both ends up, since
        the leading term is $x^4$.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (5 min)]{redbox}
Whole class, teacher-led, packets still open. Three moves, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Share out the You Do} (3 min) --- one answer per problem, not a full review.
        Problem 15: $x^3 + x^2 - 10x + 8$, with the factor $(x + 4)$ said aloud. Problem 16:
        $a = 2$. Problem 17: ``degree 3, because $3i$ brings $-3i$,'' and
        $x^3 + 2x^2 + 9x + 18$. Problem 18: the fourth zero $1 + i$, then the standard form if
        someone has it.
  \item \textbf{Name the headline} (1 min) --- say it, then make the class say it back:
        \textit{every zero gives a factor, a point gives the stretch, and with real
        coefficients a non-real zero never comes alone --- its conjugate comes with it.}
  \item \textbf{Point forward} (1 min) --- the DeltaMath assignment, \textit{Lesson 3.6}, and its
        due date: write it on the board and have students copy it into row 4 of the cover.
        Then: this was the last new lesson of Unit 3; the unit review and the unit test come
        next.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Do not} re-teach the expansion here or take new questions. If the share-out shows the
conjugate did not land, it belongs in the unit review, not in this minute.
\end{skillbox}

  \item \textbf{Multiply Polynomials (Level 2)} (3 problems) --- the standard-form expansions in

  \item \textbf{Polynomial Description/Table to Standard} (3 problems) --- matches notes problems

  \item \textbf{Select Equation from Graph Roots (L2)} (3 problems) --- matches the OFF A GRAPH
        row, problems 9--10.
  \item \textbf{Write Polynomial Function From Graph} (3 problems) --- matches notes problems 9--10.
  \item \textbf{Complex Conjugate Theorem} (4 problems) \textit{(diagnostic)} --- matches notes
        problems 11--14, 17, and 18.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{AP Practice --- extra credit, scored 1 to 5 (2 pages).} Optional, last in the packet.
Section I is five AP-style multiple-choice items (four options) whose distractors are the
lesson's errors: the factor sign flipped or the factor $x$ for the zero 0 dropped (1); the

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, silent start, no calculators. Item 1 is the exact multiplication every standard
form today runs on --- a student who loses a term there will lose it in problem 1 within the
quarter hour, so note names. Item 2 is the conjugate pair from Unit 2; the second blank
(``is the product real?'') is the whole reason it is here: it is the CONJUGATE PAIRS row in
one line. Item 3 reads the Factor Theorem forward ($p(2) = 0$, so $(x - 2)$ is a factor);
today runs it backward. Item 4 is 3.3's crossing/touching read; a student who calls $x = 2$
``crosses'' will miswrite the multiplicity in problems 9--10, so flag them before the notes.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The notes are one Main Ideas / Notes table, four instruction rows and a You Do row; in each
instruction row you read the printed sentences, mark up the display, and work the first
problem of the grid (1, 5, 9, 11), and the class works the rest with the pen in their hand,
every problem cold-called. BUILD IT lives on the check ``does the zero make the factor 0?'':
say it every time until the class says it for you, and let problem 4 catch the sign trap in
the open. FIT THE POINT is where the hook's question is answered --- the zeros leave the gold
$a$ free --- so point back at the hook when problem 5 comes out $a = -2$. Problem 8 is the
secondary trap stated as someone else's mistake; keep it, even when short of time, by cutting
problem 7 and reading 7's answer aloud.

CONJUGATE PAIRS is the spine. Walk the display before the problems: the factor
$\bigl(x - (2 + i)\bigr)$ alone would leave an $i$ in the coefficients; the pair cancels it.
Then problem 13 asks for a \emph{judgment}, not a computation: Ana's answer has the right
zeros, the wrong degree, and non-real coefficients. Do not accept ``she forgot one''; make the
class say which promise in the problem --- \emph{real coefficients} --- her answer breaks. Have
a student expand Ana's on the board, $x^2 - (4 + 2i)x + (3 + 6i)$, so the $i$ is visible.

The You Do row (15--18) is the entire independent phase and carries fifteen minutes. Problem
17 is the crux with Ana and Ben taken away; problem 18 hides the conjugate inside a quartic.
If the clock slips, cut problem 18 and let the Debrief report its fourth zero; AP Practice
6(c) asks for the same move.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Debrief]
Five minutes, and they are at risk --- the We Do runs 22 minutes and the expansions will try
to borrow them. Borrow from problem 18 instead, never from the You Do's first three problems:
18 is a finisher's question, and the Debrief can give its fourth zero to everyone in one
sentence. The point-forward minute is not optional --- it is the only time the DeltaMath due
date is said aloud, and the class needs to hear that the unit review comes next. The
share-out of problem 17 is the one to slow down for: ``degree 3, because $3i$ brings $-3i$''
is the exact shape of the diagnostic DeltaMath topic, which students will otherwise meet alone
at home.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[AP Practice]
Extra credit, optional, scored 1 to 5, and not a substitute for the DeltaMath assignment ---
say so when you hand out the packet. Its 1-to-5 score is added as extra-credit points.
Multiple-choice answers are \textbf{B, D, C, A, B}. Item 1: (A) and (D) flip the factor signs;
(C) drops the factor $x$ for the zero 0. Item 2: (A) and (C) negate the wrong part of $4 - i$;
(B) drops the imaginary part. \textbf{Item 3 is the one to glance at}: (B) 3 is the forgotten
conjugate --- exactly Ana in notes problem 13 --- while (A) 2 counts only the real zeros and
(D) 6 conjugates the real zeros too. Item 4: (B) flips the signs, (C) forgets $a$ (its
$y$-intercept would be 4, not $-8$), (D) swaps the multiplicities. Item 5: (A) $\tfrac12$
inverts $20 \div 10$; (C) $-2$ comes from the factors $(x - 1)(x + 2)(x + 5)$; (D) 20 takes the
$y$-intercept for $a$. In the free response, accept any table value other than $(1, 0)$ and
$(4, 0)$ for finding $a$ in (a), and any equivalent standard form in (b). Part (c) is the
deepest on the page: credit an answer that brings in $4 - i$ and says the remaining two zeros
are non-real, so $x = 1$ is the only place the track meets ground level.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
DeltaMath, graded, assigned as \textit{Lesson 3.6} and due on the date you set.
\textbf{Complex Conjugate Theorem} is the diagnostic: check its item-level results before the
unit review. A miss there almost always means the conjugate was forgotten --- the student
built a factor from the given non-real zero alone --- so the CONJUGATE PAIRS row did not land;
misses on \textbf{Find the Equation from Roots} that are sign errors (the zero $-4$ written as
$(x - 4)$) are the other trap, and a student missing both needs problem 15 and problem 17 sat
with again. DeltaMath cannot ask a student to judge which of two candidate polynomials has real
coefficients: that lives in notes problem 13 and You Do problem 17, so read those on the
packet when you collect it. For the unit test, \textbf{Multiply Polynomials (Level 2)} and
\textbf{Polynomial Description/Table to Standard} predict the factored-to-standard items, and
\textbf{Write Polynomial Function From Graph} predicts any item that pairs a graph with an
equation; reuse a missed one as a unit-review warm-up item rather than re-teaching it.
\end{teachernote}
